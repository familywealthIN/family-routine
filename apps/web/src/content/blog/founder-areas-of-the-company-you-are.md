A solo founder can run life as five departments of the company they already are, each with its own head, budget and year goal. It solves a specific problem: on a single to-do list, product work wins every argument because it's always the most urgent thing on the page.

Routine Notes supports this directly through hierarchical tags. Here's the org chart.

## Who sits on the board?

Every routine gets an `area:` tag, and sub-areas use a second colon, so `area:ceo:body` sits under `area:ceo`.

| Time | Routine | Area tag | Points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05:50 | Wake up and stretch | `area:ceo:body` | 5 |
| 06:20 | Run | `area:ceo:body` | 10 |
| 07:00 | Meditation | `area:ceo:mind` | 5 |
| 07:30 | Plan the day | `area:coo:plan` | 10 |
| 08:00 | Build the product | `area:cto` | 35 |
| 13:30 | Write and publish | `area:cmo` | 10 |
| 15:30 | Customer conversations | `area:cmo` | 10 |
| 19:00 | Family dinner | `area:cxo` | 10 |
| 22:00 | Night review | `area:coo:review` | 5 |

The CEO looks after body and mind. The CTO owns the product. The CMO writes and talks to customers. The COO plans and reviews. The CXO is family. Add it up and the CTO has 35 points, the CEO 20, the CMO 20, the COO 15 and the CXO 10, which makes 100. Writing the split down is the point: product work is the biggest department, but it doesn't get the whole budget. Tags and points both live in each routine's editor under More → Routines.

## One year goal per department

Each area gets one year goal, and only one:

- **CEO:** a half marathon under two hours.
- **CTO:** version 1.0 shipped to the public.
- **CMO:** a post published every week.
- **COO:** a weekly review held every Sunday.
- **CXO:** at the table for dinner five nights a week.

Each department's year goal breaks down the same way. For the CTO, this month's milestone is finishing onboarding and settings, this week's is getting the onboarding flow through testing, and today's day item, "Write the empty-state copy", is linked to Build the product. Ticking items that sit inside a cascade earns Geniuses, and the higher the item, the more it's worth.

The rules are the same for every department. A week closes at five met days, a month at three met weeks, and a year at six met months. If the CMO plans seven posts in a week, that's ambition. The week still closes at five.

## The brief is the department's memo

When a tagged routine starts, the routine chat pins "Before you start" at the top of the thread. It has one block per `area:` or `project:` tag: the standing commitment, up to three next steps (each with an Add button) and up to three rows of past activity.

![Home with the week strip, the focused routine card and its checklist, and the routine chat beside it](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-day.jpg)

The brief sits first in the thread next to the focused card ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) shows where). At 13:30, the CMO's memo is waiting: what you said you'd write, the last three things you wrote, and what's next. Tap "Plan from next steps" and the chat answers with a plan built from them. Each Add button puts that step on today's checklist. Switching between departments is most of a founder's day, and the memo does the handover.

## What goes wrong when one department eats the others?

A launch week arrives. Build the product runs until 21:00, and Family dinner, at 19:00, goes unticked. The tempting fix is to check dinner in late with points. Only do that if dinner actually happened, just after its window closed at 19:30. A late check-in is for something you did late. It isn't a way to cover up something that didn't happen.

So let the miss stand. Over a week, More → Progress → On the clock, broken down by routine, shows which department is being squeezed. That's the honest version of the org chart.

## The board meeting

On the first Sunday of each month, the COO's Night review becomes the board meeting. Open Goals, look at each department's month milestone and re-plan anything slipping. You can edit a milestone, move a day item, or shift five points from CTO to CXO if family needs the weight. Re-planning isn't failure. Improving the plan is what Geniuses rewards.

## Setting it up

Write down five departments that match your life. You don't have to use corporate titles. Tag every routine with one, and give each department a single year goal. The first month will show you which department has been underfunded.
