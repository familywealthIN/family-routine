A café owner can turn opening and closing into checklists, starting with the lights on at 05:30. A small café runs on two lists that usually live in the owner's head: everything that has to happen before the door opens at 07:00, and everything after it shuts at 15:30. Both are long, both get done half-asleep or worn out, and both fail the same way: one forgotten step that shows up at 10:00 when the oat milk runs out.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05:00 | Wake up | 5 | Clothes laid out the night before |
| 05:30 | Open the café | 30 | Lights and machine on |
| 10:30 | Stock and orders | 10 | Check the walk-in fridge |
| 14:00 | Quiet-hour project | 15 | Laptop at the corner table |
| 15:30 | Close down | 25 | Flip the sign |
| 17:30 | Walk | 10 | Shoes on |
| 21:00 | Night routine | 5 | Write tomorrow's prep list |

Opening and closing take 55 of the day's 100 points between them.

## The opening checklist

Open the café gets six steps, in the order they're done:

1. Lights and machine on
2. Grinder dialled in with a test shot
3. Delivery checked in and pastries in the case
4. Tables down, chairs out
5. Milk fridge stocked from the walk-in
6. Sign turned to Open at 07:00

![Home on a phone with the week strip, the routine in progress and its checklist with one item ticked](/screenshots/home-mobile.png)

On the phone propped by the till, the checklist ticks down as the morning goes ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) shows the card). Step 1 is the cue, and the 05:30 reminder opens with it. Nobody needs willpower to flip a switch, and once you've done that, the checklist carries you through the rest. Working the steps is where Kinetics comes from, which is why the order matters: the grinder is dialled in before the first customer, not after the first complaint.

Opening can be ticked on time from 04:30 to 06:00. Most mornings it's ticked at about 05:35, as the lights come on. That's the easiest bit of Discipline in the day.

## The close-down checklist

Closing is when tired people skip steps, so it gets 25 points and its own six-step list: flip the sign, back-flush the machine, write down the fridge temperatures, floors and bins, confirm tomorrow's delivery, alarm set and door locked. It can be ticked on time from 14:30 to 16:00. On a day with a late rush it gets done at 16:30, which is what late check-ins are for.

## Who owns which job?

Not every step is the owner's. When the morning barista should own the milk order, write it as a quick task with an @mention: "@lead-barista order oat milk for Thursday". The @mention moves it into Delegate in **Priority**, where you can still see it but it isn't cluttering the owner's Do now column. If you date it for a future day it goes to Plan instead, because Delegate only covers today's work.

Use **+** during Stock and orders for anything that comes up in service, such as "Call the bakery about Saturday's order before 11am". It lands in the 10:30 slot with the time already set.

## The quiet hour has a year goal

Between the lunch rush and closing, the café nearly runs itself. That hour is the 15-point Quiet-hour project, and it serves the year goal of launching a weekend brunch menu. This month's milestone is three dishes tested with regulars. The week milestone is one dish idea tested each day the café is open, with day items like "Test the savoury waffle" linked to Quiet-hour project. Each ticked test earns Geniuses.

The café opens six days a week, and a week needs five met days, so there's one day of slack. The month needs three good weeks, and the brunch year needs six good months.

## When the day slips

A late rush pushes the close-down to 16:40, after its window closed at 16:00. Every step happened, just late, so spend the routine's 25 points from **Past** that afternoon to check it in. The streak holds and it counts as a full tick. Yesterday's earnings settled overnight, so there's usually enough to cover it.

Closed for a refit, or taking a day off while someone else runs the café? Long-press today in the week strip and take a Skip Day. The day counts as rest, the streak survives, and you get two a week.
