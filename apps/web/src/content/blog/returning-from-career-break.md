Going back to work after a career break goes best with a three-month re-entry plan: relearn the tools, own one piece of work, then lead something. The skills come back faster than most people expect. What takes longer is fitting a working day around a family that has spent years arranging itself around you being at home.

Here's how that plan looks for an illustrative parent returning to an operations role after four years away, set out as the questions people usually ask.

## Q: What does the day look like?

The day is a chain of routines from breakfast to bedtime, and work gets the biggest block:

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Wake up | 5 | Coffee machine on |
| 07:00 | Breakfast & school prep | 15 | Lunchboxes out of the fridge |
| 08:20 | School drop-off | 5 | Shoes and bags by the door |
| 09:00 | Work block | 35 | Read yesterday's notes |
| 13:00 | Lunch & catch-up reading | 10 | Open one industry article |
| 15:30 | Pick-up | 5 | Car keys from the hook |
| 18:00 | Family dinner | 15 | Phones in the basket |
| 21:30 | Plan tomorrow | 10 | Open tomorrow's list |

That's 100. The work block takes 35 because it has the most to rebuild. Family dinner takes 15 because in the first month it's the routine most likely to be sacrificed, and it shouldn't be. To change a routine's points later, open **More → Routines** and tap it. The editor shows how much of the day's budget is left.

## Q: Why a three-month plan and not a year?

Have a year goal anyway: "Be back at my old level by December". But in week one a year is hard to picture. Three month milestones under it make the next ninety days concrete:

1. **Month 1: Relearn the tools.** The systems have changed. Week milestones: "Shadow two colleagues", "Rebuild my reporting templates", "A week without asking where anything lives".
2. **Month 2: Own one piece of work.** Something small with your name on it from start to finish.
3. **Month 3: Lead something.** A meeting, a process, a fix that other people now follow.

Each week milestone gets day items linked to the Work block, such as "Rebuild the weekly ops template", so they show up on that card at 09:00. The card's Week and Month tabs show what today's item belongs to.

The year needs six good months, so these three are its first half. A month is done after three good weeks, and a week after five good days.

## Q: Where do K, D and G come in?

- **Discipline:** routines ticked inside their windows. For the 08:20 drop-off that's 07:20 to 08:50. In the first month the school-run chain matters more than anything at work: if the mornings hold, the day holds.
- **Kinetics:** checklist items and quick tasks done inside a routine. Anything that comes up at work goes in through the **+** beside the chat box. Type "Send the vendor list to the supplier team by Thursday" and the date fills itself in.
- **Geniuses:** ticking items linked into the re-entry plan. It also covers changing that plan. If month 2's "piece of work" changes once you know the team, edit the milestone.

By week three, open Priority before lunch and look at the counts. They're a fast read on whether the day is under control ([the Priority chapter](/guide/priority) explains how items sort themselves). A tall Do column at 10:00 says the morning was planned by yesterday's leftovers.

## Q: What about the days that fall apart?

They will. A child home ill, a strike day at school, a late meeting that eats into pick-up. Two tools, each for a different kind of day.

If the whole day has been rearranged, long-press today in the week strip and pick **Skip Day**. It counts as rest, not as a gap, and the streak stays. You get two a week.

If one routine just ran late, say the work block starting at 10:15 after a slow drop-off, it sits in **Past** once 09:30 has gone. Check it in from there before the day ends, using points. The run of days holds and the tick earns its points like any other. You start with 300 welcome points after onboarding, and each day's points are added overnight.

Goals are forgiving too. A day item you finished but forgot to tick can be ticked later and still counts toward its week, though it will show as **Missed**.

## Q: What's the first step?

The week before you go back, set up the eight routines and run the morning chain while you're still at home. Then add month 1 and its first week milestone. Under **More → Notifications**, turn on End of day progress for 21:00: it shows how the day went in time to plan tomorrow, and it makes a good close to that first week back.
