The four Priority counts say what kind of day you're having, because nobody sorts the matrix by hand. Every item files itself by how you created it, so Do now, Plan, Delegate and Automate are a reading of your day, not your intentions.

Most priority matrices fail because you have to sort them and you stop doing it by Wednesday. In Routine Notes the rule is fixed:

- **Started today** goes to **Do now**.
- **Scheduled for a future day** goes to **Plan**, whoever it's for and however it starts.
- **@mentions a person** goes to **Delegate**.
- **Started with an agent** goes to **Automate**.

## The day behind the counts

Here's the routine set for a team lead with a heavy work block:

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Wake up | 5 | Fill a glass of water |
| 07:00 | Walk | 10 | Shoes on, keys in pocket |
| 08:00 | Plan the day | 10 | Notebook open at the table |
| 08:30 | Deep work | 40 | Close every tab but one |
| 13:30 | Team & admin | 15 | Open the team thread |
| 18:00 | Training | 10 | Bag by the door |
| 21:00 | Family time | 5 | Clear the table |
| 22:30 | Wind-down | 5 | Phone on charge outside the bedroom |

Tasks arrive all day, from the **+** on the current routine, from the routine chat, and from the Inbox. Each one files itself the moment it's created.

The counts sit above the quadrants ([the Priority chapter](/guide/priority) explains each one). Here's how to read them.

## What do the four numbers mean?

Read them as Do · Plan · Delegate · Automate.

**2 · 3 · 1 · 2: balanced.** Two things in hand, the future parked, one handoff, two automations. Nothing to fix.

**7 · 0 · 0 · 1 at 09:00: fire-fighting.** Seven items started before mid-morning means yesterday decided nothing and today is deciding everything at once. Keep **Do now** at 3 or fewer. Open the other 4 and move their dates to tomorrow or later. Each one re-files into **Plan** on the next refresh. A tall Do-now column before 09:00 is a warning, not a badge.

**1 · 11 · 0 · 0: planning instead of doing.** Eleven items parked for later and one in hand. On a Sunday that's fine. On a Tuesday it usually means every task is waiting for a better day. Bring one Plan item to today and start it, and it moves to Do now.

**3 · 2 · 5 · 0: delegating without following up.** Five @mentions are five promises other people have made to you, and Delegate is the quadrant that rots quietly. Give each item a date and open **Priority → Delegate** during the 13:30 *Team & admin* slot.

**4 · 2 · 0 · 0, every day: nothing automated.** If the same chore shows up in Do now twice a week, such as pulling overnight requests, it belongs to an agent bound to that routine. Start it with the agent and it lives in **Automate** from then on, listed back to you as part of your machinery.

## Where do the points come from?

The matrix earns nothing on its own. It orders the work, and the work earns:

- **Kinetics** from ticking items and steps inside the routine slot. You can tick items complete straight from a quadrant without leaving the page.
- **Discipline** from ticking each routine in its window: for *Team & admin*, 12:30 to 14:00.
- **Geniuses** from Plan. Items parked there are next week's milestone material. Link them to a week goal when you write it, and ticking them later moves the week, the month and the year.

## What if the day slips anyway?

Say a meeting runs over and *Team & admin* is still unticked at 14:00. The routine moves to **Past**, and before the day ends you can spend its 15 points to check it in late. The streak holds, its agents run, and the check-in earns points like any tick. The Do-now items you didn't reach aren't lost either. Move their dates and they re-file into Plan. One you did and forgot to tick? Tick it now. Late ticks count toward goals, even though the status reads *Missed*.

## A twice-a-day habit

Read the counts at the start of Deep work, 08:30, and again after lunch. Morning tells you whether today was planned. Afternoon tells you whether the plan survived. A week of readings shows more about how you work than any amount of sorting by hand.
