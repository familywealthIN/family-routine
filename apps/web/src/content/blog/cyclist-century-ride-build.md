Building to a 100-mile ride comes down to one long Saturday ride each week, a little longer each month. The short spins, the strength work and the rest days all exist so the long ride can grow without breaking you. Plan the year around that weekly anchor and the rest of the week falls into place.

Here's how a cyclist with a desk job and a June sportive might set it up, working backwards from the date.

## Where does the plan start?

At the finish. Write the year goal with a deadline, "Ride 100 miles at the June sportive", then go back one month at a time:

- **June:** Taper and ride the event
- **May:** Long ride reaches 85 miles
- **April:** Long ride reaches 70 miles
- **March:** Long ride reaches 55 miles
- **February:** Long ride reaches 40 miles, with two midweek rides
- **January:** Back on the bike four days a week

That's six milestones, and six met months close the year, so this plan has no slack at all. Add a July milestone for after the event ("Recover, then ride with the club") and the year gets one spare month. Without it, a bad March would mean the year goal can't close.

## Why one ride routine for every day?

Routines repeat daily, so a Saturday-only routine would sit unticked from Monday to Friday. Keep one Ride routine at a fixed time and let the day items say what kind of ride today is.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 (the cue) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05:50 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 06:20 | Ride | 30 | Pump the tyres |
| 07:40 | Breakfast | 10 | Kettle on |
| 08:30 | Work | 35 | Open today's list |
| 18:30 | Strength & stretch | 10 | Mat unrolled |
| 21:00 | Bike check & plan | 5 | Bike on the stand |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 5 | Fill the bottles for the morning |

The ride carries the goal, so it gets 30 of the 100 points. That also makes it the routine that costs most to rescue, which puts the pressure in the right place. Its window runs from 05:20 to 06:50. On a Saturday you can roll out at 06:40, be home after lunch, and the start still counts as on time.

## What does a build week look like?

Each week has one milestone, for example "Long ride 50 miles, plus three short rides", and five dated items linked to the Ride:

- Tuesday: "Spin, 45 minutes, steady"
- Wednesday: "Rest: stretch, 15 minutes"
- Thursday: "Spin, 60 minutes with hills"
- Friday: "Easy spin, 30 minutes"
- Saturday: "Long ride, 50 miles"

The Wednesday stretch isn't padding. A build week only closes after five met dates, and four rides on their own can't get there. Recording the rest day lets the week close and makes rest part of the plan.

Working the checklist (tyres, kit, the ride, a spin-down, the distance logged) earns Kinetics. The on-time roll-out earns Discipline. Every ticked item sits under a week, a month and the June goal, and that's where Geniuses comes from. The longer the chain above an item, the more it counts.

## When the long ride falls through

Storms, a family lunch or a puncture two miles out. What happens next depends on what you can still do that weekend:

- **You rode at 14:00 instead.** The Ride routine moved to **Past** on Home at 06:50. Tick it there before the day ends for 30 points, and the streak holds. The long-ride item is marked Missed because of the timing, but it still counts as Saturday's met day.
- **Sunday is free.** Ask the routine chat to defer the long-ride item by a day. Saturday then counts once its short spin is ticked.
- **The weekend is gone.** Long-press today in the week strip for a Skip Day. You get two a week, and a weekend fits exactly.

Losing a whole weekend usually means changing the month, not the year. Push April's distance back a week, or accept that this month's longest ride will be 65 miles. That's Geniuses work too.

## What should Sunday's check look like?

Five minutes on **Goals**. The month calendar shows each day's goal count, so two empty days in a week stand out. Then open the year goal on **Goals → Year Goals** and count how many months are met and how many are left before June.
