Dividing 100 points between your routines tells the app what your day is for, so weight them by priority, not by how long they take. When you add a routine, the editor asks what it's worth, and it's tempting to accept the default and move on. Don't. Every routine draws on the same 100-point day budget, so the split is a statement of priorities.

## What does a routine's points number do?

It sets three things:

- **What the routine can earn.** An on-time tick earns Discipline in proportion to its points. Working the checklist earns Kinetics on the same scale. Ticking linked goal items earns Geniuses as a share of those points, and the share grows with the horizon: a year-level link counts for more than a day-level one. A 30-point routine can earn three times what a 10-point one can.
- **What a late rescue costs.** Miss the window and the routine moves to **Past** on Home. You can still check it in there today, and the price is its points. The routines that matter most cost the most to miss, which is the point.
- **What's left for the rest.** Each routine can take between 1 and 50 points. Open **More → Routines** and the editor shows how much of the 100 is still free as you change things.

Each pillar caps at 100 a day, 300 in all. Today's earnings settle overnight and can be spent tomorrow.

## Three rules for the split

**1. Start everything at 10, then move points.** Add every routine at ten, then take from the ones that are easy to keep and give to the ones that carry your goals. Moving points around is quicker than pricing each routine from scratch.

**2. Weight by consequence, not duration.** A 15-minute planning slot might deserve more than an hour-long commute if the day falls apart without it. Ask what happens to everything after a routine when it doesn't happen.

**3. Keep small routines cheap.** Wake-up, a lunch walk and a night routine at 5 points each are cheap to keep and cheap to rescue. Save the big numbers for the work that moves a year goal.

## A worked weekday

| Time | Routine | Points | Why this weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Wake up | 5 | Cheap to keep, starts the chain |
| 07:00 | Exercise | 15 | Feeds a year goal |
| 08:00 | Plan the day | 5 | Short, but the day hangs on it |
| 08:30 | Main work | 35 | Most of the day and most of the goals |
| 13:00 | Lunch walk | 5 | Small and easy |
| 18:00 | Learning | 15 | Feeds a year goal |
| 20:00 | Family | 10 | Protected time |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 10 | Sets up tomorrow |

That adds up to 100. The two routines that feed year goals get 15 each, the main work gets 35, and everything else stays small. Missing the 18:00 Learning slot and rescuing it at 21:00 costs 15. Missing the 13:00 walk costs 5.

## Three other splits

- **A student in exam season:** revision 40, sleep routine 15, exercise 15, wake-up 10, meals 10, a social slot 10. Revision is the one to protect, so it's the costly one to miss.
- **A new parent:** morning reset 20, a walk with the pram 20, the evening handover 20, a short "something for me" slot 20, night routine 20. Five equal weights say every routine counts and none is huge, which is honest when any of them might not happen.
- **A shift worker:** pre-shift 30, post-shift wind-down 30, sleep anchor 20, exercise 10, admin 10. The transitions are the hard part, so they carry the weight.

## When the split goes wrong

The numbers aren't fixed. Change them when:

- **You keep paying to rescue the same routine.** It might be worth too much for a slot at a bad time. Lower its points, or better still, move it.
- **A routine is always ticked but feeds nothing.** It might be worth too little to matter, or too much for something that's already automatic. Take five points from it and give them to a routine that's struggling.
- **The season changes.** Exams, an off-season, a new job: re-weight the whole list. It takes five minutes in the editor, and it counts as improving the plan, which is Geniuses work.

Look at the three biggest numbers on your list. If they aren't the three things you most want from this year, move points until they are.
