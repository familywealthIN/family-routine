Retired life leaves nobody scheduling your week, so designing one from scratch starts with a handful of fixed points rather than a full diary. In Routine Notes those fixed points are routines, and each one has a start time and a window that tells you whether you turned up when you said you would.

## Why does an empty week fall apart?

For forty years a train, a shift or a first meeting decided when the day began. The first month of retirement feels like a holiday. Around the third, Tuesday and Saturday start to look the same, and the things promised for retirement (the reading, the workshop, the long walks) drift to "tomorrow", because tomorrow is always free. Filling the diary doesn't fix that. A few anchors do, with everything else arranging itself around them.

## Eight anchors, one table

This retiree's week runs on eight routines:

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Morning walk | 15 | Shoes on at the front door |
| 07:30 | Breakfast & news | 5 | Kettle on |
| 09:00 | Project hour | 20 | Open the shed door |
| 11:00 | Reading | 15 | Book and glasses on the chair |
| 14:00 | Course work | 15 | Open the lesson page |
| 16:00 | Call or visit someone | 10 | Pick one name |
| 18:00 | Cook dinner | 10 | Read tonight's recipe |
| 21:30 | Wind-down | 10 | Screens off in the sitting room |

The eight add up to 100, the whole daily budget. The project hour has the largest share because, with no deadline behind it, it's the first thing to disappear. Breakfast gets 5 because it happens anyway and only needs to mark the morning.

Each window opens an hour before the start time and shuts half an hour after. So the 09:00 project hour counts as on time anywhere from 08:00 to 09:30. That's loose enough for a life without alarms, but it gives the day a clock again, the one that used to come with the job. Ticking inside it earns **Discipline**.

## Steps hold the detail

Every routine has an ordered checklist, and its first item is a small physical action. "Shoes on at the front door" is easier to agree to than "go for a walk", and the routine's reminder opens with it. The later items carry the plan: which loop to walk, the three shed jobs for today, the chapter to reach. Each item ticked off earns **Kinetics**, which marks the difference between standing at the workbench and making something there.

## A year of reading, cut into months

Reading at 11:00 every day is good. Reading towards something is better. The year goal is "Read the twelve classics I never got to":

- **Month milestones:** one book each, so "March: one classic, start to finish".
- **Week milestone:** "Read on five days this week".
- **Day items:** "Chapters 4–6", "Chapters 7–9", each linked to the Reading routine so it appears on the card at 11:00.

![The focused routine card on Home switched to the Week tab, showing the week goal and the day goals linked to it](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-cascade.jpg)

Switch the card to its Week tab and the five reading days line up under the week goal ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) walks through the tabs). A week closes once five of its days are met. A month closes after three good weeks. The year closes after six good months, even though twelve books were planned. Ticks on cascade items earn **Geniuses**, and the year-level ones count for the most.

## What happens when the walk slips?

At 06:30 it's pouring, so the walk happens at 10:00, by which time its window closed at 07:00. The routine sits in **Past** on Home. Since the walk really happened, tick it from Past before midnight and pay with points. The run of days survives, and that late tick earns the same points an on-time one would.

A weekend with the grandchildren is a different case. Long-press today in the week strip and choose **Skip Day**, which records the day as rest and leaves the streak alone. There are two a week. An honest skip is better than ticking a walk you didn't take.

Reading items are forgiving too. Finish chapters 7–9 on Wednesday, forget to tick them, tick them on Thursday, and they still count toward the week. They'll show **Missed**, because that label is about timing, but the week still moves.

## Reading the week back

Retirement removes the usual feedback: nobody reviews your month. Open **More → Progress** once a week. The *On the clock* view shows which routines start on time and which drift. If 14:00 course work slides every afternoon, move it to 10:00 while your energy is still high.

Under **More → Notifications**, switch on two things: Good morning at 07:00, for the day at a glance, and End of day progress at 20:00, for what's still possible tonight. Leave everything else off until you find you want it.

## The first step

Write down the three things retirement was supposed to make room for. Give each one a routine with a fixed time and a first step you can do with your hands, and put the remaining points on the meals and the walk. After that, the windows do the commute's old job: they tell you it's time.
