A marathon taper means doing less on purpose for three weeks without letting a single one of those weeks break. After months of building, the plan suddenly asks for less, and for many runners less feels like losing fitness. Some quietly add miles back. Others read the taper as permission to stop.

The app won't calm your nerves, but it can show the taper for what it is: three planned weeks, each with its own job.

## The routines don't change

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 (the cue) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05:30 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 06:00 | Run | 25 | Shoes on at the door |
| 07:10 | Stretch & shower | 5 | Mat out |
| 08:30 | Work | 35 | Open today's list |
| 12:30 | Lunch walk | 5 | Stand up from the desk |
| 18:30 | Mobility | 10 | Mat unrolled |
| 21:00 | Kit & plan | 10 | Lay out tomorrow's kit |
| 22:00 | Sleep routine | 5 | Phone on charge in the hall |

The run keeps its 25 points all the way through, and that's deliberate. The taper is still training, and the routine that carries it shouldn't lose its weight because the sessions get shorter. Its window runs from 05:00 to 06:30. Starting a 25-minute shakeout at 06:00 is the same Discipline as starting a 20-mile long run at 06:00.

What changes is what's in the day items, under one month milestone, "Taper and race", and a year goal, "Run a spring marathon".

## Three weeks out: cut the volume, keep the shape

**Week milestone:** "Taper week 1: about three-quarters of peak volume"

The long run gets shorter. The midweek runs stay, and the easy days stay easy. Five dated items, all linked to the Run:

- Tuesday: "Steady, 50 minutes, a few strides"
- Wednesday: "Easy, 30 minutes"
- Thursday: "Marathon-pace segment, 40 minutes total"
- Friday: "Rest: mobility, 15 minutes"
- Sunday: "Long run, about two-thirds of the peak"

## Why can a shorter week still close?

**Week milestone:** "Taper week 2: about half of peak volume"

This is the week where the rule earns its keep. The week goal counts met dates: once five have every item filed under this week ticked, it closes. Mileage doesn't come into it. A 30-minute easy run ticked on Wednesday fills its date exactly as well as a 90-minute run did in March.

![Goals switched to the week, listing this week's goals by routine with their progress](/screenshots/guide/goals-week.jpg)

So taper week two still has five items, all of them short. If the record shows a broken week, the plan was written badly. If it shows five met days, the taper did its job ([the Goals chapter](/guide/goals)).

## Race week: shakeouts and kit

**Week milestone:** "Race week: rest, shakeouts, arrive fresh"

The items get smaller again: "Shakeout, 20 minutes", "Rest: walk only", "Kit check". Lay out tomorrow's kit at 21:00, the night routine's step 1, and that becomes the most important step of the week. Give the race-day item sub-tasks (bib pinned, shoes chosen, start-area plan) and tick them the night before.

## Race day: a late start, not a missed one

Most marathons start mid-morning, long after the run's window has closed. You don't need to re-time anything for one day:

- Add the race as a day item, "Race", linked to the Run and to the race-week milestone.
- At 06:30, while you're in the queue for the toilets, the Run moves to **Past** on Home.
- After the finish, open Past and check the Run in for its 25 points. The streak holds.
- Tick "Race" as well. It's marked Missed because of the time, but late ticks still count towards the goal.

Make sure your balance covers it. Points settle overnight, so the shakeout days in race week fund the check-in on Sunday.

## After the race

The three taper weeks are enough to meet the month milestone on their own. The year goal closes once six of its month milestones are met, so if the build-up months were all met, race month may be the one that closes it. You can check on **Goals → Year Goals**.

Then write next month's milestone, "Recover: easy running only", with five short items a week. Re-planning after a big event is Geniuses work, and it stops the post-marathon weeks turning into a blank stretch of the calendar.
