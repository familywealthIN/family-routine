Reading Progress and On the clock takes ten minutes once a week, and that weekly review tells you more than fifty glances spread through the days. People who like data check too often: they open the app after every tick to watch a ring fill, and treat Tuesday as a trend. Here's the review, in order.

## What exactly are you measuring?

The week under review comes from this routine set:

| Time | Routine | Points | On time this week (example) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05:50 | Wake up | 5 | 2 of 7 |
| 06:20 | Exercise | 15 | 5 of 7 |
| 07:30 | Breakfast & get ready | 5 | 6 of 7 |
| 08:00 | Plan the day | 5 | 3 of 7 |
| 08:30 | Work | 40 | 6 of 7 |
| 18:30 | Dinner | 10 | 6 of 7 |
| 19:30 | Study | 15 | 5 of 7 |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 5 | 4 of 7 |

The last column is what *On the clock* gives you. These numbers are illustrative, but the shape is common: the long routines in the middle of the day hold, and the short ones at the edges slip.

## Step 1: which pillar is carrying you?

Open More → Progress and set the switch to Week. D, K and G come first. Read them as a balance, not a score.

- **D high, K low:** you're turning up and then not doing the work. The steps may be labels rather than actions, or the routines may be longer than what happens in them.
- **K high, D low:** the work happens, but not on time. Step 3 shows where.
- **G low while D and K look healthy:** the effort isn't connected to anything. Day items aren't linked to week milestones, so nothing climbs the cascade.

Each pillar caps at 100 a day, so a week's totals show which axis is carrying you and which is coasting.

## Step 2: Completed, then Needs attention

**Completed** is the good news and quick to read. **Needs attention** is the useful part, because it shows what's falling behind. Don't fix anything yet. Write down the two items that matter most for next week.

Keep the closing rules in mind as you read it. A week closes at 5 met days, a month at 3 met weeks, a year at 6 met months. Late ticks count. An item ticked after its window reads Missed but still moves its goal, so a week full of Missed items with progress against them isn't failing. It's late.

## Step 3: which routines run late?

*On the clock* is the Discipline view: on-time against late check-ins, routine by routine. On time means inside the 1h 30m window, from an hour before the start to half an hour after. A late check-in, bought with points from **Past** on Home, keeps the streak and still counts, but On the clock records it as late. That's the honest part.

Read it for patterns, not single days:

- **One routine late every day.** Wake up, at 2 of 7, has the wrong time. Move it to when you actually start, switch its reminder to Alarm, or make the first step the tick itself.
- **A short routine squeezed between two long ones.** Plan the day, at 3 of 7, gets crushed between breakfast and work. Merge it into a neighbour, or anchor it to something physical, such as opening the list before you leave the kitchen.
- **Evenings drifting later through the week.** A late night routine on Thursday and Friday usually means the day ran long, not that the night routine is broken.

## Step 4: make one change

Pick one:

- re-time a routine,
- rewrite a step,
- move points around within the 100-point budget,
- edit a milestone.

All four are Geniuses work, improving the plan. One change a week compounds. Five at once means next Sunday you can't tell which one helped. That's the loop of the review: turn up, do the work, read it back, adjust.

## Step 5: points, last

Points settle overnight, so the balance covers everything up to yesterday. If late check-ins have been eating it, step 3 has already named the routine responsible. Spending points on a late check-in is fine. Spending them on the same routine every day is a schedule problem.

## Where does the review go?

Routines repeat daily, so the weekly review belongs with the goals. Add a Sunday day item, "Weekly review: Progress set to Week", linked to an evening routine you already have. It shows on that routine's card when Sunday comes.
