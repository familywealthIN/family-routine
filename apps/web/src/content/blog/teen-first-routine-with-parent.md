A teenager's first routine list works when the teenager sets it up and the parent keeps a light touch. One thirty-minute session at the kitchen table is enough, with the teen holding the phone and the parent mostly asking questions. If a parent writes the list instead, it's just a chore chart in a new font, and it's abandoned by Wednesday.

## Ground rules before you open the app

- **The teen owns the account.** The routines, points and goals are theirs.
- **The parent asks and doesn't fill anything in.** "What time do you actually leave?" is fine. "You should be up at 06:30" isn't.
- **The record isn't evidence.** A late or skipped routine is information for the teen, not a topic for dinner.
- **Sharing the week is opt-in.** If both of you want it later, one of you can invite the other from **More → Groups**, and you'll each see the other's week. Decide that together. It's not a condition.

## Minutes 0–10: which routines?

Start with the school day as it is now, not the one either of you would like. Most teenagers end up with six or seven routines:

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 07:00 | Up and ready | 15 | Feet on the floor, curtains open |
| 07:40 | Breakfast | 10 | Bowl on the table |
| 08:10 | Leave for school | 10 | Bag packed the night before |
| 16:30 | Homework | 30 | Phone in the kitchen |
| 18:30 | Practice | 15 | Guitar out of its case |
| 21:00 | Pack and plan | 10 | Bag by the door |
| 22:00 | Screens off | 10 | Phone on charge downstairs |

The points add up to 100, and the teen decides how they're split. In this example homework gets 30 because it's the routine most likely to drift. Practice is whatever they've chosen, here the guitar.

## Minutes 10–20: the first steps

New routines come with suggested steps, and they're worth editing together. The one that matters most is step 1: a small physical action that starts the routine without a decision. "Phone in the kitchen" works for homework because once the phone is in another room, starting is the easy option.

Keep each list to three or four steps. For homework: phone in the kitchen, open the planner, do the hardest subject first, pack the finished work.

## Minutes 20–30: how scoring works

Give the three scores a sentence each, then stop.

- **Discipline** rewards starting on time. Homework at 16:30 counts as on time from 15:30 to 17:00.
- **Kinetics** rewards working through the checklist and any quick tasks added with the **+** beside the chat box, such as "Revise French vocab tomorrow at 5pm".
- **Geniuses** rewards linking days to bigger goals. That can wait.

## What does slipping look like in the first month?

Some afternoons homework won't start until 17:45, and it'll move to **Past**. If the work does get done, the teen can open it there and spend points to check it in late. The streak survives and the tick counts in full. Points settle overnight, and the 300 welcome points after onboarding mean the first few late check-ins cost nothing that matters.

For a school trip or a day off, long-press today in the week strip and pick **Skip Day**. It's a rest day, there are two a week, and no streak is broken.

Parents: don't comment on any of it. The Streak celebrations notification, which fires at 3, 7, 14, 21 and 30 days in a row, does the cheering for you.

## Later: one goal of their own

After a few weeks, if the teen wants, help them add one year goal they actually care about, such as passing the next guitar grade by summer. It breaks into a month milestone and a week milestone of five practice sessions, with each day's item linked to the Practice routine.

![Goals switched to the week, showing this week's goals by routine with their progress bars](/screenshots/guide/goals-week.jpg)

The week view shows the practice count climbing towards five ([the Goals chapter](/guide/goals) covers the rest). Five sessions finish a week, three good weeks finish a month, and six good months finish the year, which leaves room for exam season and holidays. A session ticked late reads Missed but still counts.

## Next step
