Ten minutes of morning mobility is enough for most desk workers, whose real goal is just to get up from the chair and move on purpose before the day takes over. Not every body goal is about a race or a heavier lift, and a small goal suits a small routine. Setting one up comes down to five decisions, each of which takes about a minute.

## Decision 1: when

Attach mobility to something that already happens, like the gap after the laptop opens and before the first email lands. Then add a smaller slot in the mid-afternoon dip.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 (the cue) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:50 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 07:10 | Morning mobility | 10 | Push the chair under the desk |
| 07:30 | Breakfast | 10 | Kettle on |
| 08:30 | Work | 45 | Open today's list |
| 15:00 | Afternoon reset | 10 | Stand up and step away from the screen |
| 18:30 | Walk | 10 | Shoes on |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 10 | Phone on charge in the hall |

That's 100 in total. Work takes 45, which is honest for a desk job. The three movement slots (mobility, the reset and the walk) share 30.

## Decision 2: the cue

Pushing the chair under the desk is step 1 because it's tiny and physical, and with the chair gone the floor is the obvious place to be. New routines arrive with suggested steps, so open this one in **More → Routines** and make sure step 1 is something you can do on autopilot. The 07:10 reminder leads with it, so it asks you to move the chair. It doesn't announce a workout.

## Decision 3: the steps

Five steps, about two minutes each:

1. Push the chair under the desk
2. Neck and shoulder rolls
3. Hip openers, both sides
4. Cat-cow on the floor
5. Two minutes walking round the room

They appear as the routine's checklist, and every tick inside the slot earns **Kinetics**. You don't need a gym, weights or kit. Ten minutes on a living-room floor with all five lines ticked is a full session.

At 07:10 the mobility card is front and centre on Home, with its five lines waiting ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard)).

The afternoon reset is even shorter: stand, reach overhead, refill your water, walk to a window and back. If someone shows you a stretch at 15:00, add it with the **+** as a quick task inside the reset.

## Decision 4: which reminder?

The two movement slots behave differently, so give them different modes:

- **Morning mobility: Notify.** It happens at roughly the same time every day, and Notify nudges you around then.
- **Afternoon reset: Alarm.** At 15:00 you're deep in a task, and a soft nudge gets swiped away. Alarm rings at 15:00 on the dot and again at 15:05 if nothing's ticked.

The reset's window runs from 14:00 to 15:30. Even an afternoon full of meetings usually has one gap somewhere in those ninety minutes.

## Decision 5: what it feeds

Linking mobility to a modest goal turns each tick into Geniuses too:

- **Year:** "Move every working day"
- **Month:** "Morning mobility on twenty working days"
- **Week:** "Five mornings this week", with five dated items linked to the routine

A Monday-to-Friday week lines up with the five met dates a week needs. Three closed weeks make a month, and six months close the year.

## When the morning slips

Some days start with a 07:30 call and the mobility doesn't happen. If you do the ten minutes at 12:30 instead, the routine is in **Past** on Home. Ticking it there before you log off costs its 10 points, and the streak continues. A few on-time mornings, settled overnight, easily cover it.

If it doesn't happen at all, let it go. Ten minutes missed isn't a crisis. At the end of the month, open **More → Progress → On the clock**. If the morning slot keeps losing to early calls, move the five steps to 15:00 and let the reset do the job. It's the same ten minutes, at an hour your calendar leaves alone.
