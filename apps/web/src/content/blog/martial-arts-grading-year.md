A year towards a belt grading works best as one syllabus block a month, with a short home drill that keeps each block moving between classes. A grading is one of the few goals that comes with its own syllabus. The examiner will ask for set stances, combinations, a form and some sparring, and the date is on the club noticeboard months ahead. The hard part is getting enough practice in between classes.

## The routines

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 (the cue) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:20 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 06:40 | Home drill | 20 | Tie the belt |
| 07:30 | Breakfast | 10 | Kettle on |
| 08:30 | Work or study | 40 | Open today's list |
| 19:00 | Class or review | 20 | Uniform into the bag |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 5 | Phone on charge in the hall |

The day adds up to 100. The two practice routines split 40 points evenly, because the home drill is where the syllabus actually gets learned. Class shows you what to fix. The drill is where you fix it.

Tying the belt is the cue for a reason: it takes ten seconds, it's physical, and once the belt is on, getting into a stance is the obvious next move. Set the drill's steps in **More → Routines**:

1. Tie the belt
2. Stances, ten of each, slowly
3. This month's combinations, twenty on each side
4. The form once slowly, then once at full speed
5. Note one correction from the last class

The 19:00 routine appears every evening. On class nights it's the class. On the others it's ten minutes reading your notes and watching the form once.

## The year, block by block

Write the year goal first, "Pass the next belt grading in October", then make the syllabus into month milestones:

- **January:** Stances and basic strikes, clean and slow
- **February:** Blocks and footwork
- **March:** Combinations 1–4
- **April:** Combinations 5–8
- **May:** The form, first half
- **June:** The form, second half
- **July:** The whole form at speed
- **August:** Sparring rules and distance
- **September:** Mock grading at class, then fix the weak spots
- **October:** Revision and the grading

![A year goal with its progress ring, the 12-month strip and one month's weeks and day items opened below](/screenshots/guide/goals-cascade.jpg)

Open the goal from **Goals → Year Goals** and the 12-month strip shows which blocks are done and which are still open ([the Goals chapter](/guide/goals)).

## Does closing the year goal mean you passed?

No, and it isn't meant to. Ten blocks are planned, and the goal closes once six of them are met. That tells you the preparation happened. Whether you pass is up to the examiner on the day.

Inside a block, March needs three met weeks, and each week closes on its fifth met date. A typical March week has five items, linked to the drill or the class routine so each one shows on the right card:

- Monday: "Combinations 1–2, twenty each side"
- Tuesday: "Class: ask about combination 3"
- Wednesday: "Combinations 3–4"
- Thursday: "Class"
- Saturday: "All four combinations, slow then fast"

Each tick sits under a week, a block and the grading, so it earns Geniuses on top of the checklist's Kinetics and the on-time start's Discipline.

## When a block falls behind

Real life doesn't always fit the blocks. Say the first half of the form takes six weeks instead of four. Re-plan: edit May's milestone, move "second half" into July, and fold "whole form at speed" into August. Ten planned months leave room for that, and re-planning is Geniuses work.

Day-to-day misses are smaller:

- **You drilled at 18:00 instead of 06:40.** The drill moved to **Past** on Home at 07:10. Check it in there that evening for its 20 points and the streak holds. The drill item is marked Missed because of the time, but it still fills its date in the week.
- **You're travelling.** Long-press today in the week strip for a Skip Day. You get two a week, and they're recorded as rest.

## The month before

Treat September's mock grading as a proper milestone, with one sub-task for each section of the syllabus. Tick the sections that went well and turn the rest into October's revision items. By grading day, nothing on the syllabus should come as a surprise.
