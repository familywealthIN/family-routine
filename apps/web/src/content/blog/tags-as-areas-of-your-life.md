Tags let you treat the areas of your life like the departments of a small company. Give every routine one `area:` tag, add a `project:` tag where a project lives, and each part of your week has an owner. The app then uses those tags to hand you context the moment a routine starts.

With six things running at once (a job, a side project, training, a course, family, a house), time usually isn't the problem. Context is. Every routine opens with ninety seconds of "where was I?", and at six routines a day that's nine minutes gone before any work.

## The company of you

Tags in Routine Notes nest with a colon, so you can build a small org chart. One scheme that works treats each area as a department head:

- `area:ceo`: you as a person, body and mind. Training, meditation, rest.
- `area:cto`: the work you're paid for.
- `area:cmo`: what you put into the world, such as writing, talks or a portfolio.
- `area:coo`: how you run the day: planning, admin, the night routine.
- `area:cxo`: the people closest to you.

Sub-areas add detail where it helps, such as `area:ceo:body` for running and `area:ceo:strength` for lifting. Projects cut across areas: `project:course` or `project:blog` goes on whichever routine does that work. Plain names like `area:health` or `area:home` work just as well. What matters is that no routine is ownerless.

## A tagged day, worked through

| Time | Routine | Points | Tags | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:00 | Reading & skills | 10 | `area:cto`, `project:course` | Phone face-down in another room |
| 07:00 | Run | 10 | `area:ceo:body` | Lace the running shoes |
| 08:00 | Plan the day | 10 | `area:coo:plan` | Open today's checklist |
| 08:30 | Deep work | 40 | `area:cto` | Close every tab but one |
| 19:00 | Writing | 10 | `area:cmo`, `project:blog` | Clear the desk |
| 19:40 | Strength | 10 | `area:ceo:strength` | Check today's split |
| 21:00 | Family time | 5 | `area:cxo` | Clear the table |
| 22:30 | Night routine | 5 | `area:coo` | Phone on charge outside the bedroom |

Add the points up by area and the budget reads like a company's: CTO 50, CEO 20, COO 15, CMO 10, CXO 5, for 100 in total. If that split looks wrong for this season, the points are what you change. Tags and points are both set in each routine's editor under **More → Routines**.

## What does a tag give you when the routine starts?

Open a routine on Home and its chat thread begins with a pinned card, **Before you start**. There's one block per `area:` or `project:` tag. Each block shows the standing commitment for that area, up to 3 next steps (each with an **Add** button that puts it on today's checklist), and up to 3 rows of past activity.

![Home on desktop with the week strip, the focused routine card and its checklist, and the routine chat beside it](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-day.jpg)

So at 19:00, Writing opens with a `project:blog` block showing what you did on Thursday and what comes next ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) shows the card and the chat). Under it sit two quick replies, *What did I do last time?* and *Plan from next steps*, which answer from the same context.

## One year goal per area

Tags keep the goal cascade balanced too. Give each area at most one year goal, linked to the routine that does its work. For `area:cmo` here, that's one post a week for a year: November's milestone is four posts, this week's is one, and the days are draft, edit, publish. Five met days close the week, three met weeks close the month, six met months close the year. If every CEO goal is behind while every CTO goal is ahead, that's Geniuses telling you to move points, not to try harder.

## What goes wrong when tags sprawl?

- **Too many areas.** Fifteen tags nobody remembers are worse than none. Five you use beat fifteen you have to look up.
- **One routine, many owners.** Three area tags on one routine produce a crowded brief. Use one `area:` tag and at most two `project:` tags.
- **A slipped week hides inside an area.** If both CEO routines were checked in late from **Past** all week, the problem is the morning, not running or lifting. Rescue them with points the same day so the streak holds, then move a start time.

## Finding it later

Tags travel with the goal items, and so do the notes on them. The **Search** tab in the add box finds a decision by the words you used. Through the MCP connector, `search_goals` can also narrow to one tag, such as every `project:course` item that mentions "module 4".

Don't retag everything tonight. Tag your two busiest routines, live with the brief for a week, then do the rest.
