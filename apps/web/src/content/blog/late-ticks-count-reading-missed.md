Late ticks count. When an item shows *Missed* next to a green tick, it was done after its window, and it still moves the week goal it belongs to. The label isn't a contradiction. Two questions are being answered at once, and once you see which is which, the Goals page reads more clearly.

## Two questions, two answers

Every goal item carries two facts:

1. **Did it happen?** That's the tick.
2. **Was it on time?** That's the status. *Done* means ticked inside the window. *Missed* means ticked outside it, or not at all.

Status records timing only. The tick never changes it, and it never changes the tick. "Ticked, Missed" is an honest record: the thing happened, and it happened late. For goals, only the first question matters. An item ticked late moves its week forward exactly as an on-time tick does.

## A worked week

Take someone whose evening wind-down carries the week goal "Wind-down routine on five nights". Here's the slice of their day the goal touches. The other 70 of their 100 points sit in morning and work routines.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 19:00 | Writing | 10 | Clear the desk |
| 21:00 | Family time | 10 | Clear the table |
| 22:30 | Wind-down | 10 | Phone on charge outside the bedroom |

Wind-down starts at 22:30, so its window opens at 21:30 and shuts at 23:00. Here's the week:

| Day | Wind-down item | Status | Counts toward the week? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Ticked 22:35 | Done | Yes |
| Tue | Done, ticked Wednesday 07:10 | Missed | Yes |
| Wed | Ticked 22:50 | Done | Yes |
| Thu | Not done | Missed | No |
| Fri | Ticked 22:40 | Done | Yes |
| Sat | Ticked 22:55 | Done | Yes |

That's 5 met nights out of 6, so the goal closes on Saturday, with 1 late tick and 1 genuinely missed night along the way. A week needs 5 met days however many were planned, and a date counts once every item linked to that week on it is ticked. Two items on Saturday still count as one day.

## Why keep both facts?

Couldn't a late tick just become *Done*? It could, but then the record would hide the one thing you most need to see. Discipline is showing up on time, and Kinetics is doing the work. A late tick is real work. It isn't Discipline, and pretending otherwise hides the pattern. If a fortnight shows three ticked-but-Missed items from the same routine, the start time is wrong.

**More → Progress** makes the same split. Its *On the clock* panel counts on-time and late check-ins by routine, separately from how much got done.

## Routines are not goal items

Don't confuse this with the routine itself. When Wind-down's window closes unticked at 23:00, the routine moves to **Past** on Home. That same day, before midnight, you can spend its 10 points to check it in late. The streak holds and its agents run. Goal items are looser. Tick them whenever you get to them, and they count.

## What goes wrong when you try to back-fill a day?

You can't create a goal item on a day that's already gone. If you went for an unplanned walk on Monday, you can't add "Walk" to Monday on Wednesday. Back-filling invented days would make every goal above it untrustworthy.

What you *can* do is tick an item that was created on its day and forgotten. If Monday's walk was already on Monday's list, tick it on Wednesday and it counts. So build the planning habit: two minutes at 22:30 adding tomorrow's items to their routines means tomorrow's work can always be counted, even when it gets ticked at 07:10 the next morning.

## How to read the Goals page now

- **Ticked + Done:** on time. Nothing to do.
- **Ticked + Missed:** done late. It counts. If one routine keeps doing it, move the routine.
- **Unticked + Missed:** not done, or done and forgotten. If you did it, tick it.
- **Rescheduled:** moved on purpose, with its original date kept.

The rule runs all the way up. Three met weeks close November, and six met months close the year, late ticks included.
