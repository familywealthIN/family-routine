Choosing between Notify, Alarm and Off for each routine matters more than how many reminders you get, because a reminder you've learned to ignore is worse than none. If your phone buzzes forty times a day, you probably swipe without reading. That's a sensible response to noise, and it's also why a routine app that pings for every routine falls apart within a fortnight. The run reminder ends up getting the same swipe as a marketing email.

## The three modes

- **Notify:** a nudge around the time you usually do the routine. It's for routines that already have a rhythm.
- **Alarm:** rings at exactly the start time, and again five minutes later if the routine isn't ticked. It's for hard starts, where five minutes late turns into forty.
- **Off:** no reminder at all. It's for routines that follow straight on from another one, or that you never forget.

Every reminder leads with the routine's first step, so if one isn't working, look at step 1 before you touch the mode. "Shoes on?" gets acted on far more often than "Run". You choose the mode in each routine's editor under **More → Routines**.

## One day, mode by mode

| Time | Routine | Points | Mode | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05:50 | Wake up | 5 | Alarm | A hard start, and the first routine of the day is the likeliest to be checked in late |
| 06:10 | Stretch | 5 | Off | Follows straight on from waking |
| 06:30 | Run | 15 | Notify | Already has a rhythm |
| 08:30 | Work | 40 | Off | You're at the desk anyway, so tick as you sit down |
| 13:00 | Lunch walk | 5 | Notify | Easy to forget, easy to do |
| 18:30 | Study | 15 | Alarm | The one that slips when evenings get busy |
| 21:00 | Family | 5 | Off | Nobody needs a reminder |
| 22:30 | Night routine | 10 | Notify | A gentle cue to start winding down |

The day adds up to 100. That's two alarms, three notifications and three routines left silent: five pings for eight routines, with the loud ones saved for the two that slip. The 18:30 Study alarm matters most. Study carries the year goal "Pass the evening course exam in June" and its week goal of five study evenings, and its window closes at 19:00, only half an hour after the first ring.

## How many alarms is too many?

More than two. If everything rings, the alarm turns into background noise like everything else. Choose the routines that are both important and easy to miss, and let the record tell you which those are. **More → Progress → On the clock** compares on-time and late check-ins for each routine. The one with the most late ticks is your first candidate for Alarm.

Move a routine back to **Notify** after a few weeks of on-time ticks. Alarm is for building a habit and Notify is for keeping it. Use **Off** more than you'd expect, too: a stretch that follows waking gets its cue from the routine before it.

## The six daily notifications

Separate from the per-routine reminders, **More → Notifications** has six daily ones, and each can be switched on or off:

- **Good morning:** your day at a glance, at a time you choose. If you keep only one, keep this.
- **Day planning:** suggested tasks from your week when nothing is planned yet.
- **End of day progress:** how the day went and what's still possible, at a time you choose.
- **Streak celebrations:** when a routine reaches 3, 7, 14, 21 or 30 days in a row, and beyond.
- **Insights for you:** when your routines have insights you haven't opened yet.
- **Welcome back:** a gentle nudge after a couple of days away, three at most.

If notifications are your problem, start with Good morning, End of day progress and two alarms, and turn the rest off. Switch others back on only when you notice you miss them.

## When a reminder fails

The alarm rang at 18:30, you swiped it at 18:35, and 19:00 came and went. Study is now in **Past** on Home. If you studied at 20:00 after all, tick it there before midnight for its 15 points. The streak keeps going and any agents on the routine still run.

If the same reminder gets swiped three days running, don't make it louder. Change the plan instead: move the start time, shrink step 1, or ask whether the routine belongs at that hour at all. No reminder can rescue a routine that sits at the wrong time.
