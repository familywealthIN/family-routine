A travelling consultant can keep one set of routines for the whole week, at home and at the client site, as long as every routine makes sense in any building. "Kitchen clean-up" doesn't travel. "Night routine" does.

The week usually looks the same: fly out Monday morning, three days on site, fly home Thursday night, Friday in the home office. Everything around that changes: the hotel, the flight time, a dinner that runs late. Here's how the one set of routines gets through a week like that.

## What makes a routine portable?

Its cue goes in the suitcase. Every first step below can be done in a hotel room.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:00 | Wake up | 5 | Curtains open, glass of water |
| 06:30 | Gym or run | 10 | Kit laid out the night before |
| 07:30 | Breakfast and prep | 10 | Open today's client agenda |
| 08:30 | Client work | 35 | Laptop open at the desk |
| 12:30 | Lunch and notes | 5 | Three lines on the morning |
| 17:30 | Write-up | 15 | Open the project note |
| 19:00 | Call home | 10 | Headphones in |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 10 | Pack tomorrow's bag |

Client work carries 35 of the 100 because it's the job. Call home carries 10, double Wake up, because a week away is exactly when calling home gets dropped.

## Monday: the travel day

The flight is at 06:40. Rather than accept a morning of misses, the consultant long-presses Monday in the week strip and takes a Skip Day. The day counts as rest and the streak survives. There are only two a week, so they're worth saving for days like this one.

Keeping Monday and letting the morning routines pass unticked is just as honest. What isn't honest is ticking Wake up at the gate at 05:50 and counting it as a normal morning.

## Tuesday to Thursday on site

The hotel has the same three cues as home: kit on the chair, the agenda open over breakfast, the bag packed at night. A 06:30 workout can be ticked on time from 05:30 to 07:00, which allows for a hotel gym that opens at 06:00.

Client work earns its Kinetics from its steps (interview, synthesis, slide draft) and from quick tasks added during the day. "Send the interview summary to the sponsor by 4" goes into the current slot with its time already set.

If the client is in a different time zone, re-time the week under More → Routines and put the times back on Friday. Start times move in 10-minute steps, so eight routines take about five minutes.

## Goals that span client weeks

Engagements last months, so they go on the cascade. The year goal is to lead a full engagement end to end. This month's milestone is discovery signed off by the sponsor. This week's is interviewing every stakeholder, with one named interview a day, each linked to Client work.

![Goals switched to the week view, listing this week's goals by routine with their progress](/screenshots/guide/goals-week.jpg)

The week view shows each goal's progress per routine ([the Goals chapter](/guide/goals) covers the views), and it shows the trap. A week closes on its own once five of its days are met. If the stakeholder week only has items on Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, it can never get past three. So Monday gets "Prepare the interview questions" and Friday gets "Write up the interview themes". That's five days that can each be met, and the month still needs only three of its four or five weeks.

## Thursday night: the late flight

The flight home lands at 22:50, and the Night routine's window closed at 22:30. The consultant does the night routine anyway, at 23:30, then spends its 10 points from **Past** to check it in. It's still the same day, and it really happened, so this is exactly what a late check-in is for. The streak holds, and any agent on the routine still runs.

An interview held on Wednesday but only ticked in Thursday's taxi still counts. The item reads Missed, since the status records when it was ticked, but the stakeholder week moves on.

## Friday: home office

On Friday the same eight routines run from home. Client work becomes the week's write-up, and Call home becomes dinner at the table.

## Rules for the road

- Pack the three cues: kit, agenda, bag.
- Save Skip Days for travel days you really can't change.
- Only check in late for things that actually happened.
- Re-time for time zones, not because you're tired.
