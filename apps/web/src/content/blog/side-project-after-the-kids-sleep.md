A 21:00 build session, held after the kids are asleep, is enough to keep shipping a side project once a month. The side project gets the hours nobody else wants: after the day job, after dinner, after bath time, after the second glass of water someone suddenly needs. Once the house goes quiet, the side project either gets built or gets scrolled away.

This post covers one routine, one monthly goal, and a detail of the week rule that changes how you plan.

## One evening slot, defended

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Wake up | 5 | Water from the bedside |
| 07:00 | School run | 10 | Bags by the door |
| 09:00 | Day job | 30 | Open today's tickets |
| 12:30 | Walk | 5 | Shoes on at the desk |
| 18:00 | Dinner & bedtime | 15 | Phones in the drawer |
| 21:00 | Build session | 25 | Laptop open at the kitchen table |
| 22:30 | Night | 10 | Laptop shut, charger in the hall |

That's 100. The build session gets 25, second only to the day job, which makes plain where the evening goes. Its window runs from 20:00 to 21:30, which covers the usual bedtime delays. If the second story runs long, starting at 21:20 still counts as on time.

## What happens in one session?

The checklist keeps a tired brain moving:

1. Laptop open at the kitchen table.
2. Read last session's note.
3. One task from today's item, nothing else.
4. Commit and push.
5. Write a note for next time.

Item 1 is the cue, and the 21:00 reminder opens with it. Items 3 and 5 do the real work. Picking one task means no hour spent browsing to decide what to do, and the note means tomorrow's item 2 has something to read. Working the list earns **Kinetics**, and ticking the routine inside its window earns **Discipline**.

## The monthly ship goal

Side projects need a deadline, and monthly is one a parent can keep:

- **Year:** "Launch the app and reach 100 sign-ups."
- **Month:** "Ship the offline mode."
- **Week:** "Sync conflicts handled and tested."
- **Day:** "Test edits on two devices", linked to Build session.

Ticking a linked day item earns **Geniuses**, and items higher in the plan weigh more. Six months of shipping something completes the year goal. Three good weeks complete each month.

## Why do four long sessions fail the week?

A week goal completes once **five days are met**, and a day counts once every item tied to that week on that date is ticked.

So four long sessions (Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday, 90 minutes each) can never complete the week, however well they go. Four is not five. Plan five shorter sessions instead, 40 to 50 minutes each, on five different evenings. That's less time in total, but the week counts, and a short session is easier to start at 21:00 anyway.

Got more energy? Plan six or seven evenings. Planning more is ambition, not a higher bar: the week still completes at five.

## When bedtime wins and the session slips

Some nights the build session doesn't happen. A child is ill, the day job has a release, or you fall asleep on the sofa at 20:45. That leaves two cases.

- **You built, but late.** Bedtime ran to 22:00, you did 40 minutes, and the window had closed by then. The routine sits in **Past** on Home. Check it in from there before midnight with points, and the streak holds. If you forgot to tick the day item too, tick it in the morning. It will show **Missed**, but the week still gets the credit.
- **You didn't build.** Let the evening go. If the whole day was lost, long-press today in the week strip and choose **Skip Day**. You get two a week, and a skipped day doesn't count against you.

Points from good evenings are added overnight, and they pay for late check-ins on bad ones.

## Notes that come back

Write the "note for next time" in the day item's notes. Then open **More → Routines**, tap Build session, and add a project tag such as `project:offline-app`.

![Home on desktop: the week strip, the focused routine card with its checklist, and the routine chat beside it](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-day.jpg)

With the tag in place, the routine's chat on Home opens with a "Before you start" brief for the project: the standing commitment, up to three next steps you can add with one tap, and recent activity ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) covers the card and the chat). Tap "What did I do last time?" and you're back where you stopped.
