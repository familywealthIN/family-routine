An evening blog slot at 19:00 holds better than a dawn routine for one plain reason: when it starts, the writer is already awake, at home and near a keyboard. A 05:30 writing routine competes with sleep. A 19:00 one only competes with the sofa.

## Why does 19:00 hold when 05:30 doesn't?

In Routine Notes the first routine of the day is often the one ticked late. Nobody is looking at a screen when it starts. An evening routine's reminder reaches someone who has had their phone in hand for hours and can act on it straight away. For someone with a day job, that's the real case for writing at night: the slot starts where you already are.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 07:00 | Breakfast | 5 | Kettle on |
| 08:30 | Work | 45 | Open today's list |
| 12:30 | Lunch walk | 5 | Shoes on |
| 18:00 | Dinner | 10 | Clear the table |
| 19:00 | Write | 20 | Clear the desk |
| 20:10 | Stretch | 5 | Mat out |
| 22:00 | Wind-down | 5 | Phone on charge |

Writing gets 20 points, second only to work. Its window opens at 18:00 and closes at 19:30, so it overlaps the end of dinner, and clearing the table leads straight into clearing the desk. Create the routine under More → Routines, and put the blog's four or five subjects in its description, one per line. When the slot opens, the question becomes "which of these?" rather than "what should I write?".

## The first month, in three ten-day blocks

**Days 1–10: be at the desk.** Only Discipline matters. Each evening has one day item, "Draft 300 words", linked to Write. The steps are:

1. Clear the desk.
2. Fill a glass of water.
3. Close every tab except the draft.
4. Write for 40 minutes.
5. Leave tomorrow's first sentence at the bottom.

Step 5 means tomorrow never starts on an empty page.

**Days 11–20: let the work earn.** Kinetics comes from working the steps and from any quick tasks finished inside the slot. When a draft needs a cover image or a broken link checked, don't open a new tab.

![The add box open over Home, adding a task to the routine that is currently in progress](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-quicktask.jpg)

Home → + opens the add box. Type "Find a cover image for the travel post" and it lands inside the Write routine ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) shows the whole box). The week goal at this point is "Write five evenings". It closes once five days are met, so a Friday out with friends doesn't cost the week.

**Days 21–30: give the words a destination.** Add Geniuses. The month milestone is "Publish four posts". Each week milestone is "One post drafted, edited and published". Above them sits a year goal: an archive you'd be glad for a stranger to read. Link each evening's item to that week. Now ticking "Edit the travel draft" moves the week, the month and the year, and the year closes at six met months.

## What if the evening slips?

Friends call at 18:45 and you go out. At 19:30 the window shuts and Write moves to **Past**. There are two honest outcomes:

- **You wrote at 22:00.** Spend points from Past to check the routine in late, today, before midnight. The streak holds and the tick earns its points. The day item reads Missed, because status records timing, but it still counts toward the week.
- **You didn't write.** Don't buy the tick. Either leave the day empty, or, if it was planned rest, long-press today in the week strip for a Skip Day. You get two a week.

Points are for checking in real work late. They don't make the work happen. If late check-ins pile up on one weekday, move that day's item to a different evening rather than paying for the same slip every week.

## What to set up tonight

- Create Write at the time you're actually free, not the time you wish you were.
- Put the blog's subjects in its description.
- Make "Clear the desk" step 1.
- Tick the routine before 19:30 tomorrow.
