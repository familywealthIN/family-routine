A first-time speaker with eight weeks to the talk should rehearse out loud from week one, on a fixed rehearsal routine, and let the slides catch up with what they actually say. Most people do the opposite: seven weeks on slides, then one week of panicked run-throughs.

## Why rehearse before the slides exist?

Because the talk is what you say, not what's on the screen. Saying the outline aloud on day three shows which parts you can't explain yet, and those parts don't need slides, they need thinking. These three routines carry the talk; the other 70 of the day's 100 points stay on work, exercise and home.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 07:00 | Rehearse | 15 | Stand up, timer on |
| 12:40 | Opening out loud | 5 | Stand by the window |
| 20:30 | Slides & notes | 10 | Deck open at today's section |

Rehearsal goes first in the day because speaking aloud to an empty room feels awkward, and awkward things get skipped later on. The 12:40 routine takes two minutes: say the opening once, standing up. The evening slot is the quiet work of tidying slides after the morning's run.

## The rehearsal checklist

The one rule of this goal's Kinetics is that you rehearse by speaking, not by reading slides in your head. The steps make that hard to dodge:

1. **Stand up, timer on.** Standing is the cue. Sitting down to rehearse turns into reading.
2. **Say the opening two minutes aloud.**
3. **Run today's section in full,** at stage pace.
4. **Record it.** Phone propped up, one take.
5. **Watch thirty seconds back.** Only thirty.
6. **Note one fix** for tonight's slides session.

Each ticked step earns Kinetics. Step 4 is the one people want to skip and the one that matters most, because the recording catches the filler words, the slide you talk over, and the joke that runs a minute long.

## Counting back from the date

Put the talk date on the month goal "Talk ready to deliver" as its deadline, and count the week milestones back from it:

| Week | Milestone |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Outline spoken aloud end to end, however rough |
| 2 | Opening and close drafted and rehearsed daily |
| 3 | Sections 1–2, recorded each morning |
| 4 | Sections 3–4, recorded each morning |
| 5 | First full run, timed |
| 6 | Feedback run with a friend |
| 7 | Three timed full runs, cuts made |
| 8 | Light runs, logistics, rest |

Each morning's day item names the section ("Rehearse section 3, record") and links to Rehearse and to that week's milestone, which adds Geniuses to every tick. With 6 rehearsals planned a week, 5 met days close it, so a packed Thursday at work doesn't break the plan.

## Where does the feedback run live?

Week 6 holds the one item you can't do alone: "Full run in front of a friend". Add it now on the date you agree.

![The four Priority quadrants, Do now, Plan, Delegate and Automate, with day goals grouped by routine](/screenshots/guide/priority-quadrants.jpg)

Dated ahead, it files itself under **Plan** in Priority and sits there for weeks ([the Priority chapter](/guide/priority) explains the sorting). It's a useful reminder that someone else's diary is now part of your plan. Book the friend in week 4, not week 6.

## When a rehearsal slips

A morning meeting means the 07:00 rehearsal starts at 07:45, after its 07:30 close, and Rehearse moves to **Past** on Home. Rehearse anyway, then spend points to check it in late that day. The streak holds and the tick earns its 15. The day item reads Missed for timing and still counts toward the week.

If mornings keep slipping, open the routine in More → Routines and switch its reminder from Notify to Alarm, which rings at exactly 07:00 and again five minutes later. If a morning goes entirely, rehearse at lunch alongside the opening routine and check in late. Even then you've had a day with an out-loud run.

## After the talk

Don't delete anything. The month goal closes, and Rehearse can stay at 5 points with one step, "Two-minute opening, out loud", which keeps the habit alive cheaply for the next talk.
