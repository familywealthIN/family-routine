Getting ready for a multi-day trek is easiest as a countdown in month milestones, starting from the departure date and working backwards. Trek preparation has two halves, and both go wrong the same way. Fitness gets left until the last six weeks, when it's too late to build properly. Kit gets left until the last weekend, when the boots turn out to pinch and the stove is missing a part. Both are timing problems, and a countdown fixes both.

## The countdown

Write the year goal with a deadline, "Complete the five-day trek in September", then add one month milestone per block, each due at the end of its month:

- **April:** Walk every day, with one long walk each weekend
- **May:** Hills, with real climbing on the weekend walks
- **June:** Carry the pack on the weekend walks
- **July:** Back-to-back long days every other weekend
- **August:** Kit finalised, with volume easing in the last fortnight
- **September:** The trek

That's six milestones, and six met months is exactly what closes the year, so there's no slack. Add a March milestone ("Boots worn in, a walk every other day") for a month of room. Otherwise one bad month means re-planning everything after it.

## Why put a deadline on every milestone?

In a gym programme, a milestone that slips by a fortnight is a nuisance. With a fixed departure date, it squeezes everything that comes after it. Give every milestone its deadline, and the month calendar on **Goals** shows the countdown getting tight before it actually is.

![The Goals page with its period ladder, a month calendar of day rings and the day's goals grouped by routine](/screenshots/guide/goals-calendar.jpg)

Each day's ring fills as that day's items are ticked, so a June week with two empty rings is obvious ([the Goals chapter](/guide/goals)).

## The routines

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 (the cue) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:10 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 06:30 | Walk | 25 | Boots on |
| 07:40 | Breakfast | 10 | Kettle on |
| 08:30 | Work | 40 | Open today's list |
| 18:30 | Stairs or strength | 10 | Shoes on, out to the stairwell |
| 21:30 | Kit & plan | 5 | Open the kit list |
| 22:10 | Night routine | 5 | Phone on charge in the hall |

On weekdays, Walk is 40 minutes round the neighbourhood in the boots you'll trek in. On Saturdays it's the long one. You still leave inside the 05:30–07:00 window and get back in the afternoon. Its steps (boots on, pack on if it's a pack week, the walk itself, distance and feet noted) earn Kinetics each time you tick them.

## A June week, item by item

The June week goal reads "Long walk with a 10 kg pack, plus four short". It closes on its fifth met date, so every week gets five dated items linked to Walk:

- Monday: "Walk 40 minutes in trekking boots"
- Tuesday: "Stairs: 15 minutes with the pack"
- Wednesday: "Walk 40 minutes"
- Thursday: "Walk 40 minutes, pack on"
- Saturday: "Long walk, 18 km, 10 kg pack"

The items appear on the Walk card every morning, so you always know whether today is a pack day. Each one sits under June, under the trek, and that's where Geniuses comes from.

## Kit as sub-tasks

Kit & plan at 21:30 is ten minutes with the list, and the list is a set of sub-tasks on August's "Kit finalised" milestone:

- Boots worn in on at least three long walks
- Pack fitted and tested at full weight
- Waterproofs checked for leaks
- Stove tested
- First-aid kit restocked
- Maps and route notes printed

Because the list sits on a dated milestone, it's never a vague "sort the kit out" hanging around. An untested stove in mid-August still leaves you a fortnight to deal with it.

## When the countdown slips

- **You walked at 19:00.** The 06:30 Walk moved to **Past** on Home at 07:00. Check it in there before bed for its 25 points and the week keeps its date.
- **Rain washed out Saturday.** Ask the routine chat to defer the long walk to Sunday.
- **You're away for work.** Long-press today in the week strip for a Skip Day. Two a week, recorded as rest.

If a whole month goes, re-plan it. Merge June's pack weeks into July and shorten the back-to-back block. Check the result on **Goals → Year Goals**. Re-planning around a fixed date is Geniuses work.

## Trek week

Put each of the five trek days in as a day item. On those days, the Walk routine is the trek.
