A nine-month training programme maps cleanly onto nine month milestones, one phase each, under a single year goal. Long programmes usually come as a spreadsheet with nine tabs. The spreadsheet itself is fine. The trouble is that nobody opens it at 06:20 on a Tuesday in month six, so the plan lives in one place and the training happens in another.

With the programme in the cascade, the session you're about to do sits on the routine card, and the phase it belongs to is one tab away.

## The routines

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 (the cue) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:00 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 06:20 | Train | 30 | Pick up the packed gym bag |
| 07:40 | Breakfast | 10 | Kettle on |
| 08:30 | Work | 35 | Open today's list |
| 13:00 | Walk | 5 | Coat on |
| 19:00 | Cardio or mobility | 10 | Shoes on |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 5 | Pack tomorrow's gym bag |

Train carries 30 of the 100, because this year the programme is the main event. Its steps stay the same whatever the phase: bag, warm-up, main lift, accessories, top set logged. Working through them earns Kinetics, and starting between 05:20 and 06:50 earns Discipline.

## Nine phases, one year

The programme runs April to December, so all of it fits in one calendar year. The year goal is "Complete every phase of the programme". One milestone per month:

1. **April:** Foundation, with technique on every main lift
2. **May:** Strength and volume
3. **June:** Strength and volume, heavier
4. **July:** Deload and re-evaluate
5. **August:** Hypertrophy, with exercise variations
6. **September:** Compound-lift focus
7. **October:** More cardio, keeping the lifts
8. **November:** More cardio, building the evening sessions
9. **December:** Maintenance and evaluation

The year closes once six of the nine are met. Planning nine is ambition, not a higher bar, and it means a June lost to moving house doesn't stop the programme closing in December.

## Why is the deload month the test?

Because a deload is where cascades usually break. The programme says do less, so people plan less, and then none of July's weeks can close.

The weekly bar is five met dates, and how heavy the session was doesn't come into it. So a deload week still gets five dated items, just lighter ones:

- Monday: "Squat and press at 60%, three sets"
- Tuesday: "Mobility, 20 minutes"
- Wednesday: "Deadlift technique, light"
- Friday: "Upper body, half the usual sets"
- Saturday: "Re-test: top set on each main lift, log it"

Five met dates close the week, and three closed weeks close July. A deload that shows up as met is a deload that actually happened, which is more than a spreadsheet tab can tell you.

Switch the Train card to Week mid-deload and you'll see how many of the five lighter sessions are already ticked ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard)).

## Re-evaluating means re-planning

July's milestone says "re-evaluate", and it means it. Take the re-test numbers and edit the months ahead. August's hypertrophy phase might run longer and September's compound focus shorter. Or October's cardio might start early because the 19:00 routine has been going well. Editing milestones mid-year is Geniuses work, and every ticked session under the rewritten plan still earns it. A revised plan you follow is worth more than an original you've quietly dropped.

## Slipped sessions

- **You trained at 18:00.** The 06:20 window shut at 06:50, so Train is waiting in **Past** on Home. Tick it from there before the day ends for its 30 points and the streak holds. The session item is marked Missed for its timing, but it still counts towards the deload week.
- **You couldn't train at all.** Ask the routine chat to move the item to tomorrow, and the week can still reach five.
- **You're away.** Long-press today in the week strip. Skip Day allows two rest days a week.

## Where to look

Switch the Train card to Month to see the phase you're in, and to Year to count the phases that are met. **Goals → Year Goals** lays the whole programme out as a 12-month strip: the spreadsheet, finally in the same place as the training.
