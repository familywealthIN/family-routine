Studying for a certification around a full-time job tends to stall in week four, when practice scores stop rising even though every session is still happening. Breaking that plateau takes a re-plan, not more hours. In Routine Notes it's a Geniuses problem rather than a Discipline or Kinetics one.

## The setup

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05:50 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 06:10 | Study | 15 | Laptop open on the study guide |
| 07:30 | Get ready | 5 | Clothes laid out the night before |
| 08:30 | Work | 45 | Open today's list |
| 12:30 | Lunch flashcards | 5 | Cards in the bag |
| 18:30 | Exercise | 10 | Kit by the door |
| 21:00 | Practice questions | 10 | Timer on, 20 questions |
| 22:30 | Night routine | 5 | Phone on charge |

The 06:10 session is the core: an hour before work, worth 15. The 21:00 questions are shorter and do the testing. Lunch flashcards are the five-point routine you keep even on the worst day.

## What does a study session look like on screen?

Each morning has one day item, linked to Study and to that week's milestone, and its sub-tasks spell the session out:

- Read section 3.2
- Write a one-page summary
- 20 questions on 3.2
- Review every wrong answer
- Add three flashcards

![Home on a phone with a routine in progress, its five-item checklist with one item ticked, and the Today, Week, Month and Year tabs](/screenshots/home-mobile.png)

On a phone the routine card shows the checklist with ticks as you go ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) explains each part). Ticking sub-tasks keeps the hour honest, and you tick the item itself once the last one is done.

## The milestone that matters most

It isn't a study session. It's a week-one day item called "Book the exam". Once the exam is booked there's a date, the date becomes the year goal's deadline, and every milestone under it has something to count down to. The rest of the cascade is conventional: the year goal is the certification, the months are its domains, and the weeks are sets such as "Domain 2, sections 1–4, plus a practice test".

## Why did the scores stop rising?

By week four there are three practice tests to compare. They level off because the plan still follows the study guide's chapter order, and chapter order gives equal time to everything, including what you already know.

Here's how to see it. Open the add box with Home → +, switch to its Search tab, and type a domain name. Your notes and sub-tasks come back with their dates, and the pattern is usually blunt. Ten sessions went to a domain you score 85% on, and three rushed ones to the domain you score 50% on.

## Breaking the plateau: re-plan by weakness, not chapter

Re-plan the next two weeks:

1. **Rank the domains by practice score**, lowest first.
2. **Edit the week milestones mid-week.** "Domain 4: sections 1–4" becomes "Domain 2: wrong-answer review, every question from the last three tests". Milestones are meant to be edited, and a better plan is what Geniuses rewards.
3. **Change what the 21:00 routine is for.** Its 20 questions now come only from the two weakest domains.
4. **Keep one strong-domain review a week** so it doesn't fade.

None of this resets progress. A week closes at 5 met days, a month at 3 met weeks and the year at 6 met months. Rewording a milestone doesn't wipe the days already met under it.

## When a study morning runs late

Some days the work inbox wins and the session starts at 06:50, ten minutes after its window closed. Home moves Study to **Past**. Do the hour anyway, then check it in late with points before the day ends: the streak stays and the tick pays out normally. The day item ticked at 06:50 shows Missed, which is the timing, and it still counts for the week.

The night before a big deadline at work, decide in advance whether the morning session is happening. If it isn't, long-press today in the week strip for a Skip Day. It's free, but there are only two a week.
