A manager with seven meetings in a day can still keep every promise by turning each one into a quick task inside the current time box. A delivery manager's Tuesday doesn't produce much in the meetings themselves. It produces promises: "I'll send that over", "let me check with legal", "I'll book the room". The work is keeping them, and the risk is a notebook that never gets opened again.

Here's one Tuesday, block by block.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:40 | Wake up | 5 | Curtains open |
| 07:20 | Exercise | 10 | Kit by the door |
| 08:30 | Plan the day | 10 | Calendar and Priority side by side |
| 09:00 | Morning meetings | 25 | Notebook open |
| 12:30 | Lunch | 5 | Away from the screen |
| 13:30 | Afternoon meetings | 25 | Re-read the morning's tasks |
| 16:30 | Follow-through hour | 15 | Open the Inbox |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 5 | Phone down |

Half of the day's 100 points sit on the two meeting blocks. The other half keeps the rest of the day standing.

## 08:30: how do you plan a day you don't control?

You plan capacity instead of time. Open **Priority** and count Do now. Anything already started today is there, and the usual rule is three items or fewer. With seven meetings ahead, two is more honest.

![The move sheet opened from a Priority item, offering the Do now, Plan, Delegate and Automate quadrants](/screenshots/guide/priority-editor.jpg)

Anything that can wait goes to Plan, either from this sheet or by giving it a future date ([the Priority chapter](/guide/priority) covers both). Thursday's work stays out of Tuesday's count.

## 09:00 to 12:30: catch everything

Morning meetings is one routine covering four meetings. It can be ticked on time from 08:00 to 09:30, so you tick it walking into the first one. Its checklist is about capture, not attendance:

1. Notebook open
2. Every promise becomes a quick task
3. Two lines of notes per meeting
4. Close the block with nothing left only in the notebook

Step 2 does most of the work. At 09:40, someone asks for a revised timeline. Tap **+** and type "Send revised timeline to the design team by 3pm". The date and time fill themselves in, and the task lands in Morning meetings, the slot that's running. Each one finished adds Kinetics to that routine. For this manager, keeping promises is the work.

## 13:30: let the chat catch the leftovers

By the afternoon, two promises got past step 2. Open the routine chat for Afternoon meetings and type "Add a task: book the big room for Thursday's planning session". It's added, and the chat shows it as a live checkbox in the thread. For something bigger, like "Prepare the quarterly planning pack", ask the chat to break it down. It suggests a short list, and Add all puts every line on the checklist.

The chat can also move things. "Defer the vendor reply to tomorrow" does that without opening an editor.

## 16:30: the follow-through hour

This routine exists to keep the morning's promises. Its first step is opening the Inbox, the tray icon at the top of Home, which holds tasks that have no routine, often ones added from Search. Each item gets one of two choices. **Do now** puts it on the current routine. **Move to routine** files it under the routine where it belongs tomorrow. Then work through the day's open quick tasks until none are left. The revised timeline went out at 14:40.

## The goal behind the meetings

Meetings aren't the outcome, so the cascade tracks what they're meant to produce. The year goal is a team that plans its own quarters. This month's milestone is a planning guide handed over to the leads. The week milestone is one section of the guide drafted each working day, with day items linked to the Follow-through hour. Each section ticked earns Geniuses.

The guide week has five sections planned on five working days, and a week needs exactly five met days. The month needs three such weeks, and the year needs six such months.

## What if the follow-through hour gets missed?

A leadership meeting runs until 17:20, and the follow-through window closed at 17:00. If the follow-up still happens at 18:00, spend 15 points from **Past** to check it in that evening. It's a full tick, and the streak holds. If it doesn't happen, leave it, and tomorrow's 08:30 read will surface every open task. A guide section drafted on Wednesday but ticked on Thursday still counts. It reads Missed, but it counts.

## The habit to build

For one week, keep one rule: nothing leaves a meeting only in the notebook. Set the Follow-through hour under More → Routines, and on Friday count how many promises from the week went out on time.
