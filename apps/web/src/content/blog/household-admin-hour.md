An admin hour clears a household backlog without eating the week, because the forms, letters and bookings all wait for one 18:00 slot instead of leaking into every evening. A school letter to sign, a passport that runs out in March, a parcel to send back, a service to book. None of it is difficult. It just piles up until each item feels heavier than it is, usually on whoever noticed it first.

## Do you need an admin hour?

Probably, if any of these sound familiar:

- You do admin in odd moments, so it's always half-done.
- Things get remembered at 23:00 and forgotten again by breakfast.
- The same three jobs have been "this week" for a month.
- You're doing jobs that really belong to someone else.

## The routine and its five steps

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:50 | Morning | 10 | Glass of water |
| 08:00 | Commute and read | 10 | Book in the bag |
| 09:00 | Work | 40 | Notebook open |
| 13:00 | Lunch | 5 | Step away from the desk |
| 18:00 | Admin hour | 20 | Open the Inbox |
| 19:00 | Dinner and family | 10 | Clear the table |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 5 | Phone on charge |

That's 100. Twenty points on admin looks like too much, and that's deliberate. A 5-point routine is easy to let slide. At 20, you notice when it goes.

Set it up in **More → Routines**, and make its checklist the method:

1. Open the Inbox
2. File everything into a routine, or delete it
3. Do the three oldest items
4. Delegate anything that isn't yours
5. Schedule anything that needs a particular day

## Feeding the pile during the week

Admin arrives at bad moments, so capture it in seconds and deal with it at 18:00.

![The add box open over Home, adding 'Call dentist tomorrow at 10am' to the current routine](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-quicktask.jpg)

Tap the **+** beside the chat box and type it the way you'd say it: "Book the car service on Thursday at 8am." The date and time are filled in for you, and the task lands in whatever routine slot you're in ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) covers the add box).

Anything without a routine waits in the Inbox, the tray icon at the top of Home with its count. During the admin hour, "Do now" pulls an item into the current slot, and "Move to routine" files it elsewhere, for example into Friday's work block.

Jobs with several stages get sub-tasks on the item itself. "Renew the passport" becomes: photo, form, countersignature, post.

## Why Kinetics is the score that matters here

**Discipline** comes from starting inside the 17:00–18:30 window. With admin, though, starting is rarely the problem. Finishing is.

**Kinetics** builds up from working the routine: each step and each quick task cleared in the slot. An evening where you opened the Inbox and filed three things scores less than one where you also cleared the three oldest items, and you can see the difference. That's what stops the admin hour becoming an hour of reorganising the list.

## Delegate by @mention

Some of the pile isn't yours. Add it with an @mention ("@partner sign the trip form tonight") and **Priority** files it under **Delegate**. Look at that column once a week. If it's always empty, the admin you're doing isn't all yours.

## A goal that keeps the pile small

A year goal such as "Household admin never more than a week old" can sit over a June milestone and a week milestone of "Inbox cleared on five evenings". Each evening's "Inbox to zero" item is linked to the Admin hour routine. Five cleared evenings close the week, three such weeks close June, and six such months close the year.

## What if the admin hour slips?

Most weeks, admin will start at 20:30 once or twice because dinner ran long. By then the routine is in **Past**. Do the hour, then spend points to check it in. The streak continues, and the items you clear still count towards the five evenings, even though they read Missed.

If you're away for the day, long-press today in the week strip and pick **Skip Day**. It's one of the week's two rest days and breaks no run. The Inbox will still be there when you get back.
