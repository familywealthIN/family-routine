A boulderer's checklist turns a session at the wall into technique work, and planned rest days give the week the five met days it needs. Without a structure, sessions drift. You arrive meaning to work on footwork, spot a friend on the new purple problem, and two hours later you've had thirty goes at one move and learned nothing about your feet.

## The day around the wall

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 (the cue) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:50 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 07:20 | Breakfast & commute | 10 | Bag by the door |
| 08:30 | Work | 40 | Open today's list |
| 12:40 | Lunch walk | 5 | Stand up from the desk |
| 18:30 | Climb | 25 | Chalk bag into the car |
| 21:00 | Log & plan | 10 | Open the session notes |
| 22:20 | Night routine | 5 | Phone on charge in the hall |

The day adds up to 100. Climb comes round every evening at 18:30, because routines repeat daily. On wall nights it's a session, and on rest nights it's something short and deliberate. Either way, the window opens at 17:30 and closes at 19:00.

## The session as six steps

The steps are the checklist, shown in order. This version keeps a session honest:

1. Chalk bag into the car
2. Warm up on five easy problems
3. Technique drill: today's focus, 20 minutes
4. Project attempts, resting between them
5. Two easy problems to finish
6. Note one thing that worked

Each step ticked in the slot earns Kinetics, the pillar for doing the work properly. The drill matters most and is the step most likely to get skipped, which is why it comes before the project. Anything extra, like filming the crux or trying a friend's problem, goes in through the **+** as a quick task inside the Climb slot.

![Home on a phone: a routine in progress with a five-item checklist, one item ticked, and Add task below it](/screenshots/home-mobile.png)

On a phone the six steps sit on the focused card, and you tick them between attempts with chalky fingers ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard)).

Step 6 takes ten seconds and pays off later. The lesson goes into the session's day item as a note, and a month from now the Search page will pull up every one of them.

## Why do rest days need items?

A climbing week closes on its fifth met date, and a date counts only when everything filed under the week for it is ticked. Three wall nights with nothing planned in between leave the week stuck at three.

So give each rest night a small, real item, linked to Climb:

- Monday: "Rest: stretch, 15 minutes"
- Tuesday: "Wall: footwork drills plus project"
- Wednesday: "Rest: watch one technique video, note one drill"
- Thursday: "Wall: movement on overhangs"
- Saturday: "Wall: project day"

That's two rest items and three sessions: five dates that can all be met. Giving rest an item also stops a rest night turning into a fourth session nobody planned.

## The goals above the chalk

- **Year:** Climb consistently at the next grade
- **Month:** "Footwork month: quiet feet on every warm-up"
- **Week:** "Three wall sessions, two rest-day items"

Three met weeks close a month, and six met months close the year. Because each item sits under all three, a tick earns Geniuses on top of the checklist's Kinetics. Rotate the month theme as you go, from footwork to body position to reading routes. Re-planning the focus is part of the work.

## When the week goes wrong

- **Traffic got you to the wall at 19:20.** Climb is already in **Past** on Home. Tick it there before bed for its 25 points and the streak holds.
- **You're too tired to pull hard.** Swap the night around: ask the routine chat to defer tonight's wall item to tomorrow, and tick a rest item instead.
- **You're away.** Long-press today in the week strip for a Skip Day, one of two a week. The record shows rest, and the streak survives.

A few weeks in, open **More → Progress** and watch the week's Kinetics total. If it's dropping while you're still at the wall three nights a week, the sessions have probably slid back into thirty goes at one move. Put the drill step back where it belongs.
