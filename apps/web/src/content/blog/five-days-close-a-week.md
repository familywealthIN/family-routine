Five met days close a week goal, even if you planned seven. That's the threshold rule, and the same idea runs all the way up: months close at three met weeks and years at six met months. Anything you plan beyond that is ambition, not the bar. People who plan seven days a week are often caught out the first time a week goal ticks itself on a Friday. It's not a bug. The whole cascade is built on it.

## What exactly is the threshold rule?

A period goal completes itself once enough of the milestones under it are met:

| Goal | Closes at | Typically planned |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week | 5 met days | 7 |
| Month | 3 met weeks | 4 or 5 |
| Year | 6 met months | 12 |

That's all it needs, however many milestones sit beneath it.

## What counts as a met day?

A date counts towards a week goal once every item linked to that week on that date is ticked. Two details catch people out:

- Several items on the same date count as one day, and only once all of them are done.
- Items dated outside the week don't count towards it, even if they're linked to it.

So ask "how many dates are complete?", not "how many items did I tick?". Nine items ticked across four dates is four met days, and the week is still open.

## A worked week

One planner's routines and the week goals they feed:

| Time | Routine | Points | Feeds this week's goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:00 | Run or walk | 20 | Move every day (seven planned) |
| 07:00 | Breakfast | 5 | — |
| 08:30 | Work | 40 | Finish the report draft |
| 19:00 | Spanish | 15 | Practise every day (seven planned) |
| 21:00 | Family | 10 | — |
| 22:00 | Plan and read | 10 | Read before bed |

That's 100 points. Here's how the movement week went:

- **Monday:** a 5 km run, ticked at 06:20. Day one.
- **Tuesday:** a brisk walk, ticked on time. Day two.
- **Wednesday:** nothing.
- **Thursday:** a lunchtime walk, ticked at 21:00, long after the window shut at 06:30. Day three. It shows Missed, because that label records timing, but late ticks count.
- **Friday:** a run. Day four.
- **Saturday:** a run. Day five, and the week goal completes itself.
- **Sunday:** a run anyway. It still earns its points and keeps the Run or walk streak going.

Seven days were planned, five were met, and the goal closed. Wednesday cost nothing.

## Why is the bar lower than the plan?

A rule that needed every planned day would punish ambition. You'd only ever plan the five days you were sure of, and the goal would end up measuring caution instead of progress. With a fixed threshold you can plan seven and still finish at five. The plan is allowed to be bigger than the bar.

Skip Day fits this neatly. You get at most two a week, from a long-press on today in the week strip. Seven days minus two skips leaves exactly five. A skipped day doesn't tick anything, so it won't add a met date, but two skips still leave enough to close the week.

## The same rule, further up

**Months.** Plan four or five week milestones, and any three met weeks close the month. A bad week in the middle doesn't sink it.

**Years.** Plan twelve month milestones, and six met months close the year. That covers a slow summer, a busy December and a house move. Each met month still adds to the total, and items that reach further up the cascade earn more **Geniuses**.

## What if a week stalls at four?

It's Saturday evening and the count says four. Look back first. An item from earlier in the week that you did but never ticked can still be ticked, and it counts. What you can't do is create a new item on a past day, so the fix only works for items that already exist. That's the reason to create the whole week's items on Monday.

If the fifth day really didn't happen, let the week go and plan the next one properly. Three of the month's weeks are enough.

## What this means for planning

1. Plan the full week anyway. Seven planned days give you two spare.
2. Don't stack items on one date. Three items on Tuesday make Tuesday harder to complete, so spread them out.
3. Re-plan mid-month. If week three is obviously lost, edit the month's milestones. Improving the plan is the work.
