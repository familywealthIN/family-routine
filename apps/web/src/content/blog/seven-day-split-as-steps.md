A seven-day gym split fits neatly into one routine's steps, with one line per weekday that you tick on its day. A split is already a schedule (push on Monday, pull on Tuesday, legs on Wednesday), so the only question is where to keep it so the right line is in front of you at 18:00.

## Why one routine and not seven?

Routines repeat. The list you build is the list for every date, so a "Monday push" routine would also turn up on Tuesday and sit there unticked. One Gym routine at a fixed time, with the split inside it, avoids seven routines that are each empty six days a week.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 (the cue) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:40 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 07:00 | Breakfast & commute | 10 | Lunch box into the bag |
| 08:30 | Work | 35 | Open today's list |
| 12:30 | Lunch | 5 | Step away from the desk |
| 18:00 | Gym | 25 | Pick up the packed gym bag |
| 20:00 | Dinner & family | 10 | Lay the table |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 10 | Pack tomorrow's gym bag |

The day adds up to 100, and the gym gets a quarter of it. That's enough that skipping it costs something, without the rest of the day stopping counting.

## The split, written as steps

Open the routine from **More → Routines** and write the week into its steps, in order:

1. Pick up the packed gym bag
2. Monday: push (chest, shoulders, triceps)
3. Tuesday: pull (back, biceps)
4. Wednesday: legs
5. Thursday: mobility and a long walk
6. Friday: upper body
7. Saturday: lower body
8. Sunday: rest, and pack the bag for Monday

Step 1 stays physical, so the 18:00 reminder asks you to pick up the bag. It doesn't tell you to go and train. Each day you tick the bag and that day's line, and the checklist reads like a small calendar. Ticking inside the 17:00–18:30 window earns Discipline. Working the lines earns Kinetics. Anything extra, like ten minutes on the rower, goes in through the **+** as a quick task inside the Gym slot.

## Sessions as day items, phases as months

The steps say what *kind* of day it is. The session itself goes in a day item linked to the Gym routine. "Push: bench, press, dips" can carry one sub-task per exercise, and you tick them off between sets.

Above it sits the plan:

- **Year:** Squat body weight for five clean reps
- **Month:** Phase 2, building volume
- **Week:** Five sessions this week
- **Day:** Legs: squat, split squat, hamstrings

Five training days line up with a week that closes on its fifth met day. Three met weeks close the phase, and six met phases close the year, however many you planned. Each ticked session sits under all three, so it earns Geniuses. Switch the Gym card to the Month tab and you'll see the current phase from the gym floor.

## When Wednesday's session slips

A meeting runs on until 18:40 and leg day doesn't start on time. What you do next depends on the rest of the evening:

- **You trained at 19:30 anyway.** By then the Gym routine is in **Past** on Home. Check it in from there before midnight for its 25 points, and the streak holds. The legs item is marked Missed for its timing, but Wednesday still counts as one of the week's five.
- **You didn't train.** Ask the routine chat to defer the legs item to Thursday. Thursday's mobility moves to Sunday, and the week can still reach five.
- **You need the night off.** Long-press today in the week strip for a Skip Day. That's one of the two you get each week, and it's recorded as rest.

## This week

Rewrite the Gym steps as your split, cue first, and add one week goal with five dated session items. On Sunday, open **Goals** and look at the week. Five met days means it closed itself, and the checklist did most of the remembering.
