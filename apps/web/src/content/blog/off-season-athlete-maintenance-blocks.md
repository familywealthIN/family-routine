The off-season works best as maintenance blocks, a lighter version of the season's structure rather than none at all. When the last race ends, the daily sessions and weekly purpose tend to end with it, and athletes who drop everything spend pre-season rebuilding habits as well as fitness. A club rower can avoid that by keeping the routines and turning them down.

## How do you turn training down without deleting it?

You move points. All routines share one 100-point day budget, so lowering training frees points for something else. Here that's a course the rower has been putting off all year.

| Time | Routine | In season | Off season | Step 1 (the cue) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Wake up | 5 | 5 | Glass of water |
| 07:00 | Morning session | 30 | 15 | Kit on before the kettle |
| 08:30 | Work | 35 | 40 | Open today's list |
| 13:00 | Mobility | 5 | 5 | Mat out under the desk |
| 18:30 | Evening session | 15 | 10 | Bag into the car |
| 20:00 | Course | — | 20 | Open the next lesson |
| 22:00 | Wind down | 10 | 5 | Phone on charge in the hall |

Both columns add up to 100. In season, the 07:00 session is the most expensive routine to miss: 30 points to check in late. Off season it costs 15, so it no longer dominates the day. The course is new for this stretch and takes the 20 points that came free. Make the change in **More → Routines**, where the editor shows how much of the 100 is left as you lower one routine and raise another.

## Three blocks, one year goal

The year goal is "Ready for pre-season trials in March". The off-season goes underneath it as month milestones:

- **Weeks 1–2: Decompress.** One item a day: "Any sport, 30 easy minutes".
- **Months 2–3: Maintain.** Three structured erg sessions a week, plus two easy days.
- **Month 4: Rebuild.** Pre-season structure comes back, and so do the points.

A maintenance month still has to be earned. It closes after three met weeks, and a maintenance week closes on its fifth met day. The items shrink ("Erg, 30 minutes steady" in place of a full piece), but each week still has five of them. Maintenance means a smaller plan you keep, not no plan.

In the month calendar, a maintenance week looks the same as a race week: five filled rings ([the Goals chapter](/guide/goals)).

## Why the off-season is Geniuses work

Most of the off-season is planning. Re-weighting points, setting maintenance targets and deciding what the course is for all belong to the third pillar. Geniuses comes from ticking items linked into the cascade, and longer horizons count for more. So a 30-minute erg linked to "Ready for pre-season trials" earns more than the same erg logged on its own.

You can also edit a milestone halfway through a month. If the third week of a maintenance block turns into a family holiday, rewrite that month's milestone rather than letting it fail quietly. The year only needs six met months, and one re-planned block doesn't cost it anything.

## What slipping looks like off-season

In season, a missed session is obvious. Off season, slips creep in. The 07:00 session becomes 07:40, then 08:10, and two weeks go by before anyone notices. **More → Progress → On the clock** shows the drift: on-time against late check-ins for each routine, long before the erg scores do.

If you rowed at 08:10, the morning session is in **Past** on Home. Checking it in there before the day ends costs its 15 points and keeps the streak. For a couple of days away, long-press today in the week strip and take a Skip Day. You get two a week. A week-long holiday needs a rewritten week goal, not seven skips.

## Turning it back up

In month 4, set the routines back towards their in-season points. Lowering the course from 20 to 5 and raising the morning session back to 30 is one visit to the editor. The aim is to reach pre-season with the routine already running, so the first structured week feels like turning the volume up on something that never stopped.
