Course notes come back at the right moment when they live on the day item, and the routine that starts each session carries a project tag. Week three of an online course usually goes like this: you sit down for the morning session and can't remember where you stopped. Finding the notes takes ten minutes, re-reading them takes ten more, and a forty-minute session becomes twenty.

The notes were never lost. They just weren't attached to the moment you needed them. This is the fix, set up for someone working through a data-analysis course before work.

## Where do the notes actually go?

On the day item, not in a separate app. That's the whole habit.

Each morning has one item, "Course: lessons 4.2 and 4.3", linked to the study routine and to that week's milestone. Its notes field takes whatever you'd otherwise have written somewhere else: what the lesson covered, the formula that finally clicked, the question you couldn't answer, the point where you stopped. Notes stay attached to the day and routine they came from, and Search finds them later by their words.

## Tag every routine that serves the course

| Time | Routine | Points | Tag | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 07:00 | Course session | 15 | `project:data-course` | Laptop open at the course page |
| 12:30 | Lunch recap | 5 | `project:data-course` | Notes open on your phone |
| 20:00 | Practice | 10 | `project:data-course` | Exercise file open |

These three carry the course, and the other 70 points of the 100 go to work, exercise and home. Add the tag in each routine's editor under More → Routines.

The tag is what brings the notes back. A routine with a `project:` or `area:` tag opens its chat on Home with a **Before you start** brief, one block per tag:

- the standing commitment for the project,
- up to three **next steps**, each with an Add button that puts it on today's checklist,
- up to three rows of **past activity** from recent sessions.

## Which two questions replace re-reading?

The brief has two quick replies, and between them they cover the first ten minutes of most sessions.

![A past day opened from the week strip, with its checklist and a chat about that day offering What did I do last time?](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-past.jpg)

**What did I do last time?** answers from your own history: the project's recent activity, what you worked on and what you ticked ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) shows where the chat sits). Seeing "Lesson 4.1: joins" there, with your note on it a tap away ("stuck on left versus full joins"), gets you back into the lesson faster than a page of re-reading.

**Plan from next steps** turns the brief's next steps into today's plan. Add the ones you want and they land on the checklist, so the session starts with its decision made.

## Steps that leave a trail

The Course session checklist makes sure each session leaves notes for the next:

1. Laptop open at the course page.
2. Ask "What did I do last time?"
3. Work through today's lessons.
4. Write three lines of notes on the day item: covered, understood, stuck.
5. Write where you stopped, as precisely as a timestamp.

Step 5 does the most. "Stopped at 12:40 in lesson 4.3, just before window functions" is the line that saves tomorrow's ten minutes. Working the steps earns Kinetics, and ticking inside the 06:00 to 07:30 window earns Discipline.

## How the course fits the cascade

The course is the goal: "Finish the data-analysis course and its capstone". Months are pairs of modules, and each week milestone is one module, such as "Module 4: joins and aggregation". Day items link to both the routine and the week, which is where Geniuses comes from, and the weight grows with the horizon. Here are the numbers: 5 met days close a module week, and 3 met weeks close the month. A missed Thursday doesn't undo a module.

## When a session slips

The session starts at 07:50, twenty minutes after its window shut, so Course session sits under **Past** on Home. Study anyway, and write the notes. Then check it in late with points, the same day. The streak holds and the 15 points are paid as usual.

The notes matter more than the timing. A late session with good notes keeps the thread. An on-time session with no notes is the one you'll repeat.

## Start tomorrow

- Give every course routine the same `project:` tag.
- Write notes on the day item and nowhere else.
- Open each session with "What did I do last time?"
