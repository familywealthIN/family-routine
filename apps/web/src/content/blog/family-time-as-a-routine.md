Family time gets a checklist because the evening slot is the first thing to go when work, email and the washing-up run over. Five plain steps starting at 18:30 protect the slot where an evening together can actually happen. The routine doesn't create the warmth. It keeps the space for it.

## Isn't scheduling your own family a bit cold?

It can feel that way, but think about what happens without a slot. Family time becomes whatever is left once the jobs are done, and most nights that's twenty minutes of everyone on separate screens. A start time and a short list don't make the evening formal. They stop it being the leftover.

## The routine, step by step

For one working parent the slot starts at 18:30, right after the commute. The steps run in order:

1. Clear the kitchen counter
2. Cook together, with one child helping
3. Eat at the table
4. One shared activity: a card game, a walk, reading aloud
5. Phones in the basket by the door

Step 1 is deliberately tiny and physical. It takes two minutes, you can't do it from the sofa, and once the counter is clear, cooking is the obvious next thing. A step should be something you can see happen, not a feeling like "be present".

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 07:00 | Breakfast and bags | 10 | Bowls on the table |
| 09:00 | Work | 40 | Close the email tab |
| 13:00 | Lunch walk | 10 | Shoes on |
| 18:30 | Family time | 25 | Clear the kitchen counter |
| 21:00 | Plan tomorrow | 5 | Open tomorrow's list |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 5 | Phone on charge downstairs |

Family time takes 25 of the 100 points, second only to work's 40. That's a deliberate choice: when the evening disappears, the day's score drops enough for you to notice.

## Where does Kinetics come in?

**Discipline** only asks whether the routine started inside its window, which here runs from 17:30 to 19:00. That matters, but you can tick it on time and then scroll all evening.

**Kinetics** is what measures follow-through. It builds up as you tick steps during the slot, plus any quick task added with the **+** beside the chat box ("Find the torch for the walk"). An evening where you cooked but skipped the activity gets a partial score. That isn't a judgement, just an accurate one.

Five steps is about the right number. Ten feels like a chore chart. Two can't tell a real evening from a rushed one. To change them, open **More → Routines**, tap Family time on the timeline, and edit its steps.

## Keeping the evening from going stale

The same activity every night goes flat within a month, so the year goal is kept broad ("A shared activity on most evenings this year") and each month gets a theme:

- **October:** card and board games
- **November:** one new recipe a week, cooked together
- **December:** a torch walk after dinner, whatever the weather
- **January:** a chapter book read aloud

Each week milestone reads "Five evenings with an activity". Day items such as "Activity: teach the kids rummy" are linked to the Family time routine.

![The focused routine card switched to its Week tab, showing the week goal and its linked day goals](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-cascade.jpg)

Switch the routine card from Today to Week and this week's activities are listed under the milestone ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) explains the four tabs). Five evenings with an activity close the week. Three such weeks close the month's theme. Six themed months close the year, so a hectic December doesn't sink it.

## What if the evening slips?

A late train gets you in at 19:20, twenty minutes after the window shut. Family time drops into **Past**. Have the evening anyway, then check it in late from Past with points. The streak holds and the tick counts in full. The day's activity item can be ticked late too. It will show Missed, because that status records timing, but it still counts towards the five.

A school play or a family party is an evening together in a different shape. Tick the steps that happened and leave the rest. **Skip Day**, two a week from a long-press on today in the week strip, is for evenings when nothing on the list will happen at all.

## One thing to try this week

Write five steps for your own evening, make step 1 something you can physically touch, and give the routine at least 20 points. After seven days, open **More → Progress** and see which steps actually got ticked. The missing ones tell you what to change next week.
