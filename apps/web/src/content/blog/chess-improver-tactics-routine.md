A chess improver's training split works best as fifteen puzzles in the morning and one reviewed game at night, with a short endgame drill in between. The classic adult plateau comes from playing lots of games and reviewing none, which mostly rehearses the mistakes you already make.

Puzzles build pattern recognition, games give practice, and reviews do the learning. The review is the part most people skip, which is exactly why it belongs on a checklist.

## The split, as three routines

These carry the chess. The other 70 of the day's 100 points go to work, exercise and the household.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 07:10 | Tactics | 10 | Board app open on today's set |
| 12:30 | Endgame drill | 5 | Endgame positions open |
| 21:00 | Game & review | 15 | Phone on silent, one game only |

Tactics is short and the same every day. The lunchtime drill takes ten minutes on whatever endgame the month is about. The evening routine carries the most points because it holds the hard part. Set all three up in More → Routines.

## Why put the review on the checklist?

Because the review is the training. Here are both checklists.

**Tactics, 07:10:**

1. Board app open on today's set.
2. Fifteen puzzles, no hints.
3. Revisit the misses slowly.
4. Write each miss's theme in one word: fork, pin, back rank.

**Game & review, 21:00:**

1. Phone on silent, one game only.
2. Play one rapid game at a time control long enough to think.
3. Review it straight away: find the move where the evaluation dropped.
4. Write down why you played it: missed a threat, rushed, didn't know the plan.
5. Add the pattern to the month's list.

Kinetics comes from working those steps, and it rewards exactly what you're trying to build. The review sits on the checklist beside the game, not as an optional extra. "One game only" is there too, so 21:00 can't turn into five blitz games and no review.

## A rating goal, broken into themes

A rating makes a fine year goal because you can't fake it: "Reach a 1600 rapid rating". It's a poor thing to plan around, though, because it moves for reasons you don't control. So the months get themes you can control:

| Month | Theme | Week milestone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Checks, captures, threats | Five days of tactics, three reviewed games |
| 2 | King and pawn endgames | Five days of endgame drills |
| 3 | One opening as White | Five days on the main line, two games in it |
| 4 | Converting won positions | Three reviewed games where you were winning |
| 5 | Defending worse positions | Three reviewed losses |
| 6 | Time management | Five games with no rushed middle-game moves |

![Goals switched to the week, with this week's goals grouped by routine and their progress](/screenshots/guide/goals-week.jpg)

The week view on Goals shows month 2's drill goal next to the tactics goal, each with its progress ([the Goals chapter](/guide/goals) covers all the views). Day items such as "Opposition, 10 positions" link to the drill routine and to the week, so each tick earns Geniuses as well. Six themes done properly close the year, because it completes at 6 met months.

The rating will move or it won't. The themes are what you steer.

## When the game starts late

A late dinner pushes the game to 21:50, twenty minutes after its 21:30 close, and Game & review moves to **Past** on Home. Play and review, then use points that evening to check it in late. The streak continues and the 15 points land as normal.

One rule worth keeping: **if you played but didn't review, don't check it in.** A game without a review is just a game. If evenings keep slipping, move the routine to 20:30 and keep the morning tactics going whatever happens. Do the sum: with 7 tactics mornings planned, 5 met mornings still close the week.

## Reading a plateau honestly

After a month, read the one-word themes from Tactics. If "back rank" appears eleven times, that's next month's theme, whatever the plan said. Editing a milestone to match the evidence is Geniuses work: improving the plan rather than following a stale one.
