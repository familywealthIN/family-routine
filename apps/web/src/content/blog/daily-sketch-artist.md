A hobby artist's first month of daily sketching comes down to twenty minutes at the same time every day, and whether the drawings are any good doesn't enter into it. Every session tends to start with "will this be any good?", and on most days the honest answer is no. Routine Notes doesn't ask. It only records whether you sat down at 07:00 and drew for twenty minutes.

## The day, with the sketch in it

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:40 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 07:00 | Sketch | 15 | Sketchbook open, pencil on the page |
| 08:00 | Get ready | 5 | Clothes laid out |
| 09:00 | Work | 45 | Open today's list |
| 12:30 | Lunch walk | 5 | Pocket sketchbook in |
| 18:30 | Dinner | 10 | Clear the table |
| 21:30 | Sketchbook review | 10 | Turn to today's page |
| 22:30 | Night routine | 5 | Pencils sharpened for the morning |

The night routine's first step sets up the morning: sharpened pencils and a book left open on the table. By 07:00, step 1 is half done.

Sketch has five small steps:

1. Sketchbook open, pencil on the page.
2. Twenty-minute timer.
3. Draw what's in front of you: a mug, a hand, the window.
4. Date the page.
5. Close the book without judging it.

Step 5 is a real step. It stops the critique from happening at 07:20, when you should be getting ready. Critique gets its own routine at 21:30, when you're less attached to the drawing.

## Days 1–10: is 07:00 real, or a wish?

The first ten days do one job: test the time. The window opens at 06:00 and closes at 07:30, so any start in that ninety minutes counts as on time, which is Discipline.

Watch when you actually tick. If it's always after 07:20, the routine wants a later start. Change it now in More → Routines, while it costs nothing. A routine set to the time you really sketch earns more than one set to the time you think you ought to.

## Days 11–20: let the checklist work

With the time settled, look at the steps. Kinetics comes from working them, and the one most often skipped is step 4, dating the page. Keep it. A month from now, dated pages are the only honest record of whether you improved.

![Home on a phone, with a routine in progress, its checklist with one item ticked, Add task, and the Today, Week, Month and Year tabs](/screenshots/home-mobile.png)

On a phone, the Sketch card shows the five steps with ticks as you go ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) explains each part). The lunch walk carries a pocket sketchbook. That isn't a second session, just permission to draw a bench. If you do, tap **+** and add "Bench, 5 minutes", and it lands in the lunch routine.

## Days 21–30: give the pages a direction

Now add Geniuses. A month milestone gives the month a subject: "Hands: 20 studies". The week milestone is "Five mornings of hands", and the day item is "Hand study, 20 minutes", linked to Sketch. Each ticked item moves the week, the month and the year. The year goal, "Fill four sketchbooks", closes at 6 met months.

The arithmetic for week four: 7 mornings planned, 5 needed. A rushed Saturday is covered.

That's the loop the month is built on: turn up (D), do the steps (K), then point them somewhere (G).

## The morning that slips

The alarm fails and you sketch at 07:50, twenty minutes past the close. Sketch sits under **Past** on Home. Draw anyway, then spend points to check it in late before the day ends. The streak holds, and the tick pays its 15. The day item reads Missed, since status records timing, and it still counts toward the week.

If you didn't draw, leave the day alone. If it was planned rest, long-press today in the week strip for a Skip Day: two a week, no cost to the streak. Don't buy a tick for a page that doesn't exist.

## At day 30

Open More → Progress and read *On the clock*: how many of the 30 sketches were on time, and how many late. Then turn the pages. The drawings will be uneven, and there will be thirty of them, which is the point of the month.

## Tomorrow morning

- Leave the sketchbook open on the table tonight.
- Set Sketch to the time you'll really sit down.
- Date every page, and close the book without judging it.
