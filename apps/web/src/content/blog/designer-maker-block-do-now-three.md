A product designer can protect the morning maker block by reading the Priority matrix three times a day, at 08:30, 13:00 and 17:00. The requests keep coming either way: a critique, a quick look at a screenshot, a ticket that needs a mock-up today. What changes is that you can see how much of the day has already been claimed before the morning goes.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:50 | Wake up | 5 | Blinds up |
| 07:30 | Sketchbook | 5 | Pencil on the page |
| 08:30 | Triage | 10 | Open Priority |
| 09:00 | Maker block | 35 | Close chat, open the file |
| 13:00 | Lunch walk | 5 | Shoes on |
| 14:00 | Critiques and meetings | 20 | Open the review list |
| 17:00 | Close the loop | 10 | Read Priority again |
| 21:30 | Night routine | 10 | Lights down |

Two of the eight routines, Triage and Close the loop, exist only to read the matrix. Between them they get 20 of the 100 points. The maker block gets 35, because that's where the year goal moves.

## How does the matrix know where things go?

You never choose a quadrant. How a task is created decides where it lands:

- **Do now:** started today.
- **Plan:** scheduled for a future day.
- **Delegate:** an @mention of the person who owns it.
- **Automate:** started with an agent.

Change how an item is set up and it moves. Give it next Tuesday's date and it goes to Plan. Mention a developer and it goes to Delegate.

## 08:30: the morning read

![Priority on an iPad: the triage card and today's Do, Plan, Delegate and Automate counts beside the matrix](/screenshots/guide/priority-counts.jpg)

The four counts show the day's shape before you open a single item ([the Priority chapter](/guide/priority) covers each one). The rule is three or fewer in Do now. Five before 09:00 means yesterday's procrastination is now running today. "Mock up the empty state for search" isn't needed until Thursday, so it gets Thursday's date and moves to Plan. Two taps, and the maker block gets its morning back.

Triage earns its 10 points through Discipline, by being ticked between 07:30 and 09:00. The matrix only puts the work in order. Doing the work is what earns the rest.

## 13:00: the midday read

The maker block is over, and four new requests have arrived. During the block, the designer typed each one into the add box without acting on it. The midday read decides which have to happen this afternoon and which can go to someone else. A request for production assets becomes "@frontend export the icon set from the library file" and moves to Delegate, leaving the Do now count at two.

## 17:00: the evening read

By 17:00 the quadrants show what happened. Ticked items are struck through, and you can tick them straight from the matrix. Anything still open in Do now either gets tomorrow's date or gets admitted to Plan. Close the loop can be ticked on time from 16:00 to 17:30, which allows for the 16:00 critique running over.

## Where the plan goes

Plan isn't a dumping ground. Next week's milestones are built from it. The year goal is to ship the redesigned mobile app. This month's milestone is getting the onboarding designs signed off. The week milestone is getting the onboarding prototype tested, with day items such as "Run the second test session" linked to the maker block, where they earn Geniuses as they're ticked. This week needs five of its days met. The month needs three weeks, and the year needs six of its months.

## What happens when the block slips?

A stakeholder moves a review to 09:00, and the maker block's window closes at 09:30 before the file is even open.

- **The block happened from 11:00.** Spend its 35 points from **Past** that afternoon to check it in. The streak survives and the tick counts in full.
- **The morning really was lost.** Leave it unticked. The evening read will show why: a Do now column full of other people's urgent work.

## Try it for a week

Add a 10-point Triage routine under More → Routines, just before your best block, with "Open Priority" as step 1. Keep the three-item rule for five working days and compare Friday's Do now count with Monday's.
