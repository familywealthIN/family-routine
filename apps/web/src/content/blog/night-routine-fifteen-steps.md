A night routine with fifteen steps is usually three routines in one, and splitting it is the fix. Order each new routine's steps by cue, rename them for what they do, and the evening starts ticking on time again. Night routines grow by accretion: pack the gym bag, clear the email, check tomorrow, floss, stretch, read. Each addition made sense. Six months later the routine is ticked at midnight, if at all, and half of it is skipped.

## What goes wrong with a fifteen-step routine?

The symptoms are consistent:

- It's the routine that most often ends up in **Past**.
- On the nights it does get ticked, the window has long since closed.
- The same few steps, usually in the middle of the list, go unticked every night.
- It starts with something like "screens off", which is the hardest thing to do all evening.

![A past day opened from the week strip, with that day's checklist and a chat about the day beside it](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-past.jpg)

To confirm it, tap a few past days in the week strip on Home and read each day's checklist ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) shows how past days open). The unticked steps cluster in the same places night after night.

## The diagnosis: one routine doing three jobs

Group the fifteen steps and three jobs usually appear:

1. **Closing the house.** Dishes, wiping the counters, packing the work bag and the gym bag, laying out clothes.
2. **Planning tomorrow.** Checking tomorrow's list, clearing the inbox, writing down tomorrow's first step.
3. **Getting to bed.** Screens off, brushing teeth, flossing, skincare, a short stretch, reading, lights out.

Each of these happens at a different time, needs a different amount of energy, and starts from a different cue. One routine means one start time for all three, and neither its window nor you can stretch that far.

## The fix: three routines, three cues

Open **More → Routines**, cut the old routine down to the first job, and add two more after it.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Wake up | 10 | Glass of water |
| 07:00 | Exercise | 15 | Shoes on |
| 08:30 | Work | 40 | Notebook open |
| 21:00 | Close the house | 10 | Fill the sink |
| 21:40 | Plan tomorrow | 10 | Open tomorrow's list |
| 22:20 | Lights down | 15 | Phone on charge outside the bedroom |

That's 100. The old night routine had 35 points, and now those 35 are spread across three routines of 10, 10 and 15.

The three windows run from 20:00 to 21:30, 20:40 to 22:10, and 21:20 to 22:50, so they overlap. Finish the kitchen at 21:25 and you're already in the planning window. Finish planning and you're in the bedtime one.

## Order steps by cue, not by importance

In each routine, step 1 is the smallest physical action that gets it started. "Fill the sink" begins the washing-up without a decision. "Phone on charge outside the bedroom" turns screens-off from the hardest step into a side effect of where the phone lives.

Then order the rest so each step sets up the next. In Lights down, teeth come before skincare because you're already at the sink, and reading comes last because the book is by the bed.

Cap each routine at five or six steps. That's the real gain for **Kinetics**, which builds as you work through the checklist in the slot. Six steps you finish outscore fifteen you give up on at step eight.

## Renaming is part of the fix

Give each rebuilt routine a name that says what it does. "Night routine" tells you nothing. "Close the house" and "Lights down" are instructions, and a reminder that leads with step 1 reads naturally under them.

Rebuilding a routine mid-season is **Geniuses** work: improving the plan instead of pushing harder against a bad one. That completes the loop. You show up for each routine on time, work its short list, then improve the plan, and the next evening starts from a better one.

## Keeping the goal link

If the evening fed a goal, link it to the new routine. "Lights down by 22:50 on five nights" goes under an October milestone and a year goal of evenings that end by 23:00, with each day's item linked to Lights down. Five good nights close the week, three good weeks close October, and six good months close the year.

## While you're adjusting

For the first week or two some nights will run late. If you closed the house at 22:00 rather than 21:00, open it from **Past** and spend points to check it in. A night out where none of it happens can just stay in Past. Keep **Skip Day**, two a week from a long-press on today, for days when the whole list is off.
