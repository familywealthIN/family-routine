An accountant facing twelve weeks of busy season should cut the routine list to five routines, then rebuild the full set on purpose once it's over. For three months of the year the calendar belongs to the filing deadline. Twelve-hour days, stacks of client files, and a normal routine set that by the second week has become a list of daily failures. Reading, the gym, the evening class: none of it happens, and every unticked routine makes the next one easier to skip.

## Which routines survive the season?

Two weeks before the season starts, go through the normal list and ask one question about each routine: would I miss this in March? Five pass. The rest are paused for the season. Write down their steps before you delete them under More → Routines, so rebuilding them takes minutes, not an evening.

Then re-weight. The budget is still 100, but now only five routines share it.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:00 | Wake up and walk | 10 | Shoes on |
| 07:00 | Breakfast | 5 | Oats on |
| 07:40 | Client work | 50 | Open the deadline tracker |
| 18:30 | Dinner at home | 15 | Laptop stays at the office |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 20 | Phone charging in the kitchen |

Client work is at 50, the most a single routine can have. That's an honest description of the season. The Night routine at 20 is the less obvious choice. Over a season this long, each night sets up the next morning, so it gets the points and the steps to match: phone in the kitchen, tomorrow's first file written down, lights out by 22:40.

## During the season: five windows, defended

With only five routines, every window counts for more. Client work at 07:40 can be ticked on time from 06:40 to 08:10, which covers early-train days as well as normal ones.

This plan runs mostly on Discipline. Kinetics comes from Client work's four steps (tracker open, the day's three files named, the hardest one first, an end-of-day status note) and from quick tasks added during the day, such as "Chase the missing paperwork from the garage by noon". Geniuses comes from a cascade built backwards from the deadline:

- **Year:** every client deadline met, all year.
- **Months:** January, records in from everyone. February, first drafts. March, everything submitted. The quieter months get lighter milestones.
- **Week:** close out a file every working day.
- **Days:** one named client file, linked to Client work.

![Goals switched to the week view, listing this week's goals by routine with their progress](/screenshots/guide/goals-week.jpg)

The week view shows each file's progress against its routine ([the Goals chapter](/guide/goals) walks through the views). A five-day working week can meet five days. A month needs three good weeks, so a week lost to a system outage doesn't sink February. The year needs six good months, and busy season can supply at most three of them, so the quieter months have to supply the rest.

## What a slipped day looks like in March

The office empties at 21:30. Dinner at home didn't happen, so leave it unticked. A late check-in is for something you did late. It isn't a way to cover up a dinner that never happened. The Night routine happened at 23:10, after its window closed at 22:30, so spend its 20 points from **Past** to check it in that night. The streak holds, and the tick counts in full.

On the one Sunday in the season with nothing planned, long-press today in the week strip and take a Skip Day. It counts as rest, so the streak survives. There are two a week, and in this season you'll want them.

If a file was closed on Thursday but only ticked on Friday morning, it still counts. It shows as Missed, because that status records timing, but the week still moves.

## After the season: rebuild on purpose

The deadline passes. The worst thing you can do is bring back every paused routine on the same Monday, which recreates the second week of busy season in reverse.

Bring them back one at a time, about every three days, using the steps you wrote down. Each time one returns, move ten points from Client work to it, so Client work goes 50, 40, 30. The editor shows how much of the 100 is left, so the arithmetic is easy. Two weeks later, More → Progress → On the clock will show whether the restored routines are actually being kept.

Adjusting the plan to the season is Geniuses work. The plan that suits March isn't the plan that suits May, and admitting that is what makes both of them work.

## This year

Add a day item for the week before busy season called "Decide the five". Make the list before the deadline makes it for you.
