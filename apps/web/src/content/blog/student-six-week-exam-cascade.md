A six-week revision cascade survives a bad exam-season day because each week only needs five met days, not seven. Here's a revision plan for a student sitting three subjects, set up so that one disastrous Wednesday costs nothing.

## The revision day

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 07:00 | Wake & breakfast | 5 | Curtains open, glass of water |
| 08:00 | Revision block 1 | 25 | Phone in another room |
| 10:30 | Revision block 2 | 20 | Fresh page, topic at the top |
| 13:30 | Past papers | 20 | Timer set, paper face-down |
| 16:00 | Exercise | 10 | Trainers on |
| 19:00 | Light review | 10 | Flashcards out |
| 22:00 | Sleep routine | 10 | Screens off, bag packed |

The three study routines carry 65 points, because for six weeks they're what matters. Exercise and sleep still get 20 between them. A student running on four hours' sleep in week five has a planning problem, not a commitment problem.

Each revision block runs the same steps: phone away, read the topic, close the book and write down everything you remember, check against the notes, then ten questions. Past papers has its own: timer, paper, mark it, list the dropped marks.

## How is the cascade built?

At the top is the **year goal**: finish the year with the results the next step needs. Under it sit two **month milestones**: "Revision cycle: every topic once" for weeks 1 to 3, and "Exam practice: timed papers" for weeks 4 to 6, with the first exam date as its deadline.

Each week then gets **one week goal per subject**. A typical week holds "Chemistry: six mechanisms from memory", "History: two essay plans per topic" and "Maths: one full paper, marked". Day items sit under these and link to whichever study routine they happen in.

![Goals switched to the week view, listing this week's goals grouped by routine with their progress](/screenshots/guide/goals-week.jpg)

Goals → Week lists all three subject goals with their progress, so a lagging subject is obvious by Wednesday ([the Goals chapter](/guide/goals) shows the other views). Because day items link up to week, month and year, ticking them earns Geniuses on top of Discipline for being on time and Kinetics for working the steps.

## Week by week

**Week 1: find the gaps.** One past paper per subject. List the topics where you lost marks. That list is the plan for weeks 2 and 3, and topics you already know stay off it.

**Weeks 2 and 3: rotate the topics.** Each morning block takes one topic from the gap list, and the afternoon paper is one section, not a whole paper. By Friday of week 3, every topic has had a session.

**Week 4: timed papers.** Afternoons become full timed papers, and mornings go to yesterday's wrong answers.

**Week 5: re-plan the weak spots.** This is Geniuses work. Read the marks, then edit the week milestones mid-week: drop the topic that's now solid and give its days to the one that isn't. A changed plan isn't a failed one.

**Week 6: light review and logistics.** Shorter blocks, flashcards, one final paper per subject, and day items for exam-day details: room, time, what to bring.

## What does one wrecked Wednesday cost?

Do the sums. Chemistry is planned for 7 days. Wednesday collapses, so 6 days are met, and the week needs 5: it closes. A month needs 3 met weeks, and a calendar month holds four or five, so one rough week fits there too. Late ticks count. Finish the morning topic at lunchtime and tick it then. It reads Missed, because status is about timing, and it still moves the week. The one thing you can't do is add an item to a day that's already gone, so plan tomorrow's items tonight.

## Rest days and slipped mornings

Plan one rest day a week. When it arrives, long-press today in the week strip for a Skip Day. It doesn't count as failure and it doesn't cost the streak, and with a cap of two a week you can't skip your way through revision.

Oversleep, and the 08:00 block starts at 08:45, after its 08:30 close, so it moves to **Past** on Home. Do the block, then spend points to check it in late that day. The streak holds, and the tick earns its points.

## Tonight

- Create the seven routines at times you'll really keep.
- Write the year goal and the two month milestones, with the exam date as a deadline.
- One paper per subject this week. Let the gaps decide the rest.
