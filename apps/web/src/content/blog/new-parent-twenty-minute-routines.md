A new parent can still run a day built from twenty-minute routines, as long as each one is short enough to survive a feed that runs long or a nap that ends early. The plan below uses seven of them, each with two or three steps, and treats late check-ins and Skip Day as part of the design rather than as failures.

## Why twenty minutes?

Anything longer than half an hour is a gamble with a baby in the house. Twenty minutes is about the longest stretch you can count on most days, and it fits comfortably inside a routine's punctuality window, which opens an hour before the start time and closes thirty minutes after.

That spread is more generous than it sounds. The 10:00 walk counts as on time if you're out at 09:20 because the baby went down early, or at 10:25 because they didn't. A long checklist is a promise you won't keep this month, so each routine gets two or three steps at most.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Morning reset | 10 | Open the curtains |
| 08:00 | Breakfast sitting down | 10 | Fill the kettle |
| 10:00 | Fresh-air walk | 15 | Shoes on, pram by the door |
| 13:00 | Nap-time reset | 10 | Clear one surface |
| 14:00 | Twenty minutes for me | 20 | Phone in another room |
| 17:30 | Tidy and prep | 15 | Load the dishwasher |
| 20:30 | Wind-down | 20 | Set out tomorrow's clothes |

The seven routines use the whole 100-point budget. The heaviest weights, 20 each, go on the two that vanish first: the time that is yours, and the wind-down. Looking after yourself is not the optional part.

## How the three scores behave in the early months

**Discipline** is the score to watch and the one to forgive. A good day might be six of seven routines ticked inside their windows. A bad one might be two. Both are normal for now.

**Kinetics** stays honest because the lists are short. "Shoes on, pram by the door" followed by "Once round the park" is the whole walk. You don't need a third step to prove anything.

**Geniuses** can wait. Leave the goal cascade alone for the first few weeks, then add one year goal and let it steer one routine.

## What happens when a morning slips after a broken night?

Some days the 06:30 reset doesn't happen until 08:15, long after its window shut at 07:00. It moves to **Past** on Home. Later that same day you can open it there and spend points to check it in late. The streak survives and the tick counts in full. Points settle overnight, so you're paying with earlier days' earnings, and the 300 welcome points from onboarding cover the first rough weeks.

Some days are worse than late. If the whole day goes to a poorly baby or a house full of visitors, long-press today in the week strip and choose **Skip Day**. It records a rest day, keeps every streak, and with a newborn you should expect to use both of the week's two.

A useful rule: if it will still happen today, check it in late. If the day is gone, skip it. Either way the record stays truthful, which is what you'll want when you look back at what worked.

## One goal, when you're ready

Pick the routine that already happens most often, usually the walk, and give it somewhere to go. "Walk 5 km in one go by summer" becomes a March milestone of walking most days, then a week milestone of five walks. Each day's walk item is linked to the Fresh-air walk routine, so it shows on that routine's card.

Five walks finish the week, which means two lost days are already allowed for. Three walking weeks finish March. Six good months finish the year, whatever you planned. That slack is the point, and with a baby it's exactly what you need.

## The first four weeks

- **Week 1:** Open **More → Routines** and enter the seven routines. Don't link any goals. Tick what you can.
- **Week 2:** Check **Upcoming** and **Past** each evening. If one routine always lands in Past, tap it in the timeline and move its start time.
- **Week 3:** Under **More → Notifications**, turn on End of day progress for a time you're usually sitting down.
- **Week 4:** Add the one year goal, linked to the routine you hit most often.
