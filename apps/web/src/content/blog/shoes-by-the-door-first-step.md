Leaving your shoes by the door is the first step that gets a new jogger out, because it takes away the decision that kills most new running habits. If you decide every morning whether today is a running day, some mornings the answer will be no. Put the shoes out the night before, put them on in the morning, and the jog has already half started.

## Before day one: five routines and one cue

A new jogger doesn't need eleven time boxes. They need one jog that actually happens and a few routines around it that make that easier.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 (the cue) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Wake up | 10 | Open the curtains |
| 06:50 | Jog | 25 | Put on the shoes by the door |
| 07:40 | Breakfast | 10 | Kettle on |
| 09:00 | Work | 40 | Open today's list |
| 21:30 | Evening reset | 15 | Put tomorrow's shoes by the door |

The points add up to the day's 100. The loop runs between the last row and the second: the 21:30 reset sets out the cue the 06:50 jog depends on.

New routines come with suggested steps you can rewrite. For the jog, keep them plain: put on the shoes, walk for five minutes, jog a minute and walk a minute eight times, walk home, note how it felt. Every reminder leads with step 1, so at 06:50 your phone asks you to put your shoes on. It doesn't say it's time for a jog.

Those five steps are the checklist on the focused card, ticked one at a time as you go ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard)).

## Days 1–7: why start on Alarm?

The jog's window opens at 05:50 and closes at 07:20. For the first week, the only target is one tick inside those ninety minutes, and that's Discipline.

Open the routine from **More → Routines** and set its reminder to **Alarm**. It rings at 06:50 on the dot and again at 06:55 if the jog isn't ticked. The second ring is for the mornings you end up sitting on the edge of the bed. Don't add goals yet. Day 3 and day 7 each bring a streak celebration, which is about the right amount of fuss for week one.

## Days 8–14: the first slip

From week two, tick each step as you finish it. The checklist decides the session, so there's nothing to haggle over, and working through it earns Kinetics. Once you're reliably out by about 06:45, switch the reminder to **Notify**, which nudges you around the time you usually go.

This is also usually when the first slip happens. You oversleep, 07:20 comes and goes, and you jog at 12:30 instead. The jog is now sitting in **Past** on Home. Tick it from there before bed and it costs the routine's 25 points. The 300 welcome points from onboarding cover that many times over, and the streak carries on. If there was no jog at all, leave it unticked. A real gap in week two teaches you more than a tick you didn't earn.

## Days 15–21: give it somewhere to go

Now the jog can feed a goal, kept small:

- **Year:** Run 5K without walking
- **Month:** Run-walk 3K comfortably
- **Week:** Five short sessions
- **Day:** "Run-walk, 8 × 1 minute", linked to the Jog

Put five dated sessions under the week. It closes on its fifth met day, so a five-session plan can close it without any heroics. Each session item sits on the Jog card under the Week tab, and ticking it now earns Geniuses as well, because it belongs to something longer than one morning. Day 14 and day 21 each bring another streak celebration.

## What should you check after three weeks?

Open **More → Progress** and look at *On the clock*, which compares on-time and late check-ins for each routine. If the jog has been late on 6 of 21 days, don't argue with yourself. Move it to 07:10 and let the window move with it. The plan should fit the mornings you actually have, and changing it is part of the work.
