Spend points on a late check-in when you did the routine and something depends on it. Let the day go when you didn't do it. That's the short answer, and three questions make it quick to apply.

Some people guard their balance like savings. They miss a window by ten minutes, look at the routine in **Past**, and leave it there to protect the number. Others redeem everything without a thought. Neither habit is quite right.

## How does the balance actually work?

Doing your routines earns up to 100 points each of Discipline, Kinetics and Geniuses a day, so 300 in all. Today's earnings become spendable only after they settle overnight, so anything you spend today comes out of earlier days. You also start with 300 welcome points after onboarding.

Each routine's window opens an hour before its start time and shuts thirty minutes after. Miss it and the routine moves to Past on Home, where for the rest of that day you can spend points to check it in late.

## What a late check-in buys

- **The streak.** The routine's run of days continues.
- **Its agents.** An agent bound to the routine starts just as it would for an on-time tick.
- **A full tick.** It earns its points like any other, and they settle overnight with the rest.
- **Goal progress.** Day items ticked late still count towards the week, month and year, even though they show Missed.

![A finished agent attached to a 15:00 routine, with every lifecycle step done and its success and failure counts](/screenshots/guide/agents-lifecycle.jpg)

That agent point is often the deciding one, because an agent's run completes on a late check-in too ([the Agents chapter](/guide/agents) shows the lifecycle).

## What it costs

A late check-in costs the routine's own points:

| Time | Routine | Points | Late check-in costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05:50 | Wake up | 5 | 5 |
| 06:30 | Run | 15 | 15 |
| 08:00 | Plan the day | 5 | 5 |
| 08:30 | Deep work | 35 | 35 |
| 13:00 | Lunch walk | 10 | 10 |
| 19:00 | Writing | 15 | 15 |
| 21:00 | Family time | 10 | 10 |
| 22:30 | Night routine | 5 | 5 |

That's 100 points across the day. Checking in the 5-point wake-up late is cheap. Checking in the 35-point deep-work block is a real decision. The cost grows with how much the routine matters, which is fair.

## Three questions before you spend

**1. Did you actually do it?** A late check-in records something that happened late, like the run you did at 08:00 when it was due at 06:30. It's not a way to make a skipped routine look done. If you didn't do it, leave it. An honest gap in the record is worth more than a fake tick.

**2. Does something depend on it?** An agent bound to the routine, a webhook that logs the session, or a day item that would complete the week. Say Thursday's "Write 500 words" is the fifth evening for the week milestone "Write on five evenings". Checking in Writing late closes that week.

**3. Is this a one-off, or a pattern?** If the same routine needs a late check-in every day, you're not unlucky. The schedule is wrong.

## When to let the day go

- You didn't do the routine, and you won't today.
- The whole day has fallen apart. **Skip Day** is the better tool for that: long-press today in the week strip. It's a rest day that breaks no streak, and you get two a week.
- Your balance is low and the routine has no agent and no goal item. Keep the points for one that does.

## What if the same routine keeps slipping?

The 05:50 wake-up is the usual culprit, because almost nobody opens an app before the kettle boils. Rather than paying 5 points for it every day, fix the plan. Under **More → Routines**, move its start to 06:10, make the tick its first step, or switch its reminder to **Alarm**, which rings at exactly 05:50 and again five minutes later.

Once a week, open **More → Progress** and look at *On the clock*, which shows on-time and late check-ins for each routine. Wherever most check-ins are late is where your points are going. This is the loop working as it should: a slipped routine shows up, you change the plan, and the next week's balance goes further.

A large balance isn't the aim. The aim is a schedule that rarely needs it.
