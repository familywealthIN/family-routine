Kinetics without busywork means writing steps that are actions your hands can do, not labels for a topic. If you can tick a step without doing anything, it's a label, and a checklist full of labels earns points that don't mean anything.

Over-planners show the pattern clearly. Discipline is fine because they're always on time. Geniuses is fine because the cascade is beautifully drawn. Kinetics is the problem: twelve steps per routine, half of them ticked out of guilt. Kinetics is the pillar for acting with full dedication, and it pays for working the checklist and the quick tasks you finish inside the slot. Here's how to make it mean something again.

## What goes wrong when steps are labels?

A label names a topic, and an action names something you physically do. Compare two versions of one morning routine:

| Label steps (before) | Action steps (after) |
| --- | --- |
| Mindset | Glass of water, drunk standing up |
| Health | Ten squats by the bed |
| Learning | Book open at the bookmark |
| Productivity | Today's list open, top three starred |
| Reflection | One line in the day item's notes |

"Mindset" can be ticked without doing a thing. "Glass of water, drunk standing up" can't be ticked without the glass. The right-hand column is also easier to start, because each step contains its own first move.

**The rule:** if you can't picture your hands doing it, rewrite it.

## Why is step 1 the most important step?

It's the cue that starts the routine, so it should be tiny and physical: shoes on before a run, mat unrolled before yoga, laptop open at the right file before deep work. Reminders lead with step 1, which is why "Shoes on?" pulls you in, while "Run: 8 km tempo" makes you put it off.

New routines come with suggested standard steps in exactly this shape. Over-planners tend to swap them for something grander. Keep the small first step, and edit what comes after it.

## How many steps should a routine have?

It depends on how long the routine runs:

| Routine length | Steps that hold up |
| --- | --- |
| 10–15 minutes | 3–4 |
| 30–60 minutes | 4–6 |
| Half-day work block | 5–6, then quick tasks |

Past that, the checklist turns into a ritual. A fifteen-step night routine (brush, floss, bag, clothes, emails, review…) is really two routines: a short wind-down and a separate set-up-tomorrow. Split it in More → Routines and give each a handful of steps.

## A routine set that keeps Kinetics honest

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 07:00 | Exercise | 15 | Shoes on |
| 08:00 | Plan the day | 5 | Today's list open |
| 08:30 | Deep work | 40 | Laptop open at the file |
| 13:00 | Walk | 5 | Shoes on |
| 14:00 | Admin & calls | 15 | Inbox sorted oldest first |
| 21:00 | Wind-down | 10 | Phone on charge in the kitchen |
| 22:00 | Set up tomorrow | 5 | Bag by the door |

The 15-minute routines carry three steps each, and the two work blocks carry five or six. Everything else is a quick task.

## Where does unplanned work go?

Steps describe a routine's usual shape. Anything that comes up during the slot is a **quick task**.

![Home on desktop, showing the week strip, the focused routine card with its checklist, and the routine chat](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-day.jpg)

The checklist sits on the focused card, and the **+** beside the chat box adds a quick task to that same routine ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) walks through the card). Type it the way you'd say it, "Reply to the supplier about Friday's delivery", and it lands inside the routine you're in. Finished quick tasks count toward Kinetics too, so improvised work gets credit without bloating tomorrow's checklist.

The test: if it happens every day, it's a step. If it's today only, it's a quick task. Anything without a routine goes to the Inbox, and "Move to routine" files it from there.

## Why is Kinetics full when nothing moves?

Effort with no direction. You can max out K while the year stands still if the steps connect to nothing. The fix is in the cascade. Link the day's real work to a week milestone, so ticking "Draft section 2" during Deep work earns Geniuses as well. Kinetics measures effort and Geniuses measures direction, and over-planners usually have plenty of the first.

## A ten-minute audit

1. Open each routine in its editor.
2. Rewrite every label as an action.
3. Make step 1 something you can do in five seconds.
4. Trim each routine to the step counts above.
5. Move one-off steps into quick tasks.
