An amateur pianist's practice routine only works if its steps force the order: scales, then the eight hard bars, then one recording. Without that order, forty minutes go on playing the pieces you already like from the top, until the difficult passage arrives and you start again.

## The three practice routines

Practice runs across three routines; the other 70 of the day's 100 points go to work, meals and the household.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 07:20 | Morning scales | 5 | Lift the lid, metronome on |
| 19:40 | Practice | 20 | Music open at the marked bars |
| 21:00 | Listen back | 5 | Headphones on, last take queued |

The morning is ten minutes of scales and nothing else. It's cheap and short, and it keeps the fingers moving on days the evening collapses. The evening session does the real work, and the 21:00 listen-back is five minutes with headphones on and the night's recording playing.

## What should the evening checklist contain?

Seven steps, in the order a teacher would set them. Add them in More → Routines, under the routine's steps:

1. **Music open at the marked bars.** The cue is small and physical, and the reminder opens with it.
2. **Warm up, five minutes.** Slow, no pieces.
3. **This week's scales.** Two keys, hands separate and then together, at a set tempo.
4. **The hard eight bars.** Hands separate, at a tempo you can actually play. Then hands together.
5. **Run-through.** The whole piece once, hard bars included, no stopping.
6. **Record one take.** Phone on the music stand.
7. **Mark tomorrow's bars.** A pencil bracket over the next problem passage.

Step 7 sets up tomorrow's step 1, so the session never opens with a decision. Step 6 is the honest one: a recording hears the rushed beat and the left hand fading out, which you don't notice while you play.

Each ticked step earns Kinetics. The score doesn't care whether the run-through went well. It rewards getting to the run-through instead of looping the opening sixteen bars.

## Where do stray questions go mid-session?

Into a quick task, not your head.

![The add box open over Home, adding a quick task with a date and time to the routine in progress](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-quicktask.jpg)

Tap **+**, type "Ask about the fingering in bar 22", and it lands inside the Practice slot ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) covers the add box). If it needs your teacher, @mention them and it files itself under **Delegate** in Priority.

## What do the bars add up to?

Kinetics is the daily score. Geniuses is where the bars are heading:

- **Year goal:** a ten-minute programme of three pieces, played for friends at home.
- **Month milestone:** piece 2, first section, up to tempo.
- **Week milestone:** bars 1–32, hands together, slow.
- **Day item:** bars 17–24, hands separate, linked to Practice.

Linked, the day item appears on the Practice card under the Week tab when you sit down, and ticking it moves the week, the month and the year. Count it out: 5 met evenings close a week, 3 met weeks close a month, and 6 met months close the year. A fortnight away for a family trip doesn't sink the recital.

When a piece is harder than planned, edit the month milestone. "Up to tempo" can become "Three-quarter tempo, no stops". That's still progress, and an honest re-plan is Geniuses work.

## When practice starts late

A late dinner pushes practice to 20:30. The window for 19:40 closed at 20:10, so Practice now sits under **Past** on Home. Play anyway, then use points to check it in late before midnight. The streak survives, and the session pays out like an on-time one.

If 20:30 becomes the norm, move the routine to 20:20 and stop paying for the same slip every night. If the reminder isn't landing, switch it from Notify to Alarm in the routine's editor. Alarm rings at exactly the start time, and again five minutes later.
