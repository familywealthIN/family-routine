Twenty pages before the house wakes is a realistic daily target for an early reader with a busy home, and a 05:40 routine is what protects it. These are the questions that come up in the first month, answered with the settings that work.

## Why 05:40 and not 06:30?

Choose the time to fit the house, not the book. If the children are up around 06:15, a 06:30 reading routine is really part of breakfast. At 05:40 the window runs from 04:40 to 06:10: even a slow start leaves twenty quiet minutes, and the window closes before the first footsteps on the stairs.

## What does the whole day look like?

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05:40 | Read | 15 | Kettle on, book on the table |
| 06:30 | Family breakfast | 10 | Lay the table |
| 07:30 | Commute | 5 | Bag by the door |
| 08:30 | Work | 45 | Open today's list |
| 13:00 | Walk | 5 | Shoes on |
| 18:30 | Dinner | 5 | Clear the counter |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 15 | Tomorrow's book on the kitchen table |

The night routine is worth 15, more than you might expect, because it sets up the morning. Leaving the book on the kitchen table at 22:00 is the cue for 05:40: you come downstairs to a book, not a phone. To add both routines, go to More → Routines, tap the timeline, and set each start time in 10-minute steps.

The Read checklist:

1. Kettle on, book on the table.
2. Phone face-down on the counter.
3. 20 pages.
4. One line of notes on today's item.
5. Bookmark in.

## Where do the notes go?

On the day item. Each morning has one item, "20 pages", linked to Read. Open it and write a line in its notes: a passage worth keeping, a question, an argument you disagree with. Notes stay attached to the day and the routine they came from, so a month of reading becomes a dated trail instead of a folder you never reopen. The Search page finds any of them by the words in them.

## How does it know where I stopped?

Give the routine a tag, for example `project:reading-list`. Once a routine carries a `project:` or `area:` tag, its chat on Home opens with a **Before you start** brief: the standing commitment, up to three next steps, and recent activity on that project.

![Home on desktop with the week strip, the focused routine card and its checklist, and the routine's chat beside it](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-day.jpg)

The chat sits beside the focused card ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) has the full layout). Tap **What did I do last time?** and the answer comes from your own history, usually enough to pick the thread up before the kettle boils.

## What if a child wakes early and the reading slips?

Then it moves, and that's fine. If you read the 20 pages on the train at 07:45, the 06:10 close has passed and Read sits under **Past** on Home. Spend points to check it in late today. The streak holds, and the tick earns its points. Today's on-time ticks settle overnight and pay for tomorrow's late ones.

If you didn't read at all, don't check it in. Leave the gap, or long-press today in the week strip for a Skip Day. You get two a week, and neither touches the streak.

## How does reading feed a bigger goal?

Through the cascade. The year goal is "Read 24 books", the month milestone is "Two books", and the week milestone is "Five mornings of 20 pages". The day items link to the week.

Work it through for one month. A week closes at 5 met mornings out of 7. A month closes at 3 met weeks out of 4. The year closes at 6 met months out of 12, which leaves room for a slow summer. Every linked item earns Geniuses on top of the Discipline from ticking on time.

## Is Discipline really the point?

Mostly. Twenty pages is easy. Being at the table at 05:40 every day is not. After a month, More → Progress shows *On the clock*: on-time against late check-ins for Read. You'll see a drift there before you feel it.

## What should I do this week?

- Create Read at 05:40 and the night routine at 22:00.
- Make "Tomorrow's book on the kitchen table" the night routine's first step.
- Tag Read `project:reading-list`.
- Write one line of notes every morning, even a dull one.
