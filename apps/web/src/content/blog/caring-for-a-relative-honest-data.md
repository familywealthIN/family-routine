Caring for a relative means no two days match, so the useful aim is an honest record, not a perfect streak. Sort each day as ordinary, late or lost, and use the tool that fits. After a month, that record shows which parts of your own day you can realistically protect.

Most planners assume you own your calendar. A carer mostly doesn't. Some weeks run to plan. Others are taken over by an early phone call, a long appointment or an afternoon that goes sideways. A tool that marks every one of those days in red teaches you nothing.

## A list built for unpredictable days

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Own morning | 10 | Glass of water |
| 08:00 | Morning visit | 20 | Keys and bag |
| 10:30 | Calls and admin | 15 | Open the Inbox |
| 13:00 | A break for me | 15 | Sit down away from the phone |
| 17:00 | Evening visit | 20 | Keys and bag |
| 19:30 | Walk | 10 | Shoes on |
| 21:30 | Wind-down | 10 | Phone on charge |

The two visits carry 20 points each because they're the core of the day. The routines that are only for you, the break and the walk, carry 25 between them, so when they slip, the score shows it.

## Three kinds of day, and what to do when one goes wrong

**Ordinary.** Routines happen near their times. You tick each one inside its window, from an hour before the start to half an hour after, so anywhere from 12:00 to 13:30 for the break. Discipline builds up quietly.

**Late.** Everything happens, just later. The morning visit overruns and your break begins at 14:20, fifty minutes after its window shut. It has moved to **Past** on Home. Once you've actually sat down with the tea, open it and spend points to check it in late. The run of days holds, the tick counts in full, and anything attached to the routine still fires. This records something you did. It doesn't invent something you didn't.

**Lost.** By mid-morning the plan is clearly gone. Long-press today in the week strip and choose **Skip Day**. The day goes down as rest, with no streak broken, and you have two of these each week. If you keep wanting a third, change the routine list, not yourself.

## Who else can carry some of it?

Caring is rarely one person's job, even when it feels that way. Add a task with an @mention of a sibling or neighbour ("@sam do the Thursday shopping run") and Priority files it under Delegate.

![The four Priority quadrants, Do now, Plan, Delegate and Automate, with day goals grouped by routine](/screenshots/guide/priority-quadrants.jpg)

Over a week, that Delegate column shows how much you're really handing over, which is often less than you thought ([the Priority chapter](/guide/priority) explains how each quadrant fills). Calls that arrive at awkward moments go in through the **+** beside the chat box. "Call about Thursday's appointment tomorrow at 9am" sets its own date and time. Anything without a routine waits in the Inbox, the tray icon at the top of Home, until the 10:30 admin slot, where "Move to routine" files it.

## Goals: small, and yours

Not every routine needs a goal. The one worth linking is the one that keeps you going. "Thirty minutes a day that are mine" can become a March milestone and then a week milestone of five breaks, with each day's break item linked to the 13:00 routine.

Five breaks in seven days finish the week, so two hard days are already allowed for. Three such weeks finish March, and six good months finish the year. A break ticked hours late still counts. It shows Missed, because that label is about timing, but the goal moves on.

## Reading the record after a month

Open **More → Progress** and look at *On the clock*, which shows on-time and late check-ins for each routine. Some routines will be mostly on time, some mostly late, and some mostly skipped. That is the honest data you've been collecting.

If the evening visit is always late, move it to 17:30. If the walk keeps getting missed, put it straight after the evening visit, when you're already out with your shoes on. Each change like that makes next month's plan more realistic.

## A first step

Set up this list or your own version, with your break as a real routine rather than an afterthought. For the first week, just label each day ordinary, late or lost, and use the matching tool.
