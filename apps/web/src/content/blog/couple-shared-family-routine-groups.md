A couple can keep two routine lists in one household and still share a rhythm. Each partner runs their own account, and a group lets each of you see how the other's week is going. The questions below are the ones that come up when two adults set this up, one doing the school run and the other cooking, one training before work and the other after.

## Can two people share one routine list?

No, and that turns out to be useful. Each account has its own routines, points and goals. A group adds visibility on top. One of you opens **More → Groups**, invites the other by email, and once they accept you can each see the other's week.

The split keeps the scores honest. Your Discipline measures whether *you* turned up on time, and nobody else can tick it for you.

## What do two matching lists look like?

Share the anchors, the moments when the household moves together, and let everything else differ. Each points column below adds up to its owner's 100.

| Time | Partner one | Points | Partner two | Points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:40 | Wake up | 5 | Wake up | 5 |
| 07:00 | Run | 15 | Yoga | 10 |
| 08:00 | School run | 10 | Breakfast and lunches | 10 |
| 09:00 | Deep work | 35 | Deep work | 35 |
| 18:00 | Dinner together | 10 | Dinner together | 10 |
| 19:30 | Kids' bedtime | 10 | Kids' bedtime | 15 |
| 21:00 | Plan tomorrow | 10 | Read | 10 |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 5 | Night routine | 5 |

Four routines line up at 06:40, 18:00, 19:30 and 22:00. The 18:00 dinner window runs from 17:00 to 18:30 on both phones, so on most evenings you're each ticking the same moment from your own list.

## Why weight the same routine differently?

Points show what matters to the person carrying the routine. Partner two leads bedtime most nights, so it carries 15 on that list and 10 on the other. Partner one owns the school run, so it gets its own 10 points and doesn't appear on the second list at all.

Agree the weights out loud when you set up. Five minutes now will save a lot of arguing later about whose job the kitchen was.

## What changes once you can see each other's week?

Mostly, the tone of Sunday evening. "Did you remember to…?" turns into two people looking at the same view. A run of late bedtimes on one side and early runs on the other reads as information, not an accusation.

One ground rule helps a lot: no comments on the other person's day unless they ask. The group is there for planning and encouragement. Use it for that, not for checking up.

## What happens when one of you slips?

The other person's list doesn't change. If a lost shoe means the school run happens at 08:45, fifteen minutes after its window closed, the routine moves to **Past** on that partner's Home. They can spend their own points to check it in late that day, and their streak survives. The points come from what they earned on earlier days, because each day's earnings settle overnight.

For a family day out, each of you long-presses today in your own week strip and picks **Skip Day**. You each get two a week, and a skipped day is recorded as rest rather than a break in the run.

## How do shared goals work?

Goals belong to one account too, so a shared ambition is mirrored rather than merged. If the household wants to cook at home most nights, both of you add a year goal saying so. Both then break it down the same way: a May milestone, a week milestone of five home-cooked dinners, and a daily "Cook" item linked to the Dinner together routine.

![Goals switched to the week view, listing this week's goals by routine with their progress](/screenshots/guide/goals-week.jpg)

Each of you watches your own dinner count climb towards five in the week view, and More → Groups shows how the other week is going ([the Goals chapter](/guide/goals) covers the cascade). Five met days finish a week, three met weeks finish May, and six met months finish the year. A dinner ticked after its window reads Missed but still counts.

To hand a job to your partner, @mention them in a task. On your **Priority** matrix it files itself under Delegate, so you can see what you've handed over.

## Where to start

1. Set up your own routines first, without looking at each other's.
2. Compare the two lists and line up three or four anchors at the same times.
3. One of you sends the group invite.
4. Agree the no-commentary rule.
5. Do the first review together after seven days.

The evening anchor is covered in more depth in [family time as a routine](/blog/family-time-as-a-routine).
