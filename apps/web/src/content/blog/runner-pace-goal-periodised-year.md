Chasing a pace for a whole year works best when the year is cut into four-week blocks, with one block per month milestone and five dated sessions inside every week. You can't run "10K in under 50 minutes" today, but today's session can belong to the block that leads there.

Routine Notes doesn't write training plans. It holds the plan you've chosen, split into horizons, and every morning it checks whether you turned up for your part of it.

## How do four-week blocks fit a year goal?

Most training plans already run in blocks of about four weeks, and that matches the month level of the goal cascade. Write the year goal first, then give each month one milestone named after its block:

- **Months 1–2: Base.** Easy running. Build up how often you run before how far.
- **Month 3: Intervals.** One quality session a week.
- **Months 4–5: Volume.** Longer long runs, keeping the quality session.
- **Month 6: Peak.** The hardest month on paper.
- **Month 7: Taper.** Less volume, same rhythm.
- **Month 8: Test.** A race or a time trial, then easy weeks.

That makes eight milestones for a year goal that closes once six of them are met. The extra two don't raise the bar. They're room for a block that collapses under a house move or a busy quarter.

## Why does a three-run week need five items?

A month milestone closes once three of its weeks are met. A week closes on its fifth met day, and a date only counts once every item filed under that week for that date is ticked.

Runners get caught here. If the week goal says "Three runs, one of them intervals" and only the three run days have items, the best the week can do is three met days out of the five it needs. So plan the easy days as well:

- Monday: "Mobility, 15 minutes"
- Tuesday: "Intervals: 5 × 800 m"
- Wednesday: "Easy walk, 20 minutes"
- Thursday: "Easy run, 40 minutes"
- Saturday: "Long run, 75 minutes"

That's five dated items and five days that can be met. Friday and Sunday stay free.

## How do D, K and G split the work?

- **Discipline**: the window for the 06:10 Run opens at 05:10 and shuts at 06:40. A run that starts at 06:30 because the alarm went off late still counts as on time.
- **Kinetics**: the checklist is shoes, warm-up, main set, cool-down, splits logged. Ticking all five as you go earns more than marking the run done afterwards.
- **Geniuses**: Tuesday's intervals sit under a week, under the intervals block, under the year. That counts for more than a loose "go for a run".

## When a block goes wrong

Blocks do break. Say a work trip takes out week 2 of the volume month, so by day 20 there's one met week and ten days to go. Nothing is lost yet: two more met weeks still close the month, and the year only needs six blocks. Three moves keep things honest:

1. **Re-plan the block.** Edit the month milestone and push the long-run build back a week. Making the plan fit the calendar is Geniuses work.
2. **Mark the travel days as Skip Days.** Long-press today in the week strip. You get two a week, and the record shows rest, not a broken streak.
3. **Rescue a late run.** If the run happened at 18:00 instead of 06:10, open **Past** on Home that evening and check it in for 25 points. The streak holds. The intervals item is marked Missed for its timing, but it still fills Tuesday's place in the week.

## Start with the block you're in

You don't need eight months typed in on day one. Write the year goal, this month's block and this week's five items. Next Sunday, add the week after. The month calendar on **Goals** shows an empty fortnight long before it becomes an empty block.
