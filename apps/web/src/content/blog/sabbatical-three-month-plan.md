Three months off with three themes (rest, learn, build) keeps a sabbatical from drifting into soft, shapeless days. The usual sabbatical problem is the reverse of the usual work problem: too much time and nothing to shape it. Week one is wonderful. By week five the days have gone soft, and by week ten there's a quiet panic about having nothing to show for it.

What helps is a seasonal plan: one theme a month, a light set of daily routines, and a year goal that the break feeds into rather than replaces.

## Three themes, one year goal

Give each month one job:

1. **Month 1: Rest and reset.** Sleep, walking, no screens before noon. The aim is to come down from work, not to get anything done.
2. **Month 2: Learn.** One course, finished properly. Pottery, data visualisation, a language: pick one.
3. **Month 3: Build.** A small project you can show someone, using what month 2 taught you.

Put them under a year goal such as **"Come back rested, with one new skill and one thing made"**, as three of its month milestones. One detail matters here. A year goal completes after six good months, so the three sabbatical months alone won't close it. Add three more for after you return ("Use the new skill at work once a week", for example), and the break becomes the first half of a lasting change rather than a pleasant gap.

## A light daily structure

The routines stay the same all three months. Only what happens in the theme block changes:

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 07:00 | Slow morning | 10 | Curtains open, coffee on |
| 08:00 | Move | 15 | Shoes on at the door |
| 09:30 | Theme block | 30 | Open this month's plan |
| 13:00 | Lunch out | 5 | Pick somewhere you can walk to |
| 15:00 | Reading | 15 | Book on the garden chair |
| 18:00 | Cook | 10 | Read the recipe through |
| 21:30 | Journal | 15 | Notebook on the pillow |

That's 100. The theme block has 30 because it's the one routine that changes every month and the easiest to let slide when nothing is due. The journal has 15 because three months go faster than you'd think, and a line or two each night is what you'll actually remember.

## How does a month break into weeks?

Here's month 2, "Learn: a data visualisation course":

- **Week 1:** "Modules 1–3 and set up the tools."
- **Week 2:** "Modules 4–6; recreate two published charts."
- **Week 3:** "Modules 7–9; one chart from my own data."
- **Week 4:** "Final project draft."

Each day item, such as "Module 5: colour and scale", is linked to the theme block, so it shows on that routine's card at 09:30. The block's own checklist (open the plan, do the module, write three lines about it) earns **Kinetics**, and ticking the block between 08:30 and 10:00 earns **Discipline**.

Five good days complete a week, and three good weeks complete the month, so if week 4 falls apart the month still closes. Ticks on linked items earn **Geniuses**, and month and year items count for more than loose tasks.

## Unplanned days and late starts

A sabbatical should have days you didn't plan: a friend's spare room by the sea, a weekend away on a whim. On Home, long-press that day in the week strip and choose **Skip Day**. You get two a week, they go down as rest, and the streak carries on. On a sabbatical that isn't cheating. It's part of the reason for taking one.

If the theme block happened in the afternoon instead of at 09:30, it will already be in **Past**. Check it in from there before bed, paying with points, and the run of days holds. Points are added overnight, so a good stretch builds up a buffer.

## Change the theme if it's wrong

If month 2's course turns out to be the wrong one, switch at the start of week 2, not week 4. Open **Goals → Year Goals**, pick the year goal, and rewrite that month and its remaining weeks. The 12-month strip shows the whole arc, and the page's chat lets you think the change through. Re-planning mid-month is the "improve the plan" step of the loop.

## Before day one

Write the year goal and all six month milestones: three for the sabbatical and three for afterwards. Set up the seven routines. For the first week, give the theme block a single step: "Sit in the garden for an hour". Month 1 is for resting, so let it be that.
