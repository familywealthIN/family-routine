A software engineer who wakes at 05:30 can stack the whole morning into one chain of routines, so reading, yoga, a run, breakfast and a plan for the day are done before the laptop opens at 08:30 and stand-up at 09:30. The chain works because each routine's punctuality window overlaps the next one, so ticking one routine is how you start the next.

Motivation isn't the problem in this kind of morning. The leaks happen in the gaps: the ten minutes between rolling up the yoga mat and lacing up for the run.

## How does a chain of routines fit before 08:30?

Every routine is a time box. Its window opens 60 minutes before the start time and closes 30 minutes after, so the Run at 07:20 can be ticked on time anywhere from 06:20 to 07:50. With seven routines packed into three hours, the windows overlap on purpose.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 (the cue) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05:30 | Wake up | 5 | Fill a glass of water |
| 06:00 | Reading & skills | 5 | Phone face-down in another room |
| 07:00 | Yoga | 10 | Unroll the mat |
| 07:20 | Run | 10 | Lace the running shoes |
| 07:50 | Breakfast & get ready | 10 | Blend the shake |
| 08:00 | Plan the day | 5 | Open today's checklist |
| 08:30 | Deep work | 30 | Clear chats and code reviews |
| 19:00 | Writing | 5 | Clear the desk |
| 19:30 | Strength training | 10 | Check today's split |
| 21:00 | Family time | 5 | Clear the table |
| 22:30 | Night routine | 5 | Phone on charge outside the bedroom |

The points add up to exactly 100, the day's shared budget. No routine can take more than 50, and the editor under More → Routines shows how much of the budget is left as you type.

## Why does the day job get 30 points?

Points state priority. Deep work fills most of the waking day and carries this engineer's work goals, so it takes the biggest share. The five-point routines still count. They stay cheap to keep, which suits ten-minute habits.

The deep-work steps are plain actions in order: check email, answer chats, finish code reviews, read today's ticket, start the main task. Anything unplanned goes in through the **+** beside the chat box on Home, so it lands inside the 08:30 time box instead of floating free.

## Where do K, D and G come from in this morning?

- **Discipline** comes from ticks inside the window. Finishing yoga at 07:10 leaves 40 minutes of the Run's window, so the hand-over is easy.
- **Kinetics** comes from the steps. Strength training keeps the weekly split as its checklist (chest Monday, arms Tuesday, legs Thursday), so the routine already knows what today holds.
- **Geniuses** comes from linked goals. Tuesday's item "Intervals: 6 × 400 m" sits under a week milestone, which sits under a month block, which sits under the year pace goal.

## What goes wrong at 05:30, and how do you recover?

The earliest routine is the one most often checked in late. Nobody opens an app at 05:30 with a toothbrush in hand. The Wake up window shuts at 06:00, the routine moves to **Past** on Home, and the morning carries on without it.

Recovery takes one tap the same day: check it in from Past and spend its 5 points. The streak holds, the agents bound to the routine still run, and the late check-in earns like a normal tick. Points settle overnight, so today's earnings fund tomorrow's slips.

If you're paying for the same routine every morning, the plan is wrong, not you:

1. **Make the tick the first step.** Phone beside the water glass, so filling the glass and ticking happen together.
2. **Switch it to Alarm.** More → Routines → Wake up → reminder **Alarm** rings at 05:30 exactly and again at 05:35 if it isn't done.
3. **Move the time.** If the phone never reaches your hand before 05:50, start the routine at 05:50. Re-planning is Geniuses work.

## How does the run feed a year goal?

The year goal "Hold a 7:00-per-mile pace" has a month milestone for the interval block in weeks 13 to 16. That block's week milestone is "Three runs, one of them intervals", and each run is a day item linked to the Run routine, so it shows on the card at 07:20.

Progress rolls up by itself. Five met days close the week, three met weeks close the month, and six met months close the year, however many more were planned. A run ticked after its window still reads **Missed**, because status records timing, but it counts.

## A first-week checklist

- Enter each routine at the time you really start it.
- Give the work routine the weight it really has.
- Keep step 1 small and physical.
- Link at most one year goal to each morning routine.
- After seven days, open More → Progress → **On the clock** and see which routines run late. Fix the plan, not your willpower.
