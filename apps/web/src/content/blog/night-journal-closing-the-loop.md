A journal at 21:40 closes the day properly when its last line is tomorrow's first step, so the morning starts itself instead of starting with a decision. That one job matters more than what else ends up on the page.

## The day it closes

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 07:00 | Exercise | 10 | Kit on |
| 08:00 | Breakfast | 5 | Kettle on |
| 09:00 | Work | 45 | Open today's list |
| 13:00 | Lunch walk | 5 | Shoes on |
| 18:30 | Dinner | 10 | Clear the table |
| 21:40 | Journal | 15 | Notebook open at today's page |
| 22:30 | Lights out | 5 | Phone on charge outside the room |

At 15 points the journal outweighs everything except work. That's on purpose: it's the routine that makes the other seven easier tomorrow.

## Why time a notification ten minutes earlier?

So the page is never blank. Routine Notes sends an **End of day progress** notification at a time you choose, covering how the day went and what's still possible. In More → Notifications, set it for 21:30.

When you sit down at 21:40 you already know which routines were on time, which slid into **Past**, and what's still open. The checklist:

1. Notebook open at today's page.
2. Read the End of day progress summary.
3. **What moved.** Three lines: which goals got closer?
4. **What slipped, and why.** One line per slip, about the cause rather than the guilt. "Walk started at 13:50, the meeting overran" is useful. "Bad day" isn't.
5. **Tomorrow's first step.** One concrete action for the first routine of the morning.
6. Phone on charge.

## How does step 5 close the loop?

Writing tomorrow's first step means the morning routine opens with its decision made. Make it concrete by adding it as a day item for tomorrow, linked to the morning routine: "Exercise: 20-minute run, the canal loop".

![Priority on an iPad, with the triage card and today's Do, Plan, Delegate and Automate counts beside the matrix](/screenshots/guide/priority-counts.jpg)

Because it's dated tomorrow, the item files itself under **Plan** in Priority tonight, then shows on the Exercise card in the morning ([the Priority chapter](/guide/priority) explains how the quadrants sort). You can add items to future days, not past ones, which is one more reason to do this at night.

That's the loop in one routine. Turning up at 21:40 is Discipline, working the six steps is Kinetics, and improving tomorrow's plan from tonight's notes is Geniuses.

## What if dinner runs late?

The journal's window runs from 20:40 to 22:10. Evening routines catch their window more easily than dawn ones, since you're already home and awake. This one has a single weak spot: it comes after dinner, and dinner runs long.

When it does, the journal drops into **Past** at 22:10. That leaves two honest choices:

- **You wrote at 22:25.** Spend points to check it in late, tonight. The streak holds and the tick pays its 15. Today's earnings settle overnight and can be spent tomorrow.
- **You didn't write.** Leave it. A missing entry is better data than an invented one. If it was planned rest, long-press today in the week strip for a Skip Day. There are two a week, and the streak doesn't break.

If the journal is late every night, it's in the wrong slot. Move it to 21:00 in More → Routines. And if the reminder gets swiped away, switch it from Notify to Alarm: it rings at 21:40 exactly, and again five minutes later.

## Where do the entries go?

Each night can have a "Journal" day item linked to the routine, and its notes are a good home for step 4's line. After a month, search the slips by their causes. Twelve appearances of "meeting overran" is next month's planning problem, and fixing it is Geniuses work. The week goal "Journal five nights" closes at 5 met nights out of 7, so a late Saturday doesn't cost the week.

## Tonight

- Set End of day progress for ten minutes before the journal.
- Make tomorrow's first step a step, and add it as tomorrow's day item.
- Write slips as causes, not verdicts.
