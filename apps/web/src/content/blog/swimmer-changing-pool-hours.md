When the pool changes its hours, the swim routine has to be re-timed the same week, or the late check-ins that follow will look like a discipline problem when they're really a timetable one. Swimmers don't fully own their schedule. Lane sessions move with the school term, a club books the deep end on Thursdays, and the whole building shuts for a week of maintenance.

## The setup

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 (the cue) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05:40 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 06:10 | Swim | 25 | Pick up the packed kit bag |
| 07:30 | Breakfast | 10 | Kettle on |
| 08:30 | Work | 40 | Open today's list |
| 18:00 | Dryland | 10 | Mat unrolled |
| 21:30 | Pack kit & wind down | 10 | Towel and goggles into the bag |

The day adds up to 100. The kit is packed at 21:30 and picked up at 06:10. The swim's checklist follows from there: bag in the car, warm-up lengths, main set, cool-down, and the main set's times written on the session item. The swim's window runs from 05:10 to 06:40, which allows for a slow car park and a queue at the turnstile.

## Why not just turn up at the new time?

Because the routine is still waiting at the old one. Say the early lanes move from 06:10 to 06:40 for the new term, and nobody changes the routine. You arrive for the 06:40 lanes, tick at 06:45, and the swim has already been in **Past** for five minutes. Your effort hasn't changed. The plan has gone out of date.

So re-time first. Open **More → Routines**, tap the swim, and step the start time up by 10-minute increments to 06:40. The window moves with it, to 05:40–07:10. While you're in the editor, move the routines that depend on the swim. If it's thirty minutes later, breakfast probably moves too. The 24-hour dial on that screen shows the knock-on effect before you save.

## Alarm through the changeover

For the first two weeks on a new timetable, set the swim's reminder to **Alarm** rather than **Notify**. Notify reminds you around the time you usually swim, and in week one of a new term "usually" still means the old time. Alarm rings at exactly 06:40, then again at 06:45 if the routine isn't ticked. Switch back to Notify once the new time feels normal.

## Pool closures: when a skip fails you

There are two tools for closures, and they cover different situations.

- **No swim, nothing instead.** Long-press today in the week strip for a Skip Day. It goes on the record as a rest day and the streak survives. You get two a week.
- **You swam, just not in the window.** If the morning lanes were cancelled and you swam at 12:30, the swim is in **Past** on Home. Tick it from there before midnight and pay its 25 points.

A week-long maintenance closure needs five more skips than the two the week allows. Change the plan for that week instead: turn the swim into a dryland session for seven days, and rewrite the week goal as "Five dryland sessions while the pool is shut". The routine keeps running and the week can still close.

## What does the record show afterwards?

Two weeks after a timetable change, look at **More → Progress → On the clock**. It compares on-time and late check-ins for each routine. A short burst of late check-ins in the first few days that then stops is the change working its way through. Late check-ins that carry on after you've re-timed are worth a closer look, because the new time may clash with something else in the day.

## The goals don't move

Re-timing never touches the cascade. A year goal like "Swim 1,500 m continuous front crawl" keeps its month milestones, and the week still closes on its fifth met date. A session ticked after its window is marked Missed because of the timing, but it still counts for its day. The timetable can move as often as it likes. Only the routine's start time has to move with it.
