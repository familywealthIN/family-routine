Dog walks make the best anchors, because the routine that never skips is the one the dog insists on. Hang a weaker habit straight after each walk and it borrows the walk's momentum. At 06:30 the dog is at the door whatever kind of week you're having, which gives you one part of the day that never needs motivation.

So don't try to turn the walk into a habit. Use it to carry the habits you're still building.

## What are anchors and passengers?

An anchor is a routine that happens no matter what. A passenger is a routine you're still building, placed right after an anchor so it starts while you're already moving. You're out, you're awake, your shoes are on. The next small thing is easier from there than from the sofa.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Morning walk | 15 | Pick up the lead |
| 07:10 | Stretch | 10 | Mat by the back door |
| 07:40 | Breakfast | 5 | Kettle on |
| 08:30 | Work | 35 | Notebook open |
| 12:30 | Lunch walk | 10 | Pick up the lead |
| 13:00 | Ten pages | 5 | Book on the bench |
| 18:00 | Evening walk | 15 | Pick up the lead |
| 21:30 | Night routine | 5 | Phone on charge |

That's 100 points. Each walk is an anchor with a passenger behind it: the morning walk carries the stretch, and the lunch walk carries ten pages on a park bench.

## Why the windows make stacking easy

The stretch is set for 07:10, and its window runs from 06:10 to 07:40, an hour before to half an hour after. It's open the whole time you're out with the dog. Get back at 07:05, unroll the mat, and the tick is on time.

That's where **Discipline** comes from here. The walks never fail, so the passengers are where it's really earned, each one started while it's still easy to start.

## Move the reminders to where they help

The dog already reminds you about the walks, so a 06:30 notification is just noise. In **More → Routines**, tap each walk and set its reminder to **Off**.

Spend the reminders on the passengers instead. Set them to **Notify**, and for the one you skip most, use **Alarm**, which rings at exactly the start time and again five minutes later if the routine isn't ticked. The phone then goes off as you come back through the door, just when the passenger needs to start.

## What each score measures

- **Discipline** counts on-time starts. The anchors give it easily, and the passengers are where it's earned.
- **Kinetics** counts the checklist. The stretch has four steps (mat out, hips, hamstrings, back), and each one ticked in the slot adds to it.
- **Geniuses** counts what the passengers feed. "Stretch most mornings this year" has an April milestone and a week milestone of five stretch sessions, and each day's stretch item is linked to its routine.

![Home on a phone: the week strip of day rings, a routine in progress with its checklist and the Today, Week, Month and Year tabs](/screenshots/home-mobile.png)

The phone stays in your coat pocket on the walk, and Home is what you see when you take it out ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) walks through the card). Five stretches close the week, three good weeks close April, and six good months close the year, however many you planned. That's the loop each morning: show up on time with the dog, work the steps, and let the goal record it.

## What about the days it goes wrong?

**The walk happened but nobody ticked it.** You left your phone at home, which happens a lot on dog walks. By the time you notice, the walk is sitting in **Past**. Open it and spend points to check it in late. Your streak carries on, and the tick counts in full. A late check-in is exactly for this: something you did, just not on time.

**The passenger didn't happen.** Leave it in Past. If it's the same passenger every time, move it. Ten pages might fit better after the 18:00 walk than after lunch.

**Someone else walked the dog.** If a friend or a dog walker did the morning walk, that anchor wasn't yours today. Leave it rather than checking in something you didn't do.

**The whole day is off.** On a travel day, long-press today in the week strip and choose **Skip Day**, one of the two rest days you get each week, with no streak lost.

## Next step

List your anchors: the dog walks, plus anything else that happens no matter what. Put one passenger after each, switch the anchors' reminders off and the passengers' on. After a week, check *On the clock* under **More → Progress** to see which passengers held up.
