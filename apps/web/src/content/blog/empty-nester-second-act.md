An empty nest makes a good second act when the freed hours get one year goal before they fill up by themselves. For eighteen years, 17:00 to 20:00 belonged to other people: lifts to practice, homework at the kitchen table, dinner for five on a weeknight. When the last child leaves home those hours suddenly empty, and after a few restful weeks they tend to fill with whatever's nearest, usually more work or more television.

Here's how an illustrative empty nester gives the evening a new purpose: learning the cello well enough to join a community orchestra.

## Why start from the goal?

Most people set up a routine and hope a goal turns up. Starting from the top works better.

**Year:** "Join the community orchestra by next September."

Six month milestones are enough to complete it, even if twelve are planned:

1. Posture, bowing and open strings.
2. First position: scales in C, G and D.
3. Three beginner pieces, played through.
4. Reading orchestral parts at a slow tempo.
5. A lesson every fortnight, with a recording sent to the teacher.
6. The audition piece, learnt and recorded.

Each month breaks into weeks ("Five practice days and one lesson"), and each week into day items ("G major, two octaves, slowly"). The day items are linked to the practice routine, so they appear on its card when it comes up.

## The new day

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Morning walk | 10 | Boots on by the back door |
| 07:30 | Breakfast | 5 | Kettle on |
| 09:00 | Work | 30 | Open today's list |
| 13:00 | Lunch break | 5 | Step away from the desk |
| 18:00 | Cello practice | 25 | Cello out of the case |
| 19:30 | Dinner together | 10 | Table laid for two |
| 20:30 | Call the kids | 5 | Pick who's next |
| 22:00 | Wind-down | 10 | Lights low, book out |

That's 100. Cello practice gets 25, the biggest share after work, because the second act deserves the weight. Calling the kids gets 5: a small, steady way to turn the new distance into a habit of keeping in touch. To shift points later, tap a routine in **More → Routines**. The editor shows how much of the 100 is left.

## The first step is the case

Getting the instrument out is the hardest part of any practice session. Make that the first item on the checklist, and the reminder at 18:00 opens with it. The rest sets the order:

1. Cello out of the case.
2. Rosin the bow, tune.
3. Ten minutes of open strings and scales.
4. Today's piece, slowly.
5. Record one take.

The checklist earns **Kinetics**. Ticking the routine between 17:00 and 18:30 earns **Discipline**. Ticking the day item linked to the year goal earns **Geniuses**, and the higher the item sits in the plan, the more it weighs.

## Missed practice when the kids come home

The children come back for a weekend. Cooking for five again leaves no time for scales. On each of those days, long-press today in the week strip on Home and take a **Skip Day**. It works on the day itself, not in advance. You get two a week, they go down as rest rather than gaps, and the streak carries on.

If you practised but late, at 21:00 after a long dinner, the routine has already gone to **Past**. Check it in from there before you go to bed, paying with points, and the run of days holds.

The week goal forgives too. It completes once five days are met. If you played on Thursday but forgot to tick it, tick it on Friday and it still counts. The item will show **Missed**, which records the timing, not the effort.

## Let the plan grow up

By month three you'll know whether a lesson every fortnight is enough, whether the audition is in September or April, and whether the cello was the right instrument in the first place. Open **Goals → Year Goals** and rework the months.

![The Year Goals page with a year goal's progress ring, its 12-month strip and the focused month's weeks and days](/screenshots/guide/goals-cascade.jpg)

The strip shows the whole year, and the focus card shows the month you're in. The page's own chat is a good place to talk the plan through ([the Goals chapter](/guide/goals) shows how months, weeks and days connect). Changing the plan is part of the loop, not backing out.

## Tonight

Write the year goal. If you're not sure how to split it, tap the **+** beside the chat box on Home and use the Goal tab with a prompt like "Learn the cello well enough to join a community orchestra in a year". Edit the dated milestones before you save. Then set up one routine at 18:00 whose first step is the case.
