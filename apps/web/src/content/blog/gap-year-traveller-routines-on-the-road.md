Routines that fit in a backpack keep a gap year from dissolving into long scrolls between check-out times. Without them, many travellers find by month four that the phrasebook hasn't been opened since the first border and the journal stopped in week two.

The year is meant to be unstructured, and that's the appeal. The trick is a light structure that needs no desk, gym or fixed address, only things every stop already has: a morning, a bed, a town to walk around and an evening.

## A list that fits in a bag

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 07:00 | Morning reset | 20 | Repack the day bag |
| 09:00 | Language practice | 15 | Earphones in, lesson open |
| 12:00 | Explore on foot | 20 | Pick one place on the map |
| 17:00 | Journal & photos | 15 | Notebook out on the table |
| 19:00 | Plan tomorrow | 15 | Check the next bus or train |
| 22:00 | Sleep routine | 15 | Charge everything on one plug |

That's 100 points. The morning reset and the walk get the most, 20 each, because they're what turn a day in transit into a day somewhere. Every first step is small and physical: repacking a day bag works as well in a hostel dorm in Lisbon as in a guesthouse in Hanoi.

## The window is the only appointment

With no job or classes, a routine's window is about the only fixed point the day has. For the 09:00 language lesson it opens at 08:00 and closes at 09:30. Tick within that and the lesson earns **Discipline**. It's a light commitment, but it stops "I'll do it later" turning into "I'll do it in the next country".

The checklists earn **Kinetics**. Explore on foot might read: pick a place, walk there without the map, find the market, write down three words you saw on signs. Ticking them turns sightseeing into practice.

## How do you handle a time-zone jump?

Routine times are clock times. Fly from Lima to Madrid and the 07:00 reset lands six hours away from where your body thinks the morning is. The app doesn't decide how you adjust. You do, and it takes a minute: open **More → Routines**, where the day shows as a 24-hour dial beside a timeline, and tap a routine to edit its start time.

1. **Before the flight,** decide whether you'll live on the new clock from day one. If you will, leave the times alone and accept two rough mornings.
2. **For a big jump,** move only the morning reset, in 10-minute steps if you like, and walk it back over a few days. The rest can follow.
3. **Don't set times you won't keep.** An 11:00 routine you start at 11:00 tells you more than a 07:00 one you check in late every day.

## When a travel day goes wrong

A fourteen-hour bus ride isn't a failed day. It's a travel day. On Home, long-press today in the week strip and pick **Skip Day**. The day goes down as rest, not a miss, and the streak carries on. You get two a week, which covers most itineraries.

If something just ran late, say the journal written at 23:00 after a delayed ferry, its window has closed and it sits in **Past**. Before the day ends, check it in from there with points. The streak holds and it counts as a full tick. Earnings are added overnight, so a good day in Seville pays for a messy one in Porto.

![A past day opened from the week strip: today's list with D, K and G badges, that day's checklist marked past, and a chat offering What did I do last time?](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-past.jpg)

On the next bus, tap yesterday in the week strip to look back at it ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) covers the strip). Past days are for reviewing only. They can't take new items or routine ticks, which is why late check-ins have to happen on the day itself.

## A year goal for the year off

A gap year is already year-shaped, so give it a year goal: "Get by in the language of every long stop". Then cascade it:

- **Month milestones:** one per country, e.g. "Portuguese: order, ask the way, say thanks".
- **Week milestone:** "Five practice sessions, one real conversation".
- **Day items:** "Lesson 12: numbers and prices", linked to Language practice.

Ticking linked items earns **Geniuses**. Five good days complete a week, three good weeks complete a month, and six good months complete the year, however many countries you planned. If the route changes in month seven, rewrite the milestones that are left. That's re-planning, and nothing breaks.

## Before you leave

Set up the six routines at home and run them for a week while the times still mean something. Check that every first step fits in your bag. Then, on the first long travel day, use a Skip Day without feeling guilty about it.
