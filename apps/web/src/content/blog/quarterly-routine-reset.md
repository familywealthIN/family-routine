A quarterly routine reset takes five passes in about forty-five minutes: times, points, steps, structure and goals, in that order. It uses what the last thirteen weeks actually showed to rebuild the plan for the next thirteen. That's Geniuses work in the KDG framework: improving the plan, so the same showing up counts for more.

Routines decay quietly. The 05:30 start that felt good in June feels heroic in December. A step added in a burst of enthusiasm gets skipped every day. A finished course still holds the points it earned when it mattered most. A weekly review catches drift inside the plan. Once a season, the plan itself needs a look.

## Pass 1: times (10 minutes)

Open **More → Progress**, choose the longest view you have data for, and read **On the clock**. It splits check-ins into on time and late, routine by routine.

Look for routines checked in late more often than on time. A late check-in (spending points to tick a routine from **Past** the same day) is a good rescue. The streak holds and its agents still run. But a routine rescued most days is scheduled at the wrong time. Each window opens 60 minutes before the start time and shuts 30 minutes after, so moving a start by 20 to 40 minutes often turns a habitually late routine into an on-time one.

## Pass 2: points (5 minutes)

Every routine shares one 100-point day budget, and none can take more than 50. Points are priorities, and priorities change with the season. Ask of each routine: *if I could keep only three this quarter, would this be one?* Those three should hold most of the budget.

Here's an autumn reset for someone whose summer revolved around a course they've now finished:

| Time | Before (summer) | Pts | After (winter) | Pts | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05:30 to 06:10 | Wake up | 5 | Wake up | 5 | Fill a glass of water |
| 06:00 to 06:30 | Course study | 15 | Reading | 5 | Phone face-down in another room |
| 07:00 | Run | 15 | Run | 10 | Lace the running shoes |
| 08:30 | Deep work | 30 | Deep work | 35 | Close every tab but one |
| 19:00 | Writing | 5 | Writing | 15 | Clear the desk |
| 19:30 | Strength | 10 | Strength | 10 | Check today's split |
| 21:00 | Family time | 10 | Family time | 10 | Clear the table |
| 22:30 | Night routine | 10 | Wind-down | 10 | Phone on charge outside the bedroom |

Both columns total 100. Course study's 15 became Reading's 5 plus 10 more for Writing, which is this winter's project. Run gave 5 to Deep work. The 05:30 start slid 40 minutes, because dark mornings kept pushing Wake up into Past.

## Pass 3: steps (10 minutes)

Open each routine under **More → Routines** and read its checklist as a stranger would.

- **Is step 1 a tiny physical action?** "Lace the running shoes", not "Run". Step 1 is the cue, and reminders lead with it.
- **Is every step an action?** "Review" is a label. "Read yesterday's notes and write one next step" is an action. Kinetics comes from working the checklist, and labels give you nothing to work.

Delete any step you've skipped for a month. It isn't ambition any more. It's noise.

## Pass 4: retire, merge, rename (10 minutes)

Some routines need rebuilding. A fifteen-step night routine might become a six-step *Wind-down*, renamed so the name says what it's for. Renaming mid-season is the plan improving, which is exactly what Geniuses measures. If two short routines always run back to back, merge them, making one the other's first step. If a routine has no linked goal and you can't say why it exists, retire it and give its points to one that has a reason.

## Pass 5: goals (10 minutes)

![Goals with the period ladder, the month calendar of day rings and today's goals grouped by routine](/screenshots/guide/goals-calendar.jpg)

Start on the Goals page's month calendar. A blank fortnight shows up straight away ([the Goals chapter](/guide/goals) covers each view). Then count each year goal's met months. A year closes at 6, however many were planned. A goal with 2 met months and 5 to go is on course. One with 1 met month and 3 to go needs a new plan, not more willpower.

Write the next quarter's three month milestones for each year goal and link each to its routine. Here, Writing's year goal gets December, January and February. Leave week milestones for each Monday, when the month's shape is clear. A month needs 3 met weeks, and a week needs 5 met days.

## What goes wrong in the week after a reset?

The new times feel off for a week, and that's normal. Don't judge the schedule until it has run for two full weeks. If the changeover week includes travel, use Skip Day by long-pressing today in the week strip. You get at most 2 a week, and the streak survives. If a moved routine still lands in Past three days running, move it again rather than waiting for next quarter.

Then put the next reset in your diary, 13 weeks out.
