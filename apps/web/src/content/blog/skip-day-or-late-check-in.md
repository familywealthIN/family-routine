On travel days, take Skip Day when almost nothing on the list will happen, and use a late check-in for each routine you did but missed. This decision guide turns that into four questions. Travel breaks routines unevenly. The early run doesn't happen, deep work happens in fragments on a train, and the evening might go perfectly in a hotel room. The two tools do different jobs.

## The two tools

**Skip Day** covers the whole day. Long-press today in the week strip (or right-click it on the web) and confirm. The day is recorded as rest, not failure, and every routine keeps its streak. You get at most two a week. A skipped day ticks nothing for you, so it won't add a met date to a week goal.

**A late check-in** covers one routine. Once a routine's window closes, thirty minutes after its start time, the routine moves to **Past** on Home. For the rest of that day you can open it and spend points to check it in. The streak holds, its agents still run, and the tick is a full one. It costs that routine's points.

## Which tool fits today?

**1. Will most of today's routines happen in some form?** If yes, don't skip. Do what you can, tick inside the windows where possible, and handle the rest one routine at a time.

**2. For each routine that missed its window, did you do it later?** If yes, check it in late. If no, let it stay in Past.

**3. Will almost nothing on the list happen?** A 04:00 airport start, a twelve-hour drive or a day of back-to-back conference sessions. Then take Skip Day, early in the day, so you can stop thinking about the list.

**4. Have you already used both Skip Days this week?** Then today is a late-check-in day or a let-it-go day. If you want a third skip most weeks, your travel weeks need a different plan.

## A travel day, routine by routine

One consultant's normal list, and what happened on a day with a 09:00 train and an afternoon client session:

| Time | Routine | Points | On the travel day |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:00 | Wake up | 10 | Done at 06:40 on the way to the station. Late check-in |
| 06:30 | Run | 15 | Didn't happen. Let it go |
| 07:30 | Breakfast | 5 | Ticked on time at the station café |
| 09:00 | Deep work | 40 | Done on the train, inside the window |
| 18:00 | Language practice | 10 | Lesson 14 on the train back, on time |
| 21:00 | Call home | 10 | Ticked on time |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 10 | Ticked on time |

That's 100. Six of the seven routines happened, so a Skip Day would have wiped out a good day's record. One late check-in, 10 points from the balance earlier days had built up, kept the wake-up streak going. The run is just a gap, and an honest one.

## When the trip goes wrong in other ways

- **The red-eye.** You land at 06:00 after no sleep and the day is gone. Skip it and pick up the list tomorrow.
- **Two travel days in one week.** Skip the worse of the two and get through the other with late check-ins.
- **A week-long trip.** Two skips can't cover seven days. Re-time the routines for the trip instead: in **More → Routines**, move the run to 18:00 and deep work to whenever the client sessions end, then move them back when you're home. Adjusting the plan to fit real life is Geniuses work too.

## What happens to the goals?

The rules are generous. Five met days close a week, three met weeks close a month, and six met months close a year, however many you planned. A week with two Skip Days still has five days left, which is exactly enough.

![A past day opened from the week strip, showing that day's checklist and a chat about the day](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-past.jpg)

Back home, tap the trip's days in the week strip to review what got done ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) covers past days). Past days can't take new items or routine ticks. But a goal item that was created on its day and forgotten can still be ticked afterwards, and it counts. So the language practice you did on the train still moves the Spanish goal, even marked Missed.

## Before your next trip

- In **More → Notifications**, turn on **Good morning**, which shows your day at a glance at a time you choose. On a travel morning it's your cue to decide skip-or-not before 07:00.
- Check your balance the evening before. If it's low, plan to tick the light routines on time and save points for the one with an agent or a goal item.

## Next step

On your next travel day, run through the four questions once, in the morning, and stick with the answer. For the points side of the decision, see [When to spend points](/blog/when-to-spend-points).
