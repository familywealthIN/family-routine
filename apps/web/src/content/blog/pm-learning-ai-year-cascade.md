A product manager can learn AI in a year by cutting the plan into months with one theme each, and weeks of two or three courses. Without that structure, the material (models, prompting, evaluation, designing AI features, a certificate for each) turns into a pile of half-watched videos and a steady sense of being behind.

## The study day

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05:40 | Wake up | 5 | Glass of water |
| 06:00 | Learning | 15 | Laptop open at today's lesson |
| 07:00 | Exercise | 10 | Trainers on |
| 08:00 | Plan the day | 5 | Today's list open |
| 08:30 | Work | 40 | Inbox, then the roadmap |
| 19:00 | Build something | 10 | Yesterday's prototype open |
| 21:00 | Family time | 10 | Phone in the drawer |
| 22:30 | Night routine | 5 | Tomorrow's lesson queued |

Two routines serve the plan. Learning, at 06:00, takes in new material. Build something, at 19:00, puts it to use: a small prototype, a prompt test, a feature write-up. Give both the tag `project:ai-learning` in More → Routines, and each one's chat opens with a **Before you start** brief listing next steps and recent sessions.

## What does the year look like, month by month?

Start from a prompt. Home → + → Goal opens the AI plan. Write: "Become a product manager who can design, evaluate and ship AI features within a year." The AI drafts a plan with its own title and dated milestones. Edit it until it reads like your year, then save it as the parent goal with its milestones.

A version that holds up:

| Month | Theme |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | How models work, for product people |
| 2 | Prompting and evaluation basics |
| 3 | UX research for AI features |
| 4 | A design certificate, courses 1–3 |
| 5 | The same certificate, courses 4–6 |
| 6 | An AI product certificate |
| 7 | Capstone: one AI feature, spec to evaluation |

Seven months are planned. The year closes at **6 met months**, so the seventh is headroom, not a harder target.

## Weeks as courses

Each month breaks into week milestones you can finish. "Design certificate, courses 1–3" becomes one course a week plus a review week. Each day item is one session, such as "Course 2, module 3: usability testing", linked to Learning and to that week. The evening routine's items link the same way: "Prototype: a support-reply classifier, prompt v2".

That's where Geniuses comes from. A ticked item under a week, a month and the year outweighs a loose task, and the weight grows with the horizon. For one month the arithmetic is: 6 sessions planned a week, 5 met days close it, and 3 met weeks close the month. A launch at work that eats one week doesn't sink the month.

Six weeks in, part of the plan is usually wrong. Evaluation matters more than you thought, or one certificate repeats another. Edit the month milestones as you go. Swapping a redundant course for a deeper week on evaluation isn't falling behind, and the app rewards it.

## The 06:00 session that slips

After a late release, Learning starts at 06:50. Its window ran from 05:00 to 06:30, so Home lists it under **Past**. Study anyway, then use points that day to check it in late. The streak holds and the 15 points arrive as usual. An item ticked late shows Missed and still counts toward the week.

If a heavy week at work is going to swallow the mornings, plan for it: move that week's day items to the 19:00 routine instead of paying for six late check-ins in a row.

## How the pillars add up

- **Discipline**: the 06:00 tick, inside its window, every morning.
- **Kinetics**: the checklist. Lesson, notes, one practice question, one line for tomorrow.
- **Geniuses**: every linked session moving a course, a month and the year, plus the edits that keep the plan honest.
