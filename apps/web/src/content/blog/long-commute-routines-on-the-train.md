Two hours on the train each day turn into Kinetics once each leg of a long commute becomes a routine with its own checklist. An hour each way adds up to ten hours a week, more than most people give any hobby, and most of it goes on scrolling because the train feels like a gap between things rather than a thing in itself.

In Routine Notes, a routine has a start time, a checklist and a share of the day's points. The checklist is where **Kinetics** comes from: points for doing the work, not only for turning up.

## The commuter's day

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:00 | Wake & go | 10 | Clothes laid out the night before |
| 06:40 | Train out: plan & read | 15 | Earphones in, today's list open |
| 08:00 | Work | 30 | First coffee at the desk |
| 12:30 | Walk | 5 | Jacket on |
| 17:30 | Train home: study | 15 | Laptop on the tray table |
| 19:00 | Family dinner | 10 | Phone in the hall |
| 20:30 | Stretch | 10 | Mat by the sofa |
| 22:00 | Night | 5 | Bag packed for tomorrow |

Between them, the two train routines get 30 of the 100 points, the same as the working day. That's deliberate. Give them real weight and you start treating them as real slots, not dead time.

## Which steps work on a moving train?

The checklist has to suit a seat with a tray table and patchy signal. Outbound:

1. Earphones in, today's list open.
2. Go through the day's items and move anything that won't happen.
3. Read 20 pages.
4. Write three lines about what you read.

Homebound:

1. Laptop on the tray table.
2. One course lesson, or 300 words of the draft.
3. Note where you stopped.
4. Laptop away two stops before yours.

Every ticked item adds Kinetics. The first item matters most because it's the cue, and the routine's reminder opens with it, so "Earphones in" is what you read on the platform. Switch the outbound routine to Alarm in **More → Routines** and it rings at exactly 06:40, then again five minutes later if it's still unticked. That second ring is a good prompt to take out the book rather than the phone.

## Catching tasks on the move

Commutes throw up tasks: an email you remembered, a call to make at lunch, something to pick up on the way home. Tap the **+** beside the chat box and type it as you'd say it.

![The add box open over Home, adding Call dentist tomorrow at 10am as a task to the current routine](/screenshots/guide/dashboard-quicktask.jpg)

The date and time fill themselves in, and the task lands in the routine you're in, so it becomes part of the commute rather than a separate backlog ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) has more on quick tasks). Anything that belongs to no routine goes to the Inbox, the tray icon at the top of Home. Clearing it with "Move to routine" is a good use of the last ten minutes before your stop.

## Where the goals come in

The reading step suits a year goal: "Read 24 books this year". That's two a month ("Two books in March") and, per week, "Read on five commutes". Day items like "Chapters 9–12" are linked to the outbound routine and appear on its card.

Five good days complete the week, which happens to be one working week of commutes. Three good weeks complete the month, and six good months the year. Ticks on linked items add **Geniuses**, so the reading becomes a plan rather than a habit you hope is going somewhere.

## Late trains and empty weekends

Commuter routines run into two problems sooner or later.

**Cancellations.** The 06:40 is cancelled, you catch the 07:30, and the window (05:40 to 07:10) has closed before you sit down. The routine moves to **Past** on Home. If you read on the later train, check it in from there that evening with points. The streak holds, and the late tick counts like any other.

**Weekends.** There's no 06:40 on a Saturday. You could re-time the train routines as weekend reading slots, let them pass unticked, or long-press the day in the week strip for a Skip Day. You get two Skip Days a week and they don't cost the streak, but each one covers every routine that day, and spending both on the weekend leaves none for a sick day. Most commuters re-time instead: "Train out" becomes 08:00 reading at the kitchen table.

## A Friday look back

On Friday's train home, open **More → Progress** on the Week view. *On the clock* shows which routines you start on time. If the evening train is always late because you always miss the 17:30, move the routine to 17:50 and stop fighting the timetable.

To start, pick one direction, give it four steps that work on a train, and keep it for a week before adding the other.
