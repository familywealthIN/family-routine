Alternating weeks work best with one routine list that suits both the kid weeks and the solo weeks, and week goals that carry the difference. If you rebuild the list every Sunday, you end up with a record nobody can compare from one week to the next.

With the children one week and at their other parent's the next, the two weeks really are different. One has the school run, homework, bath and stories. The other has a lot of empty evening and a strong pull to waste it.

## Why name routines by purpose, not activity?

"Kids' bath" means nothing in a solo week. "Evening block" works in both. Use names that describe a slot in the day, and let that day's items say what fills it this week.

| Time | Routine | Points | Kid week | Solo week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30 | Morning | 10 | Wake the kids | Run |
| 07:30 | Out of the door | 15 | School run | Early train |
| 09:00 | Work | 35 | Focused work | Focused work |
| 17:30 | Evening block | 20 | Homework and dinner | Training or a class |
| 20:00 | Second evening | 10 | Bedtime and stories | Friends or a project |
| 21:30 | Reset | 10 | Lunchboxes, laundry | Plan the next kid week |

The total is 100 in either week. Times and points never change. Only what goes into each slot does.

## Keep the steps general

Each routine has a single checklist, so write steps that fit both weeks. The Evening block's might be: phone in the drawer, start the main thing, tidy up after. Step 1 is the same small action whichever week it is, which makes it a cue you can rely on.

The week-specific detail goes into day items linked to the routine. In a kid week, Tuesday's Evening block item might be "Homework, then fish pie". In a solo week it might be "Pottery class".

Both kinds of item appear on the same card when you switch its tab from Today to Week ([the Dashboard chapter](/guide/dashboard) covers the four tabs).

## Swap the week goals, not the routines

This is where the two weeks really split. At the start of each week, set a week milestone that suits it:

- **Kid week:** "Five dinners at the table, phones away"
- **Solo week:** "Five training sessions"

Each sits under its own year goal, one about family evenings and one about fitness, with a month milestone in between. A month with two kid weeks and two solo weeks can feed both years. Three met weeks close a month, and each year closes at six met months, so neither goal needs every week.

With seven nights of children and five dinners as the target, two nights are already spare for a party or a late finish.

## Handover days

Handover days are the awkward ones: the school run in the morning and an empty house by evening, or the other way round. Don't fight it. Tick what happens. If the Evening block is half the children and half you, add both items to that day.

If the block runs late because handover was at 19:00, a full hour after its window closed at 18:00, open it from **Past** and spend points to check it in. The run of days carries on, and the items you tick still count towards the week, even marked Missed. If handover takes the whole day, long-press today in the week strip for **Skip Day**. You get two a week, and no streak suffers.

## Why do solo-week mornings slip?

Solo weeks fail in a predictable way. With no school run to anchor 06:30, the Morning routine drifts later, and by Thursday it's in Past every day.

The record makes this obvious. Under **More → Progress**, *On the clock* shows on-time and late check-ins for each routine, and the solo weeks stand out as the late ones. Give solo mornings an anchor of their own, such as a run with a friend. Or go to **More → Routines** and set the Morning routine's reminder to **Alarm**, which rings at 06:30 and again five minutes later if it isn't ticked.

## What to do this Sunday

Rename any routine that only makes sense in one kind of week, keep its time, and write next week's milestone for the week that's coming. When the children are with you, [family time as a routine](/blog/family-time-as-a-routine) covers the evening slot in detail.
