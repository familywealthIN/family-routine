A weekend renovation runs best through the Plan quadrant. Schedule Saturday's job on a weekday evening and it waits under Plan until its day. The DIY project itself becomes a year goal, and the materials sit as sub-tasks, so nothing is missing at 09:00 on Saturday.

Renovations rarely stall for lack of energy. They stall on Saturday morning, when you discover there's no adhesive, the old tiles are still on the wall, and the hour you meant for tiling goes on a trip to the shop.

## A daily project block

Every routine repeats daily, and the renovation routine does too. That suits a renovation better than it sounds: twenty minutes on a weekday evening for measuring, ordering and prep, then half a day under the same routine at the weekend. Set it up in **More → Routines**.

| Time | Routine | Points | Step 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 07:00 | Morning | 10 | Glass of water |
| 08:00 | Work | 35 | Notebook open |
| 12:30 | Lunch walk | 10 | Shoes on |
| 18:30 | Project block | 25 | Tool bag on the table |
| 20:00 | Family | 10 | Phones in the basket |
| 22:00 | Night routine | 10 | Lights down |

That's 100 points, with 25 on the project block. Putting the tool bag on the table is physical, needs no willpower, and makes the rest of the session hard to dodge. After it: check today's item, do it, put the tools away, sweep up.

## The project as a cascade

Work top-down:

- **Year goal:** finish the ground floor.
- **Month milestones:** the kitchen splashback in March, the hallway in April, the downstairs loo in May.
- **Week milestone:** "Tile the kitchen splashback".
- **Day items**, each linked to Project block: Monday, measure and order the tiles. Tuesday, confirm the delivery slot. Wednesday, take off the old tiles. Thursday, fill and sand the wall. Saturday, tile. Sunday, grout and seal.

The small weekday jobs count for more than they look. A week milestone closes on its fifth complete date, and Monday's measuring completes a date just as well as Saturday's tiling. The weekday evenings make the weekend possible, and they also carry the week to five.

## Why does the weekend's job wait in Plan?

Anything dated for a future day is filed under **Plan** on Priority as soon as you create it. On Wednesday evening, then, the matrix shows today's prep under **Do now** and the weekend's tiling waiting under Plan.

![Priority on an iPad: the triage card and today's Do, Plan, Delegate and Automate counts beside the matrix](/screenshots/guide/priority-counts.jpg)

The four counts give you the week's shape at a glance ([the Priority chapter](/guide/priority) explains how each quadrant fills). If Do now is full of tiling jobs on a Tuesday, the plan has slipped. Keep it to three items or fewer. Jobs for a friend or a contractor go in with an @mention ("@electrician confirm the socket position") and land under **Delegate**.

## Materials as sub-tasks

Most ruined Saturdays come down to one missing part, so the Saturday item carries the shopping list as sub-tasks: tile adhesive, spacers, grout, a sponge, a new blade for the cutter. Tick each one off as it arrives on Thursday. A sub-task still unticked on Friday night is your warning, with a day left to act on it.

Clearing sub-tasks in the slot earns **Kinetics**. Ticking items that roll up into the week, March and the year earns **Geniuses**, and the higher an item reaches, the more it's worth.

## What if the weekend falls through?

**The delivery is late.** Open Saturday's item and move its date to next weekend, or ask the routine chat to defer it. Swap in something you can do now, such as painting the skirting, so the day still has a finished item.

**The block starts late.** If you pick up the tools at 20:30, ninety minutes after the window closed at 19:00, open Project block from **Past** and spend points to check it in. The streak survives, and the items you finish that evening still count towards the week, even though they're marked Missed.

**The weekend belongs to the family.** Long-press today in the week strip for **Skip Day**. It's one of the week's two rest days, and it doesn't break any run.

## Re-plan the months freely

Renovations always overrun. When the hallway turns out to need replastering, edit April's milestone and push the loo back to June. Adjusting the plan to fit the house is part of the work. The year needs only six met months to close, however many you planned.
